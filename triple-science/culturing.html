Culturing microorganisms

Biology · rank 18 of 31 · 22 marks · 2.5% of Biology marks · in 4 of 9 papers · Required practical: Effect of antiseptics or antibiotics on bacterial growth

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Bacteria double at each division. After n divisions you have 2ⁿ times as many, so numbers grow very fast.

2Step by step

Bacteria divide by binary fission. In good conditions this can happen every 20 minutes.

Number of bacteria = number at start × 2number of divisions. Number of divisions = total time ÷ time for one division.

Aseptic technique stops contamination:

  • Sterilise Petri dishes and agar before use.
  • Pass the inoculating loop through a flame.
  • Tape the lid on, but do not seal it fully, so oxygen can get in and anaerobic pathogens do not grow.
  • Incubate at a maximum of 25 °C in school labs. This reduces the chance of growing human pathogens.

In the required practical, paper discs with antibiotic go on a bacterial lawn. A clear zone (inhibition zone) shows where bacteria were killed. A larger zone means a more effective antibiotic. Area = πr².

CAUTIONUse the radius in πr². If you measure the diameter, halve it first.

3Another way to see it

A Petri dish of agar is a tiny farm for bacteria. To grow only the bacteria you want, and to stay safe, use aseptic technique:

  1. Sterilise the Petri dish and agar before use.
  2. Pass the inoculating loop through a flame.
  3. Tape the lid on, but do not seal it all round (oxygen gets in, so harmful anaerobic bacteria do not grow).
  4. Incubate at 25 °C in school, so bacteria that cause human disease do not grow well.

To compare antibiotics, measure the clear zone around each disc. Area = πr². A bigger clear zone means the antibiotic killed more bacteria.

4Watch out for

  • Using the diameter instead of the radius in πr².
  • Saying the dish is sealed all round. It is taped in a few places only.
  • Saying 37 °C is used in school. It is 25 °C, to avoid growing human pathogens.
  • Counting the number of generations wrongly in doubling questions.
  • Saying a small clear zone means a strong antibiotic. Bigger zone = more effective.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Why are cultures incubated at 25 °C in school labs?

    Answer: Pathogens that harm humans grow best at about 37 °C, so 25 °C reduces the risk of growing them

    Example 2 Standard

    A clear zone has a diameter of 12 mm. Calculate its area.

    1. r = 6 mm
    2. π × 6² = 113.1

    Answer: 113 mm²

    Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

    A bacterium divides every 20 minutes. (a) Calculate the number of bacteria after 4 hours, starting from one cell. (b) A clear zone has a diameter of 14 mm. Calculate its area.

    1. (a) 4 hours = 240 minutes; 240 ÷ 20 = 12 divisions
    2. Number = 1 × 2¹² = 4096
    3. (b) Radius = 14 ÷ 2 = 7 mm
    4. Area = π × 7² = 153.9…

    Answer: (a) 4096 bacteria; (b) 154 mm²

    1. A: π × 4² = 50.3 mm²

    Answer: A ≈ 50 mm², B ≈ 154 mm²: B is more effective

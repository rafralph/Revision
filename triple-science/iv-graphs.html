I–V characteristics and components

Physics · rank 14 of 29 · 35 marks · 3.5% of Physics marks · in 3 of 10 papers · Required practical: I–V characteristics

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

An I–V graph is a component's fingerprint. A straight line through the origin means constant resistance. A curve means the resistance changes.

2Step by step

For an ohmic conductor at constant temperature, current is directly proportional to p.d. The graph is a straight line through the origin.

For a filament lamp, the graph curves. As the current increases, the filament gets hotter. The ions vibrate more, so the resistance increases.

A diode lets current flow in one direction only. It has a very high resistance in the reverse direction.

A thermistor's resistance decreases as the temperature increases. An LDR's resistance decreases as the light intensity increases.

In the required practical, use a variable resistor to change the current. Measure V and I. Reverse the supply to get negative values.

CAUTIONResistance = V ÷ I at a point. It is not the gradient of the curve.

3Another way to see it

An I–V graph shows how much current flows for each p.d. The slope tells you about resistance: steeper = less resistance.

  • Resistor (at constant temperature): a straight line through the origin. It obeys Ohm's law.
  • Filament lamp: a curve that flattens, because the filament gets hot and its resistance rises.
  • Diode: current flows one way only, like a one-way door.
  • Thermistor: resistance falls as temperature rises (used in thermostats).
  • LDR: resistance falls as light increases (used in night lights).

4Watch out for

  • Calculating resistance from the gradient of a curve. Use R = V ÷ I at that point.
  • Saying a filament lamp's resistance falls as it heats up. It rises.
  • Mixing up the thermistor (temperature) and the LDR (light).
  • Saying a diode lets current through both ways.
  • In the practical, forgetting to reverse the connections to get negative values.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

What does a diode do?

    Answer: It lets current flow in one direction only

    Example 2 Standard

    At one point on a lamp's I–V graph, V = 3.0 V and I = 0.20 A. Calculate the resistance.

    1. R = V ÷ I = 3.0 ÷ 0.20

    Answer: 15 Ω

    Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

    Explain why the I–V graph for a filament lamp is a curve.

    1. As the current increases, the filament gets hotter
    2. The ions in the metal vibrate more, so electrons collide with them more often
    3. The resistance increases, so the current increases less for each volt

    Answer: Hotter filament → more resistance → curve flattens

    Show answer
    1. More current heats the filament.
    2. The metal ions vibrate more.
    3. Electrons collide with them more often, so resistance increases.

    Answer: Higher temperature → ions vibrate more → more collisions → more resistance

    Q6Exam-style

    Show answer

    Answer: When cold, the thermistor's resistance rises, so the p.d. across it rises; this p.d. is used to switch on the heater circuit

DNA, meiosis and genetic crosses

Biology · 70 marks · 7.9% of Biology marks · in 4 of 9 papers

The picture ideaEach parent gives one allele to each child, by chance. A Punnett square lists every equally likely combination.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

DNA is a polymer made of two strands in a double helix. A gene is a small section of DNA that codes for a protein. Meiosis makes gametes (eggs and sperm) with half the number of chromosomes. AQA tests genetic crosses, inherited disorders and sex determination.

  1. Write the genotype of each parent, for example Ff.
  2. Draw a 2 × 2 Punnett square. Put one parent's alleles across the top and the other's down the side.
  3. Fill each box with one allele from the top and one from the side.
  4. Count the boxes with each phenotype.
  5. Give the answer as a ratio, a fraction, a percentage or a probability, as the question asks.

Dominant alleles (capital letter) show with one copy. Recessive alleles show only with two copies.

CAUTIONA probability of 1 in 4 is for each child. It does not mean exactly one of four children.

Example exam question

Cystic fibrosis is caused by a recessive allele, f. Both parents are carriers (Ff). Use a Punnett square to find the probability that their child has cystic fibrosis.

[3 marks]
  1. Gametes from each parent: F or f
  2. Offspring: FF, Ff, Ff, ff
  3. Only ff has cystic fibrosis: 1 out of 4

Answer: 0.25 (1 in 4, or 25%)

Ecosystems, adaptations and sampling

Biology · 57 marks · 6.5% of Biology marks · in 4 of 9 papers Required practical: Field investigation (quadrats)

The picture ideaYou cannot count every plant in a field. Count a few random samples, find the mean, then scale up to the whole area.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

An ecosystem is all the organisms in an area and the non-living parts. AQA tests adaptations, food chains, competition, biotic and abiotic factors, and the quadrat practical.

To estimate a population with quadrats:

  1. Place quadrats at random. Use random numbers for coordinates.
  2. Count the organisms in each quadrat.
  3. Find the mean number per quadrat.
  4. Find the number per m²: mean ÷ area of one quadrat.
  5. Multiply by the total area.

To show how a population changes across a habitat, use a transect line. Put a quadrat at regular intervals along it.

CAUTIONA 0.5 m × 0.5 m quadrat has an area of 0.25 m², not 0.5 m².

Example exam question

A field measures 20 m by 10 m. A student placed 10 quadrats, each 0.5 m × 0.5 m, at random. The mean number of daisies per quadrat was 3. Estimate the number of daisies in the field.

[3 marks]
  1. Area of one quadrat = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25 m²
  2. Daisies per m² = 3 ÷ 0.25 = 12
  3. Area of field = 20 × 10 = 200 m² ; 12 × 200

Answer: 2400 daisies

Heart, blood, vessels and lungs

Biology · 56 marks · 6.3% of Biology marks · in 4 of 9 papers

The picture ideaBlood goes through the heart twice on each full lap: once to the lungs to collect oxygen, and once to the body to deliver it.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

The heart pumps blood around the body in a double circulation. AQA tests the route through the heart, blood vessels, blood components and the lungs.

  1. Deoxygenated blood enters the right atrium from the vena cava.
  2. The right ventricle pumps it to the lungs in the pulmonary artery.
  3. Oxygenated blood returns to the left atrium in the pulmonary vein.
  4. The left ventricle pumps it to the body in the aorta. Its wall is thicker because it pumps blood further.
  5. Valves stop blood flowing backwards.

Arteries have thick, elastic walls for high pressure. Veins have valves. Capillaries are one cell thick for exchange.

CAUTIONArteries carry blood away from the heart. The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood.

Example exam question

Describe the route taken by blood from the vena cava to the aorta.

[4 marks]
  1. Vena cava → right atrium → right ventricle
  2. → pulmonary artery → lungs (blood is oxygenated)
  3. → pulmonary vein → left atrium → left ventricle
  4. → aorta

Answer: Right atrium → right ventricle → lungs → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta

Digestion and enzymes

Biology · 51 marks · 5.8% of Biology marks · in 4 of 9 papers Required practical: Effect of pH on amylase; food tests

The picture ideaAn enzyme works like a lock and key. Its active site fits one substrate. Heat or the wrong pH changes the shape, so the key no longer fits.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Enzymes are biological catalysts. Digestive enzymes break large molecules into small ones that can be absorbed. AQA tests the lock and key model, temperature and pH, the amylase practical and food tests.

  1. Carbohydrase (amylase) breaks starch into sugars.
  2. Protease breaks proteins into amino acids.
  3. Lipase breaks lipids into fatty acids and glycerol.
  4. Above the optimum temperature, the active site changes shape. The enzyme is denatured.
  5. In the amylase practical, test samples with iodine. Rate = 1 ÷ time for the starch to disappear.

Food tests: iodine turns blue-black with starch; Benedict's turns brick red with sugar (heat it); biuret turns purple with protein.

CAUTIONDo not say enzymes are "killed". They are proteins, not living things. Say "denatured".

Example exam question

In a practical, amylase is mixed with starch at pH 7. Samples are tested with iodine every 10 seconds. The iodine stops turning blue-black after 40 seconds. Calculate the rate of reaction.

[2 marks]
  1. Rate = 1 ÷ time
  2. = 1 ÷ 40

Answer: 0.025 per second (s⁻¹)

Diffusion, osmosis and active transport

Biology · 48 marks · 5.4% of Biology marks · in 6 of 9 papers Required practical: Osmosis in potato

The picture ideaIn the potato practical, the line crosses zero where water stops moving overall. That concentration matches the inside of the potato cells.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Diffusion is the net movement of particles from high to low concentration. Osmosis is the diffusion of water through a partially permeable membrane. Active transport moves substances from low to high concentration and needs energy from respiration.

  1. Weigh each potato cylinder. Put them in sugar solutions of different concentrations.
  2. After a set time, blot dry and weigh again.
  3. Percentage change = (final − start) ÷ start × 100.
  4. Plot % change against concentration. Draw a line of best fit.
  5. Where the line crosses zero, the solution matches the cell sap.

CAUTIONUse percentage change, not just change in mass. The cylinders start at different masses.

Example exam question

A potato cylinder has a mass of 2.50 g. After 30 minutes in water, its mass is 2.80 g. Calculate the percentage change in mass, and explain the change.

[3 marks]
  1. Change = 2.80 − 2.50 = 0.30 g
  2. % change = 0.30 ÷ 2.50 × 100 = 12%
  3. Water moved into the cells by osmosis, from a dilute solution to a more concentrated one

Answer: +12% (water entered by osmosis)

Cell structure and microscopy

Biology · 42 marks · 4.8% of Biology marks · in 4 of 9 papers Required practical: Using a light microscope

The picture ideaMagnification just scales size. The image is the real object multiplied up, so divide to get back to the real size.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Animal and plant cells have a nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane, mitochondria and ribosomes. Plant cells also have a cell wall, chloroplasts and a permanent vacuole. Bacteria (prokaryotes) have no nucleus. AQA tests cell parts, microscopes and magnification.

  1. Use: magnification = image size ÷ real size.
  2. To find the real size: real size = image size ÷ magnification.
  3. Make the units the same before you divide.
  4. 1 mm = 1000 µm (micrometres).
  5. Give the answer in the unit the question asks for.

Electron microscopes have higher magnification and resolution than light microscopes, so they show smaller parts such as ribosomes.

CAUTIONMagnification has no units. Write ×400, not 400 mm.

Example exam question

The image of a cell is 24 mm wide. The magnification is ×400. Calculate the real width of the cell in micrometres (µm).

[3 marks]
  1. Real size = image size ÷ magnification
  2. = 24 ÷ 400 = 0.06 mm
  3. 0.06 × 1000 = 60 µm

Answer: 60 µm

Non-communicable disease

Biology · 39 marks · 4.4% of Biology marks · in 5 of 9 papers

The picture ideaIn coronary heart disease, fat builds up inside the arteries that feed the heart. A narrower pipe carries less blood, so the heart muscle gets less oxygen.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

A non-communicable disease is not passed from person to person. Examples are coronary heart disease, cancer and type 2 diabetes. AQA tests risk factors, treatments and interpreting data.

  1. Fatty material builds up in the coronary arteries.
  2. The arteries become narrower.
  3. Less blood flows to the heart muscle, so it gets less oxygen.
  4. Treatments: stents hold the artery open; statins lower blood cholesterol; a transplant replaces the heart.
  5. Risk factors include poor diet, smoking, obesity and lack of exercise.

Cancer is uncontrolled cell division. Benign tumours stay in one place. Malignant tumours spread in the blood.

CAUTIONA correlation in data does not prove cause. Say "is linked to" unless the question says it causes.

Example exam question

A patient has coronary heart disease. Explain how a stent helps the patient.

[3 marks]
  1. The stent holds the narrowed coronary artery open
  2. More blood can flow to the heart muscle
  3. The heart muscle gets more oxygen for respiration

Answer: Keeps the artery open → more blood and oxygen to the heart muscle

DecompositionTriple only

Biology · 38 marks · 4.3% of Biology marks · in 3 of 9 papers Required practical: Effect of temperature on decay

The picture ideaDecay is digestion by microorganisms. Like any enzyme reaction, it goes faster when it is warm, wet and has oxygen, until it gets too hot.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Decomposers are bacteria and fungi. They break down dead plants, dead animals and waste. This returns carbon dioxide to the air and mineral ions to the soil.

The rate of decay depends on three conditions:

  1. Temperature. Warm conditions make the enzymes work faster. Very hot conditions denature them.
  2. Water. Microorganisms need water to grow.
  3. Oxygen. Most decomposers use aerobic respiration.

Gardeners make compost quickly, so they keep it warm, moist and aerated. Anaerobic decay makes methane gas, which can be used as a fuel (biogas).

In the required practical, lipase breaks down the fat in milk into fatty acids. The fatty acids lower the pH. Measure the time for an indicator to change colour at different temperatures.

CAUTIONThe pH falls because of fatty acids, not because of carbon dioxide. Write "fatty acids" for the mark.

Example exam question

A student mixed milk, lipase and an indicator at 30 °C. The pH fell from 8.4 to 7.0 in 280 seconds.
(a) Explain why the pH fell. (b) Calculate the mean rate of pH change in pH units per minute.

[4 marks]
  1. (a) Lipase breaks down fat (lipids) in the milk
  2. This makes fatty acids, which lower the pH
  3. (b) Change in pH = 8.4 − 7.0 = 1.4 ; time = 280 ÷ 60 = 4.67 minutes
  4. Rate = 1.4 ÷ 4.67

Answer: (a) Lipase makes fatty acids; (b) 0.3 pH units per minute

Photosynthesis

Biology · 37 marks · 4.2% of Biology marks · in 2 of 9 papers Required practical: Light intensity and rate of photosynthesis

The picture ideaThe rate is held back by whichever factor is in shortest supply. Increase that factor and the rate rises, until something else becomes the limit.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Photosynthesis is an endothermic reaction. It happens in chloroplasts. The equation is: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen. AQA tests limiting factors, the inverse square law and the pondweed practical.

  1. Name the limiting factor: light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration or temperature.
  2. On a rate graph, where the line goes up, the factor on the x-axis is limiting.
  3. Where the line is flat, another factor is limiting.
  4. (Higher) Light intensity ∝ 1 ÷ distance². Double the distance gives ¼ of the light.
  5. In the practical, count oxygen bubbles per minute at different lamp distances.

CAUTIONDo not say temperature is "used up". Temperature limits the rate because enzymes work slowly when it is cold.

Example exam question

A student moves a lamp from 10 cm to 20 cm away from some pondweed. (a) By what fraction does the light intensity change? (b) Explain why the rate of photosynthesis falls.

[3 marks]
  1. Light intensity ∝ 1 ÷ distance²
  2. Distance × 2, so intensity × 1/2² = 1/4
  3. Light is the limiting factor, so less light energy means a slower rate

Answer: (a) ¼ of the original (b) light is limiting

Plant tissues and transpiration

Biology · 35 marks · 4.0% of Biology marks · in 3 of 9 papers

The picture ideaWater evaporating from the leaves pulls a continuous column of water up the stem, like drinking through a straw.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Plant organs include roots, stems and leaves. Xylem carries water and mineral ions up the plant. Phloem carries dissolved sugars up and down (translocation). AQA tests leaf structure, stomata, transpiration and the potometer.

  1. Water enters root hair cells by osmosis.
  2. Water moves up the xylem.
  3. Water evaporates from leaf cells and diffuses out through the stomata.
  4. Transpiration is faster when it is warmer, drier, windier or brighter.
  5. Potometer: rate = distance the bubble moves ÷ time.

Guard cells open and close the stomata.

CAUTIONA potometer measures water uptake, not exactly water loss. Some water is used in photosynthesis.

Example exam question

In a potometer, the air bubble moves 15 mm in 5 minutes. (a) Calculate the rate of water uptake in mm per minute. (b) Give one change that would increase the rate.

[3 marks]
  1. (a) Rate = distance ÷ time = 15 ÷ 5
  2. = 3 mm per minute
  3. (b) Use a fan / raise the temperature / increase light intensity / lower humidity

Answer: (a) 3 mm/min (b) e.g. a fan to increase air movement

Respiration and metabolism

Biology · 34 marks · 3.9% of Biology marks · in 4 of 9 papers

The picture ideaRespiration releases energy from glucose. Without enough oxygen, muscles take a short cut that releases less energy and leaves lactic acid behind.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Respiration is an exothermic reaction that happens continuously in all living cells. AQA tests aerobic and anaerobic respiration, the effects of exercise and metabolism.

  1. Aerobic: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water. It releases a lot of energy.
  2. Anaerobic in muscles: glucose → lactic acid. It releases less energy.
  3. Anaerobic in yeast and plants: glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide (fermentation).
  4. During exercise, heart rate and breathing rate increase to supply more oxygen.
  5. Oxygen debt: the extra oxygen needed after exercise to break down lactic acid.

CAUTIONRespiration releases energy; it does not "make" or "produce" energy.

Example exam question

Compare aerobic and anaerobic respiration in human muscle cells.

[4 marks]
  1. Aerobic uses oxygen; anaerobic does not
  2. Aerobic produces carbon dioxide and water; anaerobic produces lactic acid
  3. Aerobic releases much more energy per glucose molecule
  4. Anaerobic causes an oxygen debt

Answer: Oxygen use, products, energy released and oxygen debt all differ

Body temperature and the kidneysTriple only

Biology · 34 marks · 3.9% of Biology marks · in 3 of 9 papers

The picture ideaHomeostasis works like a thermostat. A receptor detects a change, a control centre compares it with the set point, and an effector reverses the change.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Homeostasis keeps internal conditions constant. AQA Triple tests body temperature and water balance.

Body temperature. The thermoregulatory centre in the brain monitors blood temperature. Receptors in the skin also send impulses to it.

  • Too hot: sweat glands release more sweat. Blood vessels near the skin dilate (vasodilation), so more heat is lost by radiation.
  • Too cold: blood vessels constrict (vasoconstriction). Muscles shiver, and respiration releases heat.

The kidneys. The liver breaks down excess amino acids to make urea (deamination). The kidneys:

  1. Filter the blood.
  2. Reabsorb all glucose, some ions and the water the body needs (selective reabsorption).
  3. Release urea, excess ions and excess water as urine.

ADH comes from the pituitary gland. More ADH makes the tubules reabsorb more water, so the urine is smaller in volume and more concentrated.

CAUTIONBlood vessels do not move up or down in the skin. They dilate or constrict.

Example exam question

A runner has lost a lot of water by sweating. Explain how the body reduces the volume of water lost in urine.

[4 marks]
  1. The water concentration of the blood falls; this is detected by receptors in the brain
  2. The pituitary gland releases more ADH into the blood
  3. ADH makes the kidney tubules more permeable, so more water is reabsorbed into the blood
  4. The runner makes a small volume of concentrated urine

Answer: More ADH → more water reabsorbed → small volume of concentrated urine

Biomass, food chains and food securityTriple only

Biology · 31 marks · 3.5% of Biology marks · in 3 of 9 papers

The picture ideaEach step up a food chain keeps only about one tenth of the biomass. Most of it is lost before the next animal can eat it.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

A pyramid of biomass shows the mass of living material at each trophic level. Producers are at the bottom. Each level is smaller than the one below.

Biomass is lost between levels because:

  • Some parts are not eaten, for example bones and roots.
  • Some food is not absorbed and is lost as faeces.
  • Much glucose is used in respiration, and the energy goes to the surroundings.
  • Some is lost as urea and carbon dioxide (waste).

Efficiency = biomass at higher level ÷ biomass at lower level × 100.

Food security means enough food for everyone. Threats include population growth, new pests and diseases, drought and the cost of farming. Solutions include fishing quotas and larger net mesh, factory farming (less movement, so less energy lost), and mycoprotein from the fungus Fusarium.

CAUTIONEnergy is not "used up". It is transferred to the surroundings, mostly as heat from respiration.

Example exam question

The biomass of producers in a field is 5000 g/m². The biomass of herbivores is 450 g/m².
(a) Calculate the efficiency of biomass transfer. (b) Give two reasons why not all the biomass is transferred.

[4 marks]
  1. (a) Efficiency = 450 ÷ 5000 × 100
  2. = 9%
  3. (b) Some parts of the plants, such as roots, are not eaten
  4. Glucose is used in respiration and the energy is transferred to the surroundings

Answer: (a) 9%; (b) e.g. not all eaten; lost in respiration (or faeces, waste)

Classification, fossils and extinctionTriple only

Biology · 27 marks · 3.1% of Biology marks · in 3 of 9 papers

The picture ideaClassification is a family tree. Species that share a recent ancestor sit on nearby branches.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Linnaeus put organisms into groups: kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species. Use the phrase "King Philip Came Over For Good Soup" to remember the order.

The binomial name has two parts: genus then species. For example, the lion is Panthera leo.

Carl Woese made the three-domain system from chemical analysis: Archaea, Bacteria and Eukaryota. Evolutionary trees show how closely species are related.

Fossils form in three ways:

  1. Parts do not decay, because the conditions for decay are absent (no oxygen, too cold or too acidic).
  2. Parts are replaced by minerals as they decay.
  3. Traces are preserved, such as footprints and burrows.

There are few fossils of early life. Early life forms were soft-bodied, and geological activity destroyed many fossils.

Extinction causes: new predators, new diseases, more successful competitors, and changes to the environment, such as climate change or a catastrophic event.

CAUTIONWrite the genus with a capital letter and the species with a small letter.

Example exam question

The tiger is Panthera tigris.
(a) Give the genus of the tiger. (b) Explain why there are very few fossils of the first life forms on Earth.

[3 marks]
  1. (a) The genus is the first word of the binomial name
  2. (b) Early life forms were soft-bodied, so they decayed and left few traces
  3. Geological activity has destroyed many of the fossils that did form

Answer: (a) Panthera; (b) soft bodies decayed; fossils destroyed by geological activity

Variation, evolution and selective breeding

Biology · 24 marks · 2.7% of Biology marks · in 5 of 9 papers

The picture ideaNatural selection is a filter on a population. The individuals that fit the environment best survive more often, so their alleles become more common in each new generation.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Evolution is a change in the inherited characteristics of a population over time. It happens by natural selection. AQA also tests selective breeding, fossils, extinction, classification and antibiotic resistance.

To explain natural selection, use these steps:

  1. State the variation. Some individuals have a different allele, often from a mutation.
  2. Name the selection pressure, for example predators, disease or an antibiotic.
  3. Say which individuals are better adapted, and that they survive.
  4. Say that they reproduce and pass on the allele.
  5. Say that over many generations the allele becomes more common.

Selective breeding is the same idea, but humans choose the parents.

CAUTIONDo not write that organisms "choose" to change or "try" to adapt. The mutation happens by chance first.

Example exam question

Some bacteria are resistant to an antibiotic. Explain how a population of antibiotic-resistant bacteria develops.

[4 marks]
  1. A mutation gives some bacteria resistance to the antibiotic
  2. The antibiotic kills the non-resistant bacteria
  3. The resistant bacteria survive and reproduce
  4. They pass on the resistance allele, so the resistant population grows

Answer: Mutation → selection by antibiotic → survive → reproduce and pass on the allele

Monoclonal antibodiesTriple only

Biology · 24 marks · 2.7% of Biology marks · in 4 of 9 papers

The picture ideaA lymphocyte makes the right antibody but does not divide. A tumour cell divides forever. Fuse them, and you get a cell that does both.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Monoclonal antibodies come from one clone of cells. They all bind to one specific antigen.

To make them:

  1. Inject a mouse with the antigen.
  2. The mouse lymphocytes make the specific antibody. Collect these lymphocytes.
  3. Fuse the lymphocytes with tumour (myeloma) cells. This makes hybridoma cells.
  4. The hybridoma cells divide quickly and make the antibody.
  5. Clone one hybridoma cell. Collect and purify the antibodies.

Uses: pregnancy tests (they bind to the hormone HCG in urine), diagnosis of disease, measuring hormone levels, finding pathogens in research, and treating cancer. For cancer, a drug or radioactive substance is attached, so it goes only to the cancer cells.

Problem: they caused more side effects than scientists expected.

CAUTIONThe tumour cell does not make the antibody. It gives the hybridoma the ability to divide.

Example exam question

Describe how monoclonal antibodies are produced.

[4 marks]
  1. Inject a mouse with the antigen; the mouse lymphocytes make specific antibodies
  2. Collect the lymphocytes and fuse them with tumour cells to make hybridoma cells
  3. The hybridoma cells divide and make the antibody; clone a single hybridoma cell
  4. Collect and purify the antibodies

Answer: Mouse + antigen → lymphocytes → fuse with tumour cell → hybridoma → clone → collect antibodies

Biodiversity and human impact

Biology · 24 marks · 2.7% of Biology marks · in 3 of 9 papers

The picture ideaMany human activities do two kinds of damage at once: they add carbon dioxide to the air, and they destroy habitats.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Biodiversity is the variety of all the different species in an ecosystem or on Earth. High biodiversity makes ecosystems more stable. AQA tests pollution, deforestation, peat bogs, global warming and ways to protect biodiversity.

  1. Deforestation and burning peat release carbon dioxide.
  2. Fewer trees means less carbon dioxide is taken in by photosynthesis.
  3. More carbon dioxide adds to global warming.
  4. Habitats are destroyed, so biodiversity falls.
  5. To protect biodiversity: breeding programmes, protecting rare habitats, hedgerows and field margins, less deforestation, recycling.

CAUTIONIn 6-mark questions, give both sides: why the activity is done (for example, land for food) and the harm it causes.

Example exam question

Large areas of tropical forest are cut down and burned to grow crops. Explain two ways this increases the carbon dioxide concentration in the atmosphere.

[4 marks]
  1. Burning the trees releases carbon dioxide (combustion)
  2. Fewer trees, so less carbon dioxide is removed by photosynthesis
  3. Microorganisms decay the remaining dead wood and release carbon dioxide by respiration

Answer: Combustion releases CO₂; less photosynthesis removes CO₂

Culturing microorganismsTriple only

Biology · 22 marks · 2.5% of Biology marks · in 4 of 9 papers Required practical: Effect of antiseptics or antibiotics on bacterial growth

The picture ideaBacteria double at each division. After n divisions you have 2ⁿ times as many, so numbers grow very fast.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Bacteria divide by binary fission. In good conditions this can happen every 20 minutes.

Number of bacteria = number at start × 2number of divisions. Number of divisions = total time ÷ time for one division.

Aseptic technique stops contamination:

  • Sterilise Petri dishes and agar before use.
  • Pass the inoculating loop through a flame.
  • Tape the lid on, but do not seal it fully, so oxygen can get in and anaerobic pathogens do not grow.

Energy changes and bond energies

Chemistry · 82 marks · 7.5% of Chemistry marks · in 8 of 11 papers Required practical: Temperature changes

The picture ideaBreaking bonds costs energy. Making bonds pays energy back. If you get back more than you spent, the reaction is exothermic.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

An exothermic reaction transfers energy to the surroundings, so the temperature rises. An endothermic reaction takes in energy, so the temperature falls.

A reaction profile shows the energy of the reactants and products. The activation energy is the minimum energy that particles need to react.

To calculate the energy change from bond energies:

  1. Add up the energy of all bonds broken in the reactants.
  2. Add up the energy of all bonds made in the products.
  3. Energy change = bonds broken − bonds made.
  4. A negative answer means exothermic. A positive answer means endothermic.

In the required practical, measure the temperature change in a polystyrene cup with a lid. The cup reduces energy transfer to the surroundings.

CAUTIONCount every bond. 2HCl has two H–Cl bonds, not one.

Example exam question

Hydrogen reacts with chlorine: H–H + Cl–Cl → 2 H–Cl.
Bond energies (kJ/mol): H–H 436, Cl–Cl 243, H–Cl 432.
Calculate the energy change. State whether the reaction is exothermic or endothermic.

[3 marks]
  1. Bonds broken = 436 + 243 = 679 kJ/mol
  2. Bonds made = 2 × 432 = 864 kJ/mol
  3. Energy change = 679 − 864 = −185 kJ/mol; negative, so exothermic

Answer: −185 kJ/mol, exothermic

  2. = 1.5 cm³/s

  4. More collisions have energy equal to or greater than the activation energy

Relative formula mass (Mr) = sum of the relative atomic masses (Ar) in the formula.

To find a reacting mass:

  1. Find the moles of the substance you know.
  2. Use the balancing numbers to find the moles of the substance you want.

The limiting reactant is the one that is used up first. It sets the mass of product.

CAUTIONUse the ratio from the equation. 2Mg makes 2MgO, so the ratio is 1 : 1.

[3 marks]

The periodic table and groups

Chemistry · 64 marks · 5.8% of Chemistry marks · in 7 of 11 papers

The picture ideaReactivity is a tug of war between the nucleus and the outer electrons. Distance and shielding decide who wins.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Elements are arranged by atomic number. Elements in the same group have the same number of outer electrons, so they react in similar ways.

Mendeleev left gaps for undiscovered elements and changed the order of some pairs, such as tellurium and iodine. The discovery of isotopes later explained this.

  • Group 0 (noble gases): full outer shell, so unreactive. Boiling points increase down the group.
  • Group 1 (alkali metals): reactivity increases down the group. The outer electron is further from the nucleus and more shielded, so it is lost more easily.
  • Group 7 (halogens): reactivity decreases down the group. The outer shell is further from the nucleus, so an electron is gained less easily. A more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive one.
  • Transition metals (Triple): high melting points, high density, coloured compounds, ions with different charges, and they are good catalysts.

CAUTIONGroup 1 loses electrons and Group 7 gains them. So the trends go in opposite directions.

Example exam question

Explain why potassium is more reactive than lithium.

[3 marks]
  1. Potassium's outer electron is in a shell further from the nucleus

  • Mass number = protons + neutrons.
  • Isotopes have the same number of protons but a different number of neutrons.

  1. Dalton: tiny solid spheres.
  2. Thomson found the electron: plum pudding model.
  3. Rutherford's alpha scattering: most alpha particles went straight through, so most of the atom is empty space. A few bounced back, so the mass and positive charge are in a small nucleus.

  5. Chadwick found the neutron.

CAUTIONUse the mass numbers, not the atomic number, in the relative atomic mass calculation.

[4 marks]

  2. = 10.8
  3. (b) Most alpha particles went straight through, so most of the atom is empty space
  4. A few were deflected or bounced back, so the positive charge and mass are in a small nucleus

  4. The delocalised electrons move through the structure and carry charge

  3. 450 °C is a compromise between a reasonable yield and a fast rate

The picture ideaA system at equilibrium pushes back. Change a condition, and the position moves to oppose the change.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

In a reversible reaction, the products can react to form the reactants again (⇌). If one direction is exothermic, the other direction is endothermic by the same amount.

In a closed system, equilibrium is reached when the forward and reverse reactions happen at the same rate. The concentrations then stay constant.

Le Chatelier's principle (Higher): if a condition changes, the position of equilibrium moves to oppose the change.

  • Temperature up: moves in the endothermic direction.
  • Pressure up (gases): moves to the side with fewer molecules.
  • Concentration of a reactant up: moves to make more product.

CAUTIONAt equilibrium the reactions have not stopped. They happen at the same rate.

2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g). The forward reaction is exothermic.
(a) Explain the effect of increasing the pressure on the yield of SO₃. (b) Explain the effect of increasing the temperature on the yield of SO₃.

[4 marks]
  1. (a) There are 3 molecules on the left and 2 on the right
  2. Equilibrium moves right, to the side with fewer molecules, so the yield increases
  3. (b) The reverse reaction is endothermic
  4. Equilibrium moves left, so the yield of SO₃ decreases

Answer: (a) Yield increases; (b) yield decreases

  4. The oppositely charged ions are held by strong electrostatic attraction (ionic bonds)

Nanoparticles are 1 to 100 nm across (1 nm = 1 × 10⁻⁹ m). They contain only a few hundred atoms.

A high surface area to volume ratio means a smaller amount of material is needed. Nanoparticles may have different properties from the same material in bulk.

Uses: medicine (drug delivery), electronics, cosmetics and sun creams, deodorants, catalysts.

Risks: nanoparticles may enter the body and cells. Their long-term effects are not yet known.

CAUTIONFine particles are 100 to 2500 nm. Coarse particles (dust) are 2500 to 10 000 nm. Do not mix them up.

Example exam question

A nanoparticle is a cube of side 20 nm.
(a) Calculate its surface area to volume ratio. (b) Give one use of nanoparticles.

[3 marks]
  1. (a) Surface area = 6 × 20² = 2400 nm²; volume = 20³ = 8000 nm³
  2. Ratio = 2400 ÷ 8000 = 0.3 (per nm)
  3. (b) e.g. sun creams, drug delivery, catalysts, electronics

Answer: (a) 0.3 /nm (3 : 10); (b) e.g. sun cream

Chemistry · 5 marks · 0.5% of Chemistry marks · in 2 of 11 papers

The picture ideaCompare the temperature to the melting and boiling points. Below melting: solid. Between them: liquid. Above boiling: gas.

Forces, work and springs

Physics · 78 marks · 7.8% of Physics marks · in 7 of 10 papers Required practical: Force and extension

The picture ideaA spring is a fair trade: each extra newton gives the same extra stretch, until you pass the limit of proportionality.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

A force is a push or a pull. Forces are vectors: they have size and direction. Weight = mass × gravitational field strength (W = mg, g = 9.8 N/kg).

The resultant force is the single force with the same effect as all the forces together. Resolve forces with a scale drawing (Higher).

Work done = force × distance (W = Fs). One joule is one newton metre.

Springs: force = spring constant × extension (F = ke). This is true up to the limit of proportionality. The spring stores elastic potential energy: Eₑ = ½ke².

In the required practical, hang masses on a spring. Measure the extension with a ruler at eye level. Plot force against extension. The gradient is the spring constant.

CAUTIONExtension is the increase in length, not the total length. Change cm to m before you calculate.

Example exam question

A force of 4.0 N stretches a spring by 8.0 cm.
(a) Calculate the spring constant. (b) Calculate the elastic potential energy stored.

[4 marks]
  1. (a) e = 8.0 cm = 0.080 m
  2. k = F ÷ e = 4.0 ÷ 0.080 = 50 N/m
  3. (b) Eₑ = ½ × k × e² = 0.5 × 50 × 0.080²
  4. = 0.16 J

Answer: (a) 50 N/m; (b) 0.16 J

Reflection, refraction, lenses and colourTriple only

Physics · 75 marks · 7.5% of Physics marks · in 6 of 10 papers Required practical: Reflection and refraction

The picture ideaLight slows down in glass. The side of the beam that enters first slows first, so the beam turns, like a car that drives onto sand at an angle.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection. Measure angles from the normal. A smooth surface gives specular reflection. A rough surface gives diffuse reflection.

Refraction: light changes speed when it enters a new material. If it enters at an angle, it changes direction. Into a denser material, it bends towards the normal.

Lenses: a convex lens brings light to a focus at the principal focus. A concave lens spreads light out. Magnification = image height ÷ object height.

A convex lens can make a real image. A concave lens always makes a virtual image.

Colour: an object looks red because it reflects red light and absorbs the other colours. A filter only lets one colour through.

CAUTIONMagnification has no unit. Both heights must be in the same unit.

Example exam question

(a) A lens makes an image 4.5 cm tall of an object 1.5 cm tall. Calculate the magnification. (b) A blue book is lit with red light only. Explain why it looks black.

[4 marks]
  1. (a) Magnification = image height ÷ object height
  2. = 4.5 ÷ 1.5 = 3
  3. (b) The blue book reflects only blue light; it absorbs the red light
  4. No light is reflected to the eye, so it looks black

Answer: (a) 3; (b) red is absorbed, nothing reflected

Generators, transformers and inductionTriple only

Physics · 56 marks · 5.6% of Physics marks · in 5 of 10 papers

The picture ideaMove a wire through a magnetic field, or change the field around a coil, and a potential difference appears. Transformers change the field all the time with alternating current.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

The generator effect: when a conductor cuts magnetic field lines, a potential difference is induced. If the circuit is complete, a current flows. The induced current makes its own magnetic field, which opposes the change.

An alternator makes a.c. It uses slip rings. A dynamo makes d.c. It uses a split-ring commutator. Microphones use the generator effect. Loudspeakers use the motor effect.

A transformer has two coils on an iron core. An alternating current in the primary coil makes a changing magnetic field in the core. This induces an alternating p.d. in the secondary coil.

Vₚ ÷ Vₛ = nₚ ÷ nₛ. For a 100% efficient transformer, Vₚ × Iₚ = Vₛ × Iₛ.

A step-up transformer has more turns on the secondary coil. The National Grid steps up the p.d., so the current is small and less energy is lost as heat.

CAUTIONTransformers only work with a.c. A direct current gives no changing field.

Example exam question

A transformer has 2000 turns on the primary coil and 100 turns on the secondary coil. The primary p.d. is 230 V.
(a) Calculate the secondary p.d. (b) The secondary current is 2.0 A. Calculate the primary current. Assume 100% efficiency.

[4 marks]
  1. (a) Vₛ = Vₚ × nₛ ÷ nₚ = 230 × 100 ÷ 2000
  2. = 11.5 V
  3. (b) Vₚ × Iₚ = Vₛ × Iₛ, so 230 × Iₚ = 11.5 × 2.0 = 23 W
  4. Iₚ = 23 ÷ 230

Answer: (a) 11.5 V; (b) 0.10 A

Space physicsTriple only

Physics · 54 marks · 5.4% of Physics marks · in 6 of 10 papers

The picture ideaLight from distant galaxies is stretched. The further away the galaxy, the more it is stretched, so space itself is expanding.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Life cycle of a star: a nebula (cloud of dust and gas) collapses under gravity to form a protostar. Fusion starts, and it becomes a main sequence star. It is stable because the outward force from fusion energy balances the inward pull of gravity.

  • A star like the Sun: red giant → white dwarf → black dwarf.
  • A much bigger star: red supergiant → supernova → neutron star or black hole.

Fusion makes elements up to iron. Heavier elements form in supernovae.

Orbits: gravity gives a circular orbit. The speed is constant, but the direction changes, so the velocity changes. A smaller orbit needs a higher speed.

Red-shift: light from distant galaxies has a longer wavelength than expected. More distant galaxies show more red-shift, so they move away faster. This supports the Big Bang theory: the universe began from a very small, hot, dense region and is expanding. Dark matter and dark energy are not yet understood.

CAUTIONRed-shift does not mean the galaxy looks red. It means the wavelength has increased.

Example exam question

Explain how red-shift gives evidence for the Big Bang theory.

[3 marks]
  1. Light from distant galaxies is red-shifted: its wavelength has increased
  2. More distant galaxies have a bigger red-shift, so they are moving away faster
  3. So the universe is expanding, which suggests it started from a very small region

Answer: Red-shift increases with distance → galaxies moving away → universe expanding from a small region

Newton's laws and terminal velocity

Physics · 52 marks · 5.2% of Physics marks · in 6 of 10 papers Required practical: Force and acceleration

The picture ideaA resultant force changes motion. Balance the forces, and the object keeps doing what it is already doing.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

First law: if the resultant force is zero, a stationary object stays still and a moving object keeps the same velocity.

Second law: resultant force = mass × acceleration (F = ma). Inertia is the tendency to stay at rest or in uniform motion.

Third law: when two objects interact, they exert equal and opposite forces on each other.

Terminal velocity:

  1. At first, weight is greater than air resistance, so the object accelerates.
  2. As speed increases, air resistance increases.
  3. The resultant force decreases, so the acceleration decreases.
  4. When air resistance equals weight, the resultant force is zero. The object falls at terminal velocity.

In the required practical, pull a trolley with a falling mass. Use light gates to measure the acceleration.

CAUTIONAt terminal velocity the object still moves. Only the acceleration is zero.

Example exam question

(a) A car of mass 1200 kg has a resultant force of 3000 N on it. Calculate its acceleration. (b) Explain why a skydiver reaches a terminal velocity.

[5 marks]
  1. (a) a = F ÷ m = 3000 ÷ 1200
  2. = 2.5 m/s²
  3. (b) At first the weight is bigger than air resistance, so the skydiver accelerates
  4. As speed increases, air resistance increases, so the resultant force decreases
  5. When air resistance = weight, resultant force is zero, so speed is constant

Answer: (a) 2.5 m/s²; (b) air resistance rises until it equals weight

Energy stores and transfers

Physics · 49 marks · 4.9% of Physics marks · in 6 of 10 papers

The picture ideaEnergy is like money moving between accounts. It moves from one store to another, but the total stays the same.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Energy stores include kinetic, gravitational potential, elastic potential, thermal, chemical and nuclear.

  • Kinetic energy: Eₖ = ½mv².
  • Gravitational potential energy: Eₚ = mgh.
  • Elastic potential energy: Eₑ = ½ke².

Energy is conserved. It cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred between stores. In a closed system, the total energy stays the same.

For a falling object with no air resistance: loss in gravitational potential energy = gain in kinetic energy.

CAUTIONSquare the velocity before you multiply by ½m. Many students square the whole answer.

Example exam question

A 0.50 kg ball is dropped from a height of 5.0 m. g = 9.8 N/kg. Ignore air resistance.
(a) Calculate the gravitational potential energy lost. (b) Calculate the speed of the ball just before it hits the ground.

[4 marks]
  1. (a) Eₚ = mgh = 0.50 × 9.8 × 5.0
  2. = 24.5 J
  3. (b) Eₖ = 24.5 J, so ½ × 0.50 × v² = 24.5
  4. v² = 98, so v = 9.9 m/s

Answer: (a) 24.5 J; (b) 9.9 m/s

Magnetism and the motor effect

Physics · 48 marks · 4.8% of Physics marks · in 6 of 10 papers

The picture ideaA current in a magnetic field feels a push. Your left hand tells you the direction: thumb for motion, first finger for field, second finger for current.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Like poles repel. Unlike poles attract. Magnetic field lines go from north to south. A permanent magnet has its own field. An induced magnet becomes a magnet only in a field.

A current in a wire makes a magnetic field. A solenoid (a coil) concentrates the field. An iron core makes an electromagnet stronger.

Motor effect: a wire carrying a current in a magnetic field feels a force. F = BIl (B in tesla, I in amps, l in metres).

Fleming's left-hand rule: thumb = thrust (force), first finger = field, second finger = current.

In an electric motor, a split-ring commutator reverses the current every half turn, so the coil keeps turning the same way.

CAUTIONIf the wire is parallel to the field, there is no force.

Example exam question

A wire 5.0 cm long carries a current of 4.0 A at right angles to a magnetic field of 0.25 T. Calculate the force on the wire.

[3 marks]
  1. l = 5.0 cm = 0.050 m
  2. F = B × I × l = 0.25 × 4.0 × 0.050
  3. = 0.05 N

Answer: 0.05 N

Wave properties

Physics · 45 marks · 4.5% of Physics marks · in 5 of 10 papers Required practical: Waves in a ripple tank and on a string

The picture ideaA wave carries energy without carrying the material. The water bobs up and down, but the wave moves across.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Transverse waves: the oscillations are at right angles to the direction of energy transfer (water waves, light). Longitudinal waves: the oscillations are parallel to the direction of energy transfer (sound). They have compressions and rarefactions.

  • Amplitude: maximum displacement from the rest position.
  • Wavelength (λ): distance from one point to the same point on the next wave.
  • Frequency (f): waves per second, in hertz.
  • Period: T = 1 ÷ f.

Wave speed = frequency × wavelength (v = fλ).

In the required practical, use a strobe light in a ripple tank, or measure several wavelengths on a vibrating string, then use v = fλ.

CAUTIONMeasure across many wavelengths and divide. This reduces the uncertainty.

Example exam question

Water waves have a wavelength of 0.12 m and a frequency of 4.0 Hz.
(a) Calculate the wave speed. (b) Calculate the period.

[3 marks]
  1. (a) v = f × λ = 4.0 × 0.12
  2. = 0.48 m/s
  3. (b) T = 1 ÷ f = 1 ÷ 4.0 = 0.25 s

Answer: (a) 0.48 m/s; (b) 0.25 s

Power and efficiency

Physics · 44 marks · 4.4% of Physics marks · in 4 of 10 papers Required practical: Thermal insulation

The picture ideaPower is how fast energy moves. Efficiency is how much of it goes where you want.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Power is the rate of energy transfer: P = E ÷ t. Power is also the rate of doing work: P = W ÷ t. One watt is one joule per second.

Efficiency = useful output energy transfer ÷ total input energy transfer. You can also use power. Multiply by 100 for a percentage.

Wasted energy is usually transferred to the surroundings by heating. To reduce it, use lubrication (less friction) and thermal insulation.

A material with a lower thermal conductivity transfers energy more slowly. Thicker walls also reduce the rate of energy loss.

In the required practical, wrap a beaker of hot water in different materials. Measure the temperature drop over the same time.

CAUTIONEfficiency can never be more than 1 (100%).

Example exam question

An electric motor takes in 1200 J of energy in 20 s. It transfers 900 J of useful energy.
(a) Calculate the efficiency. (b) Calculate the useful power output.

[4 marks]
  1. (a) Efficiency = useful output ÷ total input = 900 ÷ 1200
  2. = 0.75 (75%)
  3. (b) P = E ÷ t = 900 ÷ 20
  4. = 45 W

Answer: (a) 75%; (b) 45 W

Circuits, current, p.d. and resistance

Physics · 43 marks · 4.3% of Physics marks · in 4 of 10 papers Required practical: Resistance

The picture ideaCurrent is the flow of charge. Potential difference is the push. Resistance is how hard the path is.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Charge flow = current × time (Q = It). Potential difference = current × resistance (V = IR).

Series circuits: the same current everywhere. The p.d. of the supply is shared between the components. Total resistance: R = R₁ + R₂.

Parallel circuits: the same p.d. across each branch. The total current is the sum of the branch currents. Adding a resistor in parallel decreases the total resistance, because there are more paths for the current.

An ammeter goes in series. A voltmeter goes in parallel.

In the required practical, measure the resistance of a wire at different lengths. Resistance is directly proportional to length.

CAUTIONChange minutes to seconds in Q = It.

Example exam question

A resistor has a p.d. of 6.0 V across it. The current is 0.40 A for 2.0 minutes.
(a) Calculate the charge that flows. (b) Calculate the resistance.

[4 marks]
  1. (a) t = 2.0 × 60 = 120 s
  2. Q = I × t = 0.40 × 120 = 48 C
  3. (b) R = V ÷ I = 6.0 ÷ 0.40
  4. = 15 Ω

Answer: (a) 48 C; (b) 15 Ω

Physics · 40 marks · 4.0% of Physics marks · in 4 of 10 papers

The picture ideaPower is energy per second. A kettle's power tells you how many joules it moves every second.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

UK mains electricity is a.c. at 230 V and 50 Hz.

  • Live wire (brown): at 230 V. It carries the alternating p.d.
  • Neutral wire (blue): near 0 V. It completes the circuit.
  • Earth wire (green and yellow): a safety wire. It carries current only if there is a fault.

CAUTIONChange kW to W and minutes to seconds before you use E = Pt.

(a) Calculate the current. (b) Calculate the energy transferred in 3.0 minutes.

  2. = 10 A

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Speed is a scalar. Velocity is speed in a given direction, so it is a vector. Displacement is distance in a given direction.

Distance = speed × time. Acceleration = change in velocity ÷ time (a = Δv ÷ t).

For uniform acceleration: v² − u² = 2as.

  • Distance–time graph: gradient = speed. For a curve, draw a tangent.
  • Velocity–time graph: gradient = acceleration. Area under the graph = distance travelled.

  2. v² = u² + 2as = 0 + 2 × 2.0 × 100 = 400
  3. v = √400

Pressure in fluidsTriple only

Physics · 37 marks · 3.7% of Physics marks · in 4 of 10 papers

The picture ideaPressure under water is the weight of the water above you, spread over an area. Go deeper and there is more water on top.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Pressure = force ÷ area (p = F ÷ A). The unit is the pascal (Pa). One Pa is one N/m².

A fluid (liquid or gas) pushes at right angles to every surface.

Pressure due to a column of liquid: p = hρg (h = depth, ρ = density, g = 9.8 N/kg). Pressure increases with depth and with density.

Upthrust: the pressure on the bottom of an object is greater than on the top, because the bottom is deeper. This gives a resultant upward force. An object floats if its weight equals the upthrust before it is fully under water.

Atmospheric pressure decreases with height, because there is less air above, and the air is less dense.

CAUTIONUse depth in metres and density in kg/m³. Water is 1000 kg/m³.

Example exam question

(a) Calculate the pressure due to the water at a depth of 5.0 m. Density of water = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.8 N/kg. (b) Explain why an object under water has an upthrust.

[4 marks]
  1. (a) p = hρg = 5.0 × 1000 × 9.8
  2. = 49 000 Pa
  3. (b) The bottom of the object is deeper than the top, so the pressure on the bottom is bigger
  4. So the upward force is bigger than the downward force: a resultant upward force

Answer: (a) 49 000 Pa; (b) more pressure on the deeper bottom surface

A thermistor's resistance decreases as the temperature increases. An LDR's resistance decreases as the light intensity increases.

  2. The ions in the metal vibrate more, so electrons collide with them more often
  3. The resistance increases, so the current increases less for each volt

Radioactive decay and half-life

Physics · 31 marks · 3.1% of Physics marks · in 4 of 10 papers

The picture ideaHalf-life is a countdown that never quite ends. After each half-life, half of what is left decays.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Unstable nuclei give out radiation at random. Activity is measured in becquerels (Bq). Count rate is measured with a Geiger–Müller tube.

  • Alpha (α): 2 protons and 2 neutrons. Very ionising. Stopped by paper. Range a few cm in air.
  • Beta (β): a fast electron from the nucleus. Stopped by a few mm of aluminium.
  • Gamma (γ): electromagnetic radiation. Weakly ionising. Reduced by thick lead or concrete.

Half-life is the time for the number of unstable nuclei, or the activity, to fall to half.

Irradiation is exposure to radiation from outside. Contamination is when radioactive atoms get onto or into an object. Alpha is most dangerous inside the body.

Nuclear equations: alpha decay lowers the mass number by 4 and the atomic number by 2. Beta decay raises the atomic number by 1.

CAUTIONCount the half-lives first. Then halve that number of times.

Example exam question

A source has a count rate of 800 counts/min. Its half-life is 2 hours.
(a) Calculate the count rate after 6 hours. (b) Give the difference between irradiation and contamination.

[4 marks]
  1. (a) 6 ÷ 2 = 3 half-lives
  2. 800 → 400 → 200 → 100 counts/min
  3. (b) Irradiation: exposure to radiation from a source outside the object
  4. Contamination: radioactive atoms get onto or into the object

Answer: (a) 100 counts/min; (b) outside source v radioactive atoms on/in it

Internal energy and latent heat

Physics · 29 marks · 2.9% of Physics marks · in 3 of 10 papers

The picture ideaWhile ice melts, the energy goes into pulling the particles apart, not into speeding them up. So the temperature stays the same.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Internal energy is the total kinetic and potential energy of all the particles in a system.

Heating raises the temperature, or changes the state. During a change of state, the temperature stays constant.

Specific latent heat is the energy needed to change the state of 1 kg with no change in temperature. E = mL.

  • Latent heat of fusion: solid ↔ liquid.
  • Latent heat of vaporisation: liquid ↔ gas.

Changes of state are physical changes. The mass is conserved, and the change can be reversed.

CAUTIONDuring melting, the potential energy of the particles increases. Their kinetic energy does not change.

Example exam question

(a) Calculate the energy needed to melt 0.50 kg of ice at 0 °C. Specific latent heat of fusion of ice = 334 000 J/kg. (b) Explain why the temperature stays at 0 °C while the ice melts.

[4 marks]
  1. (a) E = m × L = 0.50 × 334 000
  2. = 167 000 J
  3. (b) The energy breaks the bonds (forces) between the particles
  4. The potential energy increases, not the kinetic energy, so the temperature does not rise

Answer: (a) 167 000 J; (b) energy changes the state, not particle speed

Specific heat capacity

Physics · 28 marks · 2.8% of Physics marks · in 4 of 10 papers Required practical: Specific heat capacity

The picture ideaSpecific heat capacity is how stubborn a material is. Water is very stubborn: it needs a lot of energy for each degree.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Specific heat capacity is the energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 °C.

ΔE = m × c × Δθ. The unit of c is J/kg °C.

In the required practical:

  1. Measure the mass of a metal block.
  2. Put an electric heater and a thermometer in the block. Wrap it in insulation.
  3. Use a joulemeter, or measure V, I and t, to find the energy (E = VIt or E = Pt).
  4. Plot temperature against energy. c = 1 ÷ (gradient × m).

The value is often too high, because some energy heats the surroundings and is not measured in the block.

CAUTIONΔθ is the change in temperature: final minus start.

Example exam question

A 2.0 kW kettle heats 2.0 kg of water from 20 °C to 70 °C. Specific heat capacity of water = 4200 J/kg °C.
(a) Calculate the energy needed. (b) Calculate the time this takes, if all the energy goes into the water.

[4 marks]
  1. (a) Δθ = 70 − 20 = 50 °C
  2. ΔE = m c Δθ = 2.0 × 4200 × 50 = 420 000 J
  3. (b) t = E ÷ P = 420 000 ÷ 2000
  4. = 210 s

Answer: (a) 420 000 J; (b) 210 s

18

Stopping distances

Physics · 28 marks · 2.8% of Physics marks · in 4 of 10 papers

The picture ideaStopping happens in two parts: the distance you travel while you react, then the distance while the brakes work.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Stopping distance = thinking distance + braking distance.

  • Thinking distance = speed × reaction time. It increases with speed, tiredness, drugs, alcohol and distractions.
  • Braking distance increases with speed, wet or icy roads, and worn brakes or tyres.

Reaction times are usually 0.2 s to 0.9 s. Measure them with the ruler-drop test.

When a car brakes, work done by friction transfers kinetic energy to thermal energy in the brakes, so the brakes get hot. Doubling the speed makes the braking distance four times larger (Eₖ depends on v²).

Large decelerations are dangerous. They need large forces, which can injure people and make the brakes overheat.

CAUTIONSpeed and thinking distance are directly proportional. Speed and braking distance are not.

Example exam question

A car travels at 20 m/s. The driver's reaction time is 0.70 s. The braking distance is 30 m.
(a) Calculate the stopping distance. (b) Give one factor that increases the braking distance.

[4 marks]
  1. (a) Thinking distance = speed × reaction time = 20 × 0.70 = 14 m
  2. Stopping distance = 14 + 30
  3. = 44 m
  4. (b) e.g. wet or icy road; worn tyres or brakes; greater speed

Answer: (a) 44 m; (b) e.g. icy road

19

Momentum

Physics · 27 marks · 2.7% of Physics marks · in 4 of 10 papers

The picture ideaMomentum is never lost in a collision. It is only shared out in a new way.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Momentum = mass × velocity (p = mv). The unit is kg m/s. Momentum is a vector.

In a closed system, the total momentum before an event equals the total momentum after it. This is conservation of momentum.

Force = change in momentum ÷ time (F = Δp ÷ t). If the time of a collision increases, the force decreases.

Safety features use this: air bags, seat belts, crumple zones, cycle helmets and soft play floors all increase the time for the momentum to change, so the force is smaller.

CAUTIONIf two objects stick together, add their masses after the collision.

Example exam question

A 2.0 kg trolley moving at 3.0 m/s hits a stationary 1.0 kg trolley. They stick together.
(a) Calculate their velocity after the collision. (b) Explain why a crumple zone reduces the force on a passenger.

[5 marks]
  1. (a) Momentum before = 2.0 × 3.0 + 1.0 × 0 = 6.0 kg m/s
  2. Momentum after = 6.0 = (2.0 + 1.0) × v
  3. v = 6.0 ÷ 3.0 = 2.0 m/s
  4. (b) The crumple zone increases the time taken for the momentum to change
  5. F = Δp ÷ t, so a longer time gives a smaller force

Answer: (a) 2.0 m/s; (b) longer time → smaller rate of change of momentum → smaller force

Gas pressure and temperature

Physics · 26 marks · 2.6% of Physics marks · in 3 of 10 papers

The picture ideaGas pressure is particles drumming on the walls. More hits, or harder hits, give more pressure.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Gas particles move randomly. When they hit a wall, they exert a force. Pressure = total force ÷ area.

At constant volume, if the temperature increases, the particles move faster. They hit the walls more often and harder, so the pressure increases.

At constant temperature, for a fixed mass of gas: pV = constant. If the volume decreases, the particles hit the walls more often, so the pressure increases.

Doing work on a gas (such as pumping a tyre) increases its internal energy, so its temperature can rise.

CAUTIONpV = constant only works if the temperature and the mass of gas do not change.

Example exam question

(a) A gas at 100 kPa has a volume of 2.0 m³. It is squashed to 0.50 m³ at constant temperature. Calculate the new pressure. (b) Explain, in terms of particles, why the pressure of a gas increases when it is heated at constant volume.

[4 marks]
  1. (a) p₁V₁ = p₂V₂: 100 × 2.0 = p₂ × 0.50
  2. p₂ = 200 ÷ 0.50 = 400 kPa
  3. (b) The particles gain kinetic energy and move faster
  4. They collide with the walls more often and with more force, so the pressure increases

Answer: (a) 400 kPa; (b) faster particles, more frequent and harder collisions

Infrared and black body radiationTriple only

Physics · 22 marks · 2.2% of Physics marks · in 4 of 10 papers Required practical: Infrared radiation (Leslie cube)

The picture ideaEverything glows with infrared. Hotter things glow more, and dull black surfaces are the best at both glowing and soaking it up.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

All bodies emit and absorb infrared radiation. The hotter the body, the more infrared it emits in a given time, and the shorter the peak wavelength.

A perfect black body absorbs all the radiation that hits it. It is also the best possible emitter.

Matt black surfaces are the best emitters and absorbers. Shiny white or silver surfaces are the worst emitters and the best reflectors.

A body stays at a constant temperature when it absorbs radiation at the same rate as it emits it. The Earth's temperature depends on the balance between absorbed and emitted radiation, and on the atmosphere.

In the required practical, fill a Leslie cube with hot water. Use an infrared detector to compare the four surfaces at the same distance.

CAUTIONCompare surfaces at the same temperature and the same distance from the detector.

Example exam question

(a) Which surface of a Leslie cube emits infrared radiation at the highest rate? (b) Explain why the temperature of an object stays constant.

[3 marks]
  1. (a) The matt black surface
  2. (b) It absorbs radiation at the same rate as it emits radiation
  3. So there is no net energy transfer, and the temperature does not change

Answer: (a) Matt black; (b) absorbs and emits at the same rate

Density

Physics · 21 marks · 2.1% of Physics marks · in 3 of 10 papers Required practical: Density

The picture ideaDensity tells you how tightly the mass is packed. Same mass in a smaller volume means a higher density.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Density = mass ÷ volume (ρ = m ÷ V). Units: kg/m³ or g/cm³. 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³.

Solids are usually denser than liquids, and liquids are much denser than gases, because the particles are closer together.

In the required practical:

  1. Measure the mass with a balance.
  2. For a regular object, measure the sides with a ruler and calculate the volume.
  3. For an irregular object, put it in a eureka (displacement) can. Measure the volume of water that is pushed out with a measuring cylinder.
  4. Calculate the density.

CAUTION1 cm³ of water is 1 ml. Read the measuring cylinder at the bottom of the meniscus.

Example exam question

A stone has a mass of 54 g. It pushes 20 cm³ of water out of a eureka can.
Calculate the density of the stone in g/cm³ and in kg/m³.

[3 marks]
  1. ρ = m ÷ V = 54 ÷ 20
  2. = 2.7 g/cm³
  3. × 1000 = 2700 kg/m³

Answer: 2.7 g/cm³ = 2700 kg/m³

Sound, ultrasound and seismic wavesTriple only

Physics · 19 marks · 1.9% of Physics marks · in 4 of 10 papers

The picture ideaAn echo is a there-and-back trip. Halve the time to find the one-way distance.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Sound is a longitudinal wave. It travels faster in solids than in gases. Humans hear 20 Hz to 20 kHz. Sound makes the eardrum vibrate.

Ultrasound has a frequency above 20 kHz. It partly reflects at a boundary between two materials. The time of the echo gives the distance. Uses: medical scans and industrial imaging (finding cracks).

Echo sounding (sonar) uses high-frequency sound to find the depth of water.

Seismic waves from earthquakes:

  • P-waves: longitudinal. They travel through solids and liquids.
  • S-waves: transverse. They cannot travel through liquids.

S-waves do not reach the other side of the Earth (a shadow zone), so the outer core must be liquid.

CAUTIONThe echo time is for the wave to go there and back. Divide the distance by 2.

Example exam question

(a) A ship sends a sound pulse to the seabed. The echo returns after 0.40 s. Speed of sound in water = 1500 m/s. Calculate the depth. (b) Explain how S-waves show that the Earth's outer core is liquid.

[5 marks]
  1. (a) Total distance = 1500 × 0.40 = 600 m
  2. Depth = 600 ÷ 2 = 300 m
  3. (b) S-waves are transverse and cannot travel through liquids
  4. S-waves are not detected on the opposite side of the Earth (shadow zone)
  5. So part of the core (the outer core) must be liquid

Answer: (a) 300 m; (b) S-waves cannot pass through the liquid outer core

Energy resources

Physics · 16 marks · 1.6% of Physics marks · in 4 of 10 papers

The picture ideaEvery energy resource is a trade-off between reliability, cost and harm to the environment.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Non-renewable resources will run out: coal, oil, gas, nuclear fuel. Renewable resources are replenished: solar, wind, hydro-electric, tides, waves, geothermal, bio-fuel.

Main uses: transport, electricity generation, heating.

When you compare resources, discuss:

  • Reliability: is it always available? Wind and solar depend on the weather. Fossil fuels and nuclear give power on demand.
  • Environment: fossil fuels release carbon dioxide (climate change) and sulfur dioxide (acid rain). Nuclear makes radioactive waste. Hydro floods valleys. Wind turbines change the landscape.
  • Cost: set-up costs and fuel costs.

Science shows the problems, but politics, money and society decide what is done.

CAUTIONIn a "compare" question, give a point for each resource. One-sided answers lose marks.

Example exam question

Compare generating electricity from wind turbines and from a gas-fired power station.

[4 marks]
  1. Wind is renewable; gas is non-renewable and will run out
  2. Wind is not reliable, because it depends on the weather; gas can produce electricity on demand
  3. Wind releases no carbon dioxide when it runs; gas releases carbon dioxide, which adds to climate change
  4. Wind has no fuel costs; wind farms take up land and change the landscape

Answer: Wind: renewable, clean, unreliable. Gas: reliable, non-renewable, releases CO₂

Atomic structure and isotopes

Physics · 15 marks · 1.5% of Physics marks · in 2 of 10 papers

The picture ideaAn atom is mostly empty space. If the nucleus were a marble, the atom would be a football stadium.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

An atom has a radius of about 1 × 10⁻¹⁰ m. The radius of the nucleus is less than 1/10 000 of the atom. Almost all the mass is in the nucleus.

The nucleus contains protons (positive) and neutrons (no charge). Electrons (negative) are at fixed energy levels around it.

  • Atomic number = number of protons.
  • Mass number = protons + neutrons.
  • Isotopes have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.

Electrons move to a higher energy level when they absorb electromagnetic radiation. They move down when they emit it. If an atom loses an outer electron, it becomes a positive ion.

Models changed with new evidence: plum pudding → nuclear model (alpha scattering) → Bohr (energy levels) → Chadwick (neutron).

CAUTIONIsotopes of an element always have the same number of protons and electrons.

Example exam question

Compare the structure of an atom of carbon-12 and an atom of carbon-14. (Atomic number of carbon = 6)

[3 marks]
  1. Both have 6 protons
  2. Both have 6 electrons
  3. Carbon-12 has 6 neutrons; carbon-14 has 8 neutrons

Answer: Same protons (6) and electrons (6); neutrons 6 v 8

Static electricityTriple only

Physics · 15 marks · 1.5% of Physics marks · in 2 of 10 papers

The picture ideaRubbing moves electrons, nothing else. The object that gains electrons becomes negative. The one that loses them becomes positive.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

When two insulators rub together, electrons move from one to the other.

  • The material that gains electrons becomes negatively charged.
  • The material that loses electrons becomes positively charged.

Like charges repel. Unlike charges attract. These are non-contact forces.

A charged object has an electric field around it. The field is strongest close to the object. A second charged object in the field feels a force.

If the p.d. between an object and the earth gets high enough, the air can break down and a spark jumps across.

CAUTIONPositive charges do not move. Always explain static with electrons.

Example exam question

A polythene rod is rubbed with a cloth. The rod becomes negatively charged. Explain why the rod and the cloth become charged.

[3 marks]
  1. Friction moves electrons from the cloth to the rod
  2. The rod gains electrons, so it becomes negative
  3. The cloth loses electrons, so it becomes positive

Answer: Electrons transferred from cloth to rod

Nuclear fission and fusionTriple only

Physics · 11 marks · 1.1% of Physics marks · in 2 of 10 papers

The picture ideaFission splits a big nucleus. Each split releases neutrons that can split more nuclei, like dominoes that knock over two more each time.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Fission is the splitting of a large, unstable nucleus, such as uranium-235.

  1. The nucleus absorbs a neutron.
  2. It splits into two smaller nuclei (daughter nuclei).
  3. It releases two or three neutrons, gamma rays and energy.
  4. The neutrons can cause more fission: a chain reaction.

In a reactor, control rods absorb neutrons, so the chain reaction is controlled. A moderator slows the neutrons down. A nuclear weapon has an uncontrolled chain reaction.

Fusion is the joining of two light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus. Some mass changes into energy. Fusion needs very high temperature and pressure, so it is hard to do on Earth.

Background radiation comes from rocks, cosmic rays, nuclear weapons testing and nuclear accidents.

CAUTIONFission splits. Fusion joins. Write the right word.

Example exam question

Describe how a chain reaction happens in a nuclear reactor, and how it is controlled.

[4 marks]
  1. A uranium-235 nucleus absorbs a neutron and splits into two smaller nuclei
  2. It releases two or three neutrons and energy
  3. These neutrons are absorbed by other uranium nuclei, which also split: a chain reaction
  4. Control rods absorb some of the neutrons, so the rate of fission is controlled

Answer: Absorb neutron → split → release neutrons → more fission; control rods absorb neutrons

Electromagnetic spectrum

Physics · 11 marks · 1.1% of Physics marks · in 3 of 10 papers

The picture ideaAll electromagnetic waves are the same kind of wave at different wavelengths. They all travel at the speed of light in a vacuum.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

Order (long wavelength, low frequency → short wavelength, high frequency): radio, microwaves, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays. Use "Rich Men In Vegas Use eXpensive Gadgets".

They are transverse waves. In a vacuum, they all travel at 3 × 10⁸ m/s. Use v = fλ.

  • Radio: TV and radio. Microwaves: satellites, cooking. Infrared: heaters, cameras. Visible: fibre optics. Ultraviolet: sun tanning, energy-efficient lamps. X-rays and gamma: medical imaging and treatment.

Dangers: UV ages the skin and increases the risk of skin cancer. X-rays and gamma are ionising: they can mutate genes and cause cancer.

Radio waves can be made by oscillations in electrical circuits, and they can induce an alternating current in an aerial.

CAUTIONHigher frequency means shorter wavelength and more energy.

Example exam question

(a) Ultraviolet light has a frequency of 1.0 × 10¹⁵ Hz. Calculate its wavelength. Speed of light = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s. (b) Give one danger of ultraviolet radiation.

[4 marks]
  1. (a) λ = v ÷ f
  2. = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.0 × 10¹⁵
  3. = 3.0 × 10⁻⁷ m (300 nm)
  4. (b) It ages the skin and increases the risk of skin cancer

Answer: (a) 3.0 × 10⁻⁷ m; (b) skin cancer / skin ageing

Moments, levers and gearsTriple only

Physics · 5 marks · 0.5% of Physics marks · in 3 of 10 papers

The picture ideaA small force far from the pivot can balance a large force close to it. That is how a lever multiplies force.

Explanation (ASD-STE100 style)

The moment of a force is its turning effect. Moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot (M = Fd). The unit is N m.

If an object is balanced, the total clockwise moment equals the total anticlockwise moment.

A lever increases the effect of a force: a small force a long way from the pivot gives a large moment.

Gears transmit turning effects. A small gear driving a large gear gives a bigger moment, but the large gear turns more slowly.

CAUTIONUse the distance from the pivot, at right angles to the force.

Example exam question

A see-saw is balanced. A 300 N force acts 0.40 m from the pivot on one side. Calculate the force needed 1.20 m from the pivot on the other side.

[3 marks]
  1. Anticlockwise moment = 300 × 0.40 = 120 N m
  2. Clockwise moment = F × 1.20 = 120
  3. F = 120 ÷ 1.20

Answer: 100 N

Classification, fossils and extinction

Biology · rank 14 of 31 · 27 marks · 3.1% of Biology marks · in 3 of 9 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Classification is a family tree. Species that share a recent ancestor sit on nearby branches.

2Step by step

Linnaeus put organisms into groups: kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species. Use the phrase "King Philip Came Over For Good Soup" to remember the order.

The binomial name has two parts: genus then species. For example, the lion is Panthera leo.

Carl Woese made the three-domain system from chemical analysis: Archaea, Bacteria and Eukaryota. Evolutionary trees show how closely species are related.

Fossils form in three ways:

  1. Parts do not decay, because the conditions for decay are absent (no oxygen, too cold or too acidic).
  2. Parts are replaced by minerals as they decay.
  3. Traces are preserved, such as footprints and burrows.

There are few fossils of early life. Early life forms were soft-bodied, and geological activity destroyed many fossils.

Extinction causes: new predators, new diseases, more successful competitors, and changes to the environment, such as climate change or a catastrophic event.

CAUTIONWrite the genus with a capital letter and the species with a small letter.

3Another way to see it

Classifying living things is like writing an address: country → city → street → house. Each step is smaller and more exact.

Linnaeus's groups: Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species. (Memory trick: King Philip Came Over For Good Soup.)

Every species has a two-part name: Genus species, e.g. Homo sapiens. Carl Woese later used chemical analysis to split life into three domains: Archaea, Bacteria and Eukaryota.

Fossils show how life changed. They form when hard parts are replaced by minerals, or when there is no decay (amber, ice, peat). Species become extinct when the environment changes faster than they can adapt.

4Watch out for

  • Putting the groups in the wrong order. Kingdom is biggest; species is smallest.
  • Writing the species name first. It is Genus (capital) then species (small letter).
  • Saying bacteria are eukaryotes. Bacteria are prokaryotes (their own domain).
  • Saying the fossil record is complete. Many soft-bodied organisms left no fossils.
  • Giving 'humans hunted them' as the only cause of extinction. There are many causes.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

The lion is Panthera leo. What is its species name and its genus?

    Answer: Genus: Panthera. Species: leo

    Example 2 Standard

    Why did Carl Woese suggest the three-domain system?

    1. New chemical analysis (of RNA) showed some 'bacteria' were very different.

    Answer: Chemical analysis showed Archaea are very different from true bacteria

    Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

    The tiger is Panthera tigris.
    (a) Give the genus of the tiger. (b) Explain why there are very few fossils of the first life forms on Earth.

    1. (a) The genus is the first word of the binomial name
    2. (b) Early life forms were soft-bodied, so they decayed and left few traces
    3. Geological activity has destroyed many of the fossils that did form

    Answer: (a) Panthera; (b) soft bodies decayed; fossils destroyed by geological activity

    Practice 6 questions · easy to exam-style

    Try each one first. Then open the answer to check your working.

    Q1Warm-up

    Which group comes between family and species?

    Show answer

    Answer: Genus

    Q2Warm-up

    Give one way fossils are formed.

    Show answer

    Answer: Hard parts are replaced by minerals; or organisms are preserved where there is no decay (amber, ice, peat); or traces like footprints are covered and preserved

    Q3Standard

    Give two causes of extinction.

    Show answer

    Answer: Any two: new predators, new diseases, more successful competitors, a change in the environment, a catastrophic event (e.g. asteroid, volcano)

    Q4Standard

    Which domain do fungi belong to?

    Show answer

    Answer: Eukaryota

    Q5Exam-style

    Explain why there are gaps in the fossil record.

    Show answer

    Answer: Early life was soft-bodied and decayed; many fossils were destroyed by geological activity (e.g. movement of rocks); many fossils have not been found yet

    Q6Exam-style

    Two animals are in the same family but different genera. Two others are in the same genus. Which pair is more closely related? Explain.

    Show answer

    Answer: The pair in the same genus: genus is a smaller group, so they share a more recent common ancestor

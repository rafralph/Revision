Hormones and blood glucose

Biology · rank 25 of 31 · 15 marks · 1.7% of Biology marks · in 3 of 9 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Blood glucose control is negative feedback, like a thermostat: a change away from normal triggers a response that brings it back.

2Step by step

Hormones are chemicals made by endocrine glands. The blood carries them to target organs. AQA tests blood glucose control, diabetes, adrenaline, thyroxine and negative feedback.

  1. Blood glucose rises after a meal.
  2. The pancreas detects the rise and releases insulin.
  3. Insulin makes liver and muscle cells take in glucose and store it as glycogen.
  4. Blood glucose falls back to normal.
  5. (Higher) If glucose falls too low, the pancreas releases glucagon. Glucagon changes glycogen back to glucose.

Type 1 diabetes: the pancreas makes too little insulin. Treat with insulin injections. Type 2 diabetes: cells stop responding to insulin. Treat with diet and exercise.

CAUTIONGlucagon and glycogen are different. Glucagon is the hormone. Glycogen is the stored carbohydrate.

3Another way to see it

Your body controls blood sugar like a thermostat controls a room. The pancreas is the sensor and the controller.

  • Sugar too high → pancreas releases insulin. Insulin is like a key: it lets glucose in to cells, and the liver and muscles store extra glucose as glycogen.
  • Sugar too low → pancreas releases glucagon. Glucagon tells the liver to turn glycogen back into glucose. (Memory trick: glucagon is used when glucose is gone.)

Hormones travel in the blood, so they are slower than nerves but their effect lasts longer. Type 1 diabetes: the pancreas makes no insulin. Type 2: the body cells stop responding to insulin.

4Watch out for

  • Mixing up glucagon (a hormone) and glycogen (a stored carbohydrate). They are spelt almost the same.
  • Saying insulin turns into glycogen. Insulin is a signal; it makes the liver convert glucose to glycogen.
  • Saying type 2 diabetes means no insulin. The cells do not respond to it.
  • Forgetting that hormones travel in the blood plasma.
  • Saying the pancreas stores glycogen. The liver and muscles store it.

Diagrams 3

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Which organ monitors and controls blood glucose concentration?

    Answer: The pancreas

    Example 2 Standard

    Explain how the body raises blood glucose when it is too low.

    1. The pancreas detects the low level.
    2. It releases glucagon into the blood.
    3. Glucagon makes the liver convert glycogen into glucose.
    4. Glucose is released into the blood.

    Answer: Glucagon → liver converts glycogen to glucose → released into blood

    Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

    After a meal, a person's blood glucose concentration rises. Describe how the body brings the concentration back to normal.

    1. The pancreas detects the high blood glucose
    2. The pancreas releases insulin into the blood
    3. Insulin causes glucose to move from the blood into cells
    4. In liver and muscle cells, glucose is converted to glycogen for storage

    Answer: Pancreas → insulin → glucose into cells → stored as glycogen

    Q1Warm-up

    Name the hormone that lowers blood glucose.

    Show answer

    Answer: Insulin

    Q2Warm-up

    Which gland is called the 'master gland'?

    Show answer

    Answer: The pituitary gland

    Q3Standard

    Give one difference between type 1 and type 2 diabetes.

    Show answer

    Answer: Type 1: the pancreas makes too little insulin (treated with injections). Type 2: cells no longer respond to insulin (treated with diet and exercise)

    Q4Standard

    Why is a nervous response faster than a hormonal response?

    Show answer

    Answer: Nerves send electrical impulses; hormones travel slowly in the blood

    Q5Exam-style

    Blood glucose rises to 9 mmol/L after a meal. It falls back to 5 mmol/L in 2 hours. Calculate the mean rate of fall.

    Show answer
    1. Change = 9 − 5 = 4 mmol/L
    2. 4 ÷ 2

    Answer: 2 mmol/L per hour

    Q6Exam-style

    Explain how thyroxine levels are kept steady by negative feedback (HT).

    Show answer
    1. High thyroxine is detected.
    2. The pituitary releases less TSH.
    3. So the thyroid releases less thyroxine.
    4. The reverse happens when thyroxine is low.

    Answer: High thyroxine → less TSH → less thyroxine (and the reverse)

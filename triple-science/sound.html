Sound, ultrasound and seismic waves

Physics · rank 23 of 29 · 19 marks · 1.9% of Physics marks · in 4 of 10 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

An echo is a there-and-back trip. Halve the time to find the one-way distance.

2Step by step

Sound is a longitudinal wave. It travels faster in solids than in gases. Humans hear 20 Hz to 20 kHz. Sound makes the eardrum vibrate.

Ultrasound has a frequency above 20 kHz. It partly reflects at a boundary between two materials. The time of the echo gives the distance. Uses: medical scans and industrial imaging (finding cracks).

Echo sounding (sonar) uses high-frequency sound to find the depth of water.

Seismic waves from earthquakes:

  • P-waves: longitudinal. They travel through solids and liquids.
  • S-waves: transverse. They cannot travel through liquids.

S-waves do not reach the other side of the Earth (a shadow zone), so the outer core must be liquid.

CAUTIONThe echo time is for the wave to go there and back. Divide the distance by 2.

3Another way to see it

Sound is a longitudinal wave of squashed and stretched particles. Humans hear from about 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz. Above that is ultrasound.

When a wave hits a boundary, some of it reflects as an echo. Measure the echo time to find a distance: distance = speed × time ÷ 2 (÷ 2 because the sound goes there and back). This is used in sonar and in ultrasound scans of babies.

Earthquakes send seismic waves through the Earth. P-waves (longitudinal) pass through solids and liquids. S-waves (transverse) pass through solids only. S-waves do not reach the far side of the Earth, which shows the outer core is liquid.

4Watch out for

  • Forgetting to halve the echo time.
  • Saying ultrasound is very loud. It is very high frequency (above 20 kHz).
  • Saying S-waves travel through liquids.
  • Saying sound travels in a vacuum. It needs particles.
  • Mixing up kHz and Hz.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

What is the range of human hearing?

    Answer: About 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz

    Example 2 Standard

    An ultrasound pulse returns 0.20 s after leaving a ship. Sound travels at 1500 m/s in water. How deep is the sea?

    1. Total distance = 1500 × 0.20 = 300 m
    2. Depth = 300 ÷ 2

    Answer: 150 m

    Example 3 Exam-style [5 marks]

    (a) A ship sends a sound pulse to the seabed. The echo returns after 0.40 s. Speed of sound in water = 1500 m/s. Calculate the depth. (b) Explain how S-waves show that the Earth's outer core is liquid.

    1. (a) Total distance = 1500 × 0.40 = 600 m
    2. Depth = 600 ÷ 2 = 300 m
    3. (b) S-waves are transverse and cannot travel through liquids
    4. S-waves are not detected on the opposite side of the Earth (shadow zone)
    5. So part of the core (the outer core) must be liquid

    Answer: (a) 300 m; (b) S-waves cannot pass through the liquid outer core

Energy changes and bond energies

Chemistry · rank 1 of 25 · 82 marks · 7.5% of Chemistry marks · in 8 of 11 papers · Required practical: Temperature changes

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Breaking bonds costs energy. Making bonds pays energy back. If you get back more than you spent, the reaction is exothermic.

2Step by step

An exothermic reaction transfers energy to the surroundings, so the temperature rises. An endothermic reaction takes in energy, so the temperature falls.

A reaction profile shows the energy of the reactants and products. The activation energy is the minimum energy that particles need to react.

To calculate the energy change from bond energies:

  1. Add up the energy of all bonds broken in the reactants.
  2. Add up the energy of all bonds made in the products.
  3. Energy change = bonds broken − bonds made.
  4. A negative answer means exothermic. A positive answer means endothermic.

In the required practical, measure the temperature change in a polystyrene cup with a lid. The cup reduces energy transfer to the surroundings.

CAUTIONCount every bond. 2HCl has two H–Cl bonds, not one.

3Another way to see it

Think of bonds as magnets stuck together. Pulling them apart takes energy (breaking bonds is endothermic). Letting them snap together gives energy out (making bonds is exothermic).

So for any reaction: energy change = energy to break bonds − energy released making bonds.

  • Answer negative → more energy given out → exothermic (surroundings get hotter), e.g. burning, neutralisation, hand warmers.
  • Answer positive → endothermic (surroundings get colder), e.g. thermal decomposition, sports ice packs.

4Watch out for

  • Doing 'made − broken'. It is broken − made.
  • Forgetting to multiply: 2 H–Cl means two bonds.
  • Missing bonds in a molecule: CH₄ has 4 C–H bonds.
  • Saying breaking bonds releases energy. Breaking takes in energy.
  • Forgetting the sign and units: −185 kJ/mol.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Is combustion exothermic or endothermic?

  1. It releases heat to the surroundings.

Answer: Exothermic

Example 2 Standard

N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃. Bond energies (kJ/mol): N≡N 945, H–H 436, N–H 391. Calculate the energy change.

  1. Broken: 945 + 3 × 436 = 2253
  2. Made: 2 × 3 × 391 = 2346 (six N–H bonds)
  3. 2253 − 2346

Answer: −93 kJ/mol (exothermic)

Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

Hydrogen reacts with chlorine: H–H + Cl–Cl → 2 H–Cl.
Bond energies (kJ/mol): H–H 436, Cl–Cl 243, H–Cl 432.
Calculate the energy change. State whether the reaction is exothermic or endothermic.

  1. Bonds broken = 436 + 243 = 679 kJ/mol
  2. Bonds made = 2 × 432 = 864 kJ/mol
  3. Energy change = 679 − 864 = −185 kJ/mol; negative, so exothermic

Answer: −185 kJ/mol, exothermic

  2. Made: 2 × 805 + 4 × 464 = 1610 + 1856 = 3466
  3. 2648 − 3466

Q6Exam-style

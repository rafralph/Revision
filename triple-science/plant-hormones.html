Plant hormones

Biology · rank 20 of 31 · 20 marks · 2.3% of Biology marks · in 2 of 9 papers · Required practical: Effect of light or gravity on seedling growth

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Auxin moves away from light. Where there is more auxin, shoot cells grow longer, so the shoot bends towards the light.

2Step by step

Plants respond to light (phototropism) and to gravity (gravitropism). The hormone auxin controls both.

  1. Light comes from one side.
  2. Auxin moves to the shaded side of the shoot.
  3. Auxin makes shoot cells on the shaded side grow longer (elongate).
  4. The shoot bends towards the light.

In roots, auxin collects on the lower side. In roots, auxin stops cells growing, so the root bends down.

Uses of plant hormones:

  • Auxins: weed killers, rooting powders, tissue culture.
  • Ethene: controls fruit ripening during storage and transport.
  • Gibberellins: end seed dormancy, start flowering, make bigger fruit.

CAUTIONAuxin has opposite effects in shoots and roots. In shoots it increases growth. In roots it decreases growth.

3Another way to see it

Auxin is a "grow longer" message made in shoot and root tips. Light and gravity make it collect on one side.

  • In shoots, auxin makes cells grow longer. Auxin collects on the shady side, so that side grows more and the shoot bends towards light (phototropism).
  • In roots, auxin slows growth. Gravity makes auxin collect on the lower side, so the upper side grows more and the root bends down (gravitropism).

People use plant hormones: auxins for weedkillers and rooting powder, ethene to ripen fruit, and gibberellins to make seeds germinate and to grow bigger fruit.

4Watch out for

  • Saying light 'pulls' the shoot. Unequal growth bends it.
  • Saying auxin makes roots grow faster. In roots, auxin slows growth.
  • Saying auxin collects on the lit side. It collects on the shaded side.
  • Mixing up ethene (ripening) and gibberellins (germination, flowering, fruit size).
  • Forgetting a control (seedlings in all-round light) in tropism experiments.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Name the plant hormone that controls phototropism.

    Answer: Auxin

    Example 2 Standard

    Explain why a root placed sideways grows downwards.

    1. Gravity makes auxin collect on the lower side of the root.
    2. In roots, auxin slows cell elongation.
    3. So the upper side grows more and the root bends down.

    Answer: Auxin on the lower side slows growth there, so the root bends down

    Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

    A seedling is lit from one side. Explain why the shoot grows towards the light.

    1. Auxin moves to the shaded side of the shoot
    2. Auxin causes cells on the shaded side to elongate (grow longer)
    3. The shaded side grows more, so the shoot bends towards the light

    Answer: Auxin on shaded side → cells elongate more → shoot bends towards light

    Q3Standard

    Why does selective weedkiller kill weeds but not grass?

    Show answer

    Answer: It is absorbed more by broad-leaved weeds; it makes them grow too fast and die, but narrow-leaved grass is not affected

    Q4Standard

    Give two uses of gibberellins.

    Show answer

    Answer: Any two: end seed dormancy (germination), promote flowering, increase fruit size

    Q5Exam-style

    The tip of a shoot is covered with foil. The shoot is lit from one side. It does not bend. Explain why.

    Show answer
    1. The tip cannot detect the light.
    2. Auxin is not moved to one side.
    3. So both sides grow equally.

    Answer: The tip cannot sense the light, so auxin spreads evenly and growth is equal

    Q6Exam-style

    Describe how to investigate the effect of light direction on seedlings.

    Show answer
    1. Grow three groups of seedlings: one in the dark, one lit from one side, one in all-round light.
    2. Keep variables the same (type, age, water, temperature).
    3. Measure height and the direction of growth daily for a week.

    Answer: Compare seedlings with light from one side, all round and none; control other variables; measure growth

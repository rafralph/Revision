Giant structures, metals and carbon

Chemistry · rank 14 of 25 · 43 marks · 3.9% of Chemistry marks · in 6 of 11 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Melting a giant structure means breaking millions of strong bonds. Conducting needs charged particles that are free to move.

2Step by step

Giant covalent structures have very high melting points, because many strong covalent bonds must break.

  • Diamond: each carbon makes 4 bonds. Very hard. Does not conduct.
  • Graphite: each carbon makes 3 bonds, in hexagonal layers. There are weak forces between the layers, so they slide and graphite is soft. One electron from each carbon is delocalised, so graphite conducts.
  • Graphene: one layer of graphite. Strong and conducts.
  • Fullerenes (such as C₆₀) and carbon nanotubes: hollow shapes. Nanotubes are strong and conduct. Uses include lubricants, drug delivery and catalysts.

Metals have a giant lattice of positive ions in a sea of delocalised electrons. The electrons carry charge and energy, so metals conduct. The layers can slide, so metals bend. Alloys contain atoms of different sizes, which distort the layers, so alloys are harder.

CAUTIONGraphite melts at a high temperature because of covalent bonds, not the weak forces between layers.

3Another way to see it

A giant covalent structure is like a huge climbing frame where every joint is welded. To melt it you must break millions of strong bonds, so the melting point is very high.

  • Diamond: each carbon bonds to 4 others. Very hard; does not conduct.
  • Graphite: each carbon bonds to 3 others in layers. Layers slide (soft); one free electron per atom, so it conducts.
  • Graphene: one layer of graphite. Fullerenes: hollow cages and tubes of carbon.

Metals are positive ions in a "sea" of delocalised electrons. The electrons move, so metals conduct. Layers slide, so metals bend. In an alloy, different-sized atoms jam the layers, so alloys are harder.

4Watch out for

  • Saying graphite conducts because of ions. It has delocalised electrons.
  • Saying diamond has weak forces. All its bonds are strong covalent bonds.
  • Saying metals conduct because ions move. The electrons move.
  • Saying alloys are harder because they are 'stronger metals'. Explain the distorted layers.
  • Mixing up graphene (one layer) and graphite (many layers).

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Explain why diamond is very hard.

    Answer: Each carbon atom forms four strong covalent bonds in a giant structure

    Example 2 Standard

    Explain why metals conduct electricity.

    1. Metals have delocalised electrons.
    2. These electrons can move through the structure and carry charge.

    Answer: Delocalised electrons move and carry the current

    Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

    Explain why graphite is soft and conducts electricity.

    1. Each carbon atom bonds to three others, forming layers
    2. There are weak forces between the layers, so the layers can slide: graphite is soft
    3. Each carbon has one electron that is delocalised
    4. The delocalised electrons move through the structure and carry charge

    Answer: Layers with weak forces slide; delocalised electrons carry charge

    Show answer

    Answer: Atoms of different sizes distort the layers, so they cannot slide easily

    Q5Exam-style

    Explain why graphite conducts electricity but diamond does not.

    Show answer
    1. In graphite each carbon uses 3 electrons in bonds, leaving 1 delocalised electron.
    2. In diamond all 4 outer electrons are in bonds, so none are free.

    Answer: Graphite has delocalised electrons; diamond does not

    Q6Exam-style

    Give two properties of carbon nanotubes and a use for each.

    Show answer

    Answer: High tensile strength (reinforcing materials such as tennis rackets); conduct electricity (electronics); high surface area (catalyst support)

Speed, acceleration and motion graphs

Physics · rank 12 of 29 · 40 marks · 4.0% of Physics marks · in 6 of 10 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

On a distance–time graph, steepness is speed. On a velocity–time graph, steepness is acceleration and area is distance.

2Step by step

Speed is a scalar. Velocity is speed in a given direction, so it is a vector. Displacement is distance in a given direction.

Distance = speed × time. Acceleration = change in velocity ÷ time (a = Δv ÷ t).

For uniform acceleration: v² − u² = 2as.

  • Distance–time graph: gradient = speed. For a curve, draw a tangent.
  • Velocity–time graph: gradient = acceleration. Area under the graph = distance travelled.

Typical speeds: walking 1.5 m/s, running 3 m/s, cycling 6 m/s.

CAUTIONIf the object starts from rest, u = 0. Do not forget to take the square root at the end.

3Another way to see it

Two graphs, two rules:

  • Distance–time graph: the gradient is the speed. Flat line = stopped.
  • Velocity–time graph: the gradient is the acceleration, and the area under the line is the distance travelled.

Speed has no direction; velocity is speed in a given direction. Acceleration = change in velocity ÷ time.

When you know distance but not time, use v² − u² = 2 a s (u = start speed, v = final speed).

Typical speeds: walking 1.5 m/s, running 3 m/s, cycling 6 m/s, sound in air 330 m/s.

4Watch out for

  • Reading the area under a distance–time graph. Area is only useful on a velocity–time graph.
  • Forgetting to square-root at the end of v² − u² = 2as.
  • Using a negative sign wrongly: slowing down gives a negative acceleration.
  • Mixing up speed and velocity.
  • Forgetting that a falling object accelerates at about 9.8 m/s².

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

A runner moves at 4.0 m/s for 25 s. How far does she run?

  1. s = vt = 4.0 × 25

Answer: 100 m

Example 2 Standard

A car speeds up from rest to 24 m/s in 8.0 s. Calculate the acceleration.

  1. a = (24 − 0) ÷ 8.0

Answer: 3.0 m/s²

Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

A car starts from rest and accelerates at 2.0 m/s² for 100 m. Calculate its final velocity.

  1. u = 0, a = 2.0 m/s², s = 100 m
  2. v² = u² + 2as = 0 + 2 × 2.0 × 100 = 400
  3. v = √400

Answer: 20 m/s

Answer: About 1.5 m/s

Q3Standard

u = 10 m/s, a = 2.0 m/s², s = 24 m. Calculate the final speed.

Show answer
  1. v² = 10² + 2 × 2.0 × 24 = 100 + 96 = 196
  2. v = √196

Answer: 14 m/s

Q4Standard

A bike slows from 20 m/s to 5.0 m/s in 3.0 s. Calculate the acceleration.

Show answer
  1. (5.0 − 20) ÷ 3.0

Answer: −5.0 m/s² (a deceleration of 5.0 m/s²)

Q5Exam-style

A car speeds up from 0 to 12 m/s in 4.0 s, then travels at 12 m/s for 6.0 s. Use the velocity–time graph to find the total distance.

Show answer
  1. Triangle: ½ × 4.0 × 12 = 24 m
  2. Rectangle: 6.0 × 12 = 72 m

Answer: 96 m

Q6Exam-style

A stone is dropped from rest. Ignoring air resistance, how fast is it moving after 3.0 s? (a = 9.8 m/s²)

Show answer
  1. v = u + at = 0 + 9.8 × 3.0

Answer: 29.4 m/s

Nuclear fission and fusion

Physics · rank 27 of 29 · 11 marks · 1.1% of Physics marks · in 2 of 10 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Fission splits a big nucleus. Each split releases neutrons that can split more nuclei, like dominoes that knock over two more each time.

2Step by step

Fission is the splitting of a large, unstable nucleus, such as uranium-235.

  1. The nucleus absorbs a neutron.
  2. It splits into two smaller nuclei (daughter nuclei).
  3. It releases two or three neutrons, gamma rays and energy.
  4. The neutrons can cause more fission: a chain reaction.

In a reactor, control rods absorb neutrons, so the chain reaction is controlled. A moderator slows the neutrons down. A nuclear weapon has an uncontrolled chain reaction.

Fusion is the joining of two light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus. Some mass changes into energy. Fusion needs very high temperature and pressure, so it is hard to do on Earth.

Background radiation comes from rocks, cosmic rays, nuclear weapons testing and nuclear accidents.

CAUTIONFission splits. Fusion joins. Write the right word.

3Another way to see it

Fission = splitting. A slow neutron hits a big nucleus (uranium-235 or plutonium-239). It splits into two smaller nuclei, 2 or 3 neutrons and a lot of energy. Those neutrons split more nuclei: a chain reaction, like one domino knocking over two, then four...

In a reactor, control rods absorb neutrons to keep the chain reaction steady. The moderator slows neutrons down so they can be absorbed by uranium.

Fusion = joining. Two light nuclei (like hydrogen) join to make a heavier one, releasing energy. This powers the Sun. It needs huge temperatures and pressures, so we cannot yet use it in power stations.

4Watch out for

  • Mixing up fission (splitting) and fusion (joining).
  • Saying control rods slow neutrons down. The moderator slows them; control rods absorb them.
  • Saying fusion is used in today's power stations.
  • Forgetting that fission also releases gamma rays.
  • Saying a nucleus usually splits on its own in a reactor. It must absorb a neutron first.

      Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

      Describe how a chain reaction happens in a nuclear reactor, and how it is controlled.

      1. A uranium-235 nucleus absorbs a neutron and splits into two smaller nuclei
      2. It releases two or three neutrons and energy
      3. These neutrons are absorbed by other uranium nuclei, which also split: a chain reaction
      4. Control rods absorb some of the neutrons, so the rate of fission is controlled

      Answer: Absorb neutron → split → release neutrons → more fission; control rods absorb neutrons

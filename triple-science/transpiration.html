Plant tissues and transpiration

Biology · rank 10 of 31 · 35 marks · 4.0% of Biology marks · in 3 of 9 papers

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Water evaporating from the leaves pulls a continuous column of water up the stem, like drinking through a straw.

2Step by step

Plant organs include roots, stems and leaves. Xylem carries water and mineral ions up the plant. Phloem carries dissolved sugars up and down (translocation). AQA tests leaf structure, stomata, transpiration and the potometer.

  1. Water enters root hair cells by osmosis.
  2. Water moves up the xylem.
  3. Water evaporates from leaf cells and diffuses out through the stomata.
  4. Transpiration is faster when it is warmer, drier, windier or brighter.
  5. Potometer: rate = distance the bubble moves ÷ time.

Guard cells open and close the stomata.

CAUTIONA potometer measures water uptake, not exactly water loss. Some water is used in photosynthesis.

3Another way to see it

Transpiration works like drinking through a straw. Water evaporates from the leaves through tiny holes called stomata. This pulls more water up the xylem from the roots, in one long chain.

  • Xylem: hollow tubes of dead cells. Carries water and minerals up.
  • Phloem: living cells. Carries dissolved sugars up and down (translocation).

Anything that speeds up evaporation speeds up transpiration: heat, wind, light, dry air. Guard cells close the stomata to save water.

4Watch out for

  • Mixing up xylem (water, up only) and phloem (sugars, both ways).
  • Saying humid air speeds up transpiration. Humid air slows it (less difference in water vapour).
  • Saying a potometer measures transpiration exactly. It measures water uptake.
  • Forgetting that light opens the stomata.
  • Saying water moves up by osmosis all the way. It is pulled up by evaporation from the leaves.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Which tissue carries water up a plant?

    Answer: Xylem

    Example 2 Standard

    Explain why transpiration is faster on a windy day.

    1. Wind blows water vapour away from the leaf.
    2. This keeps a steep concentration gradient.
    3. So water vapour diffuses out of the stomata faster.

    Answer: Wind removes water vapour, so it diffuses out faster

    Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

    In a potometer, the air bubble moves 15 mm in 5 minutes. (a) Calculate the rate of water uptake in mm per minute. (b) Give one change that would increase the rate.

    1. (a) Rate = distance ÷ time = 15 ÷ 5
    2. = 3 mm per minute
    3. (b) Use a fan / raise the temperature / increase light intensity / lower humidity

    Answer: (a) 3 mm/min (b) e.g. a fan to increase air movement

    Show answer
    1. 24 ÷ 4

    Answer: 6 mm per minute

    Q4Standard

    Explain why transpiration is slower on a humid day.

    Show answer

    Answer: The air already holds a lot of water vapour, so the concentration gradient is smaller and less water diffuses out

    Q5Exam-style

    A potometer tube has radius 0.5 mm. The bubble moves 30 mm in 5 minutes. Calculate the volume of water taken up per minute (volume = πr²l).

    Show answer
    1. Volume = π × 0.5² × 30 = 23.6 mm³
    2. 23.6 ÷ 5

    Answer: 4.7 mm³ per minute

    Q6Exam-style

    Why does a potometer not measure the exact rate of transpiration?

    Show answer

    Answer: Some water taken up is used by the plant (e.g. in photosynthesis or to keep cells turgid), so uptake is not exactly equal to water lost

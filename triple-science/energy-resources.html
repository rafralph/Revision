Energy resources

Physics · rank 24 of 29 · 16 marks · 1.6% of Physics marks · in 4 of 10 papers

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Every energy resource is a trade-off between reliability, cost and harm to the environment.

2Step by step

Non-renewable resources will run out: coal, oil, gas, nuclear fuel. Renewable resources are replenished: solar, wind, hydro-electric, tides, waves, geothermal, bio-fuel.

Main uses: transport, electricity generation, heating.

When you compare resources, discuss:

  • Reliability: is it always available? Wind and solar depend on the weather. Fossil fuels and nuclear give power on demand.
  • Environment: fossil fuels release carbon dioxide (climate change) and sulfur dioxide (acid rain). Nuclear makes radioactive waste. Hydro floods valleys. Wind turbines change the landscape.
  • Cost: set-up costs and fuel costs.

Science shows the problems, but politics, money and society decide what is done.

CAUTIONIn a "compare" question, give a point for each resource. One-sided answers lose marks.

3Another way to see it

Renewable resources will not run out: wind, solar, hydroelectric, tides, waves, geothermal and bio-fuel. Non-renewable resources will run out: coal, oil, gas (fossil fuels) and nuclear fuel.

Think of each resource as a job applicant. Ask: Is it reliable (works all the time)? Is it clean? How much does it cost? What does it do to the environment and to people nearby?

Fossil fuels are reliable and cheap to start up, but release CO₂ (global warming) and sulfur dioxide (acid rain). Wind and solar are clean but depend on the weather.

4Watch out for

  • Saying nuclear power makes CO₂ when running. It does not, but it makes radioactive waste.
  • Saying renewable means 'no environmental effects'. Dams flood valleys; turbines are noisy and change the view.
  • Saying tides are unreliable. Tides are very predictable (but output varies through the day).
  • Giving 'it is bad for the environment' without saying how.
  • Saying solar power works at night.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Name three renewable energy resources.

    Answer: Any three: wind, solar, hydroelectric, tidal, wave, geothermal, bio-fuel

    Example 2 Standard

    Give two reasons why fossil fuels are still widely used.

      Answer: Reliable (available on demand), large output, existing power stations and vehicles use them, relatively cheap

      Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

      Compare generating electricity from wind turbines and from a gas-fired power station.

      1. Wind is renewable; gas is non-renewable and will run out
      2. Wind is not reliable, because it depends on the weather; gas can produce electricity on demand
      3. Wind releases no carbon dioxide when it runs; gas releases carbon dioxide, which adds to climate change
      4. Wind has no fuel costs; wind farms take up land and change the landscape

      Answer: Wind: renewable, clean, unreliable. Gas: reliable, non-renewable, releases CO₂

      Practice 6 questions · easy to exam-style

      Try each one first. Then open the answer to check your working.

      Q1Warm-up

      What is a renewable energy resource?

      Show answer

      Answer: One that is being (or can be) replenished as it is used

      Q2Warm-up

      Give two environmental problems caused by burning fossil fuels.

      Show answer

      Answer: Carbon dioxide (global warming) and sulfur dioxide (acid rain)

      Q3Standard

      Give one advantage and one disadvantage of nuclear power.

      Show answer

      Answer: Advantage: reliable, no CO₂ when running. Disadvantage: radioactive waste; expensive to build and decommission

      Q4Standard

      Why is solar power unreliable in the UK?

      Show answer

      Answer: It produces no electricity at night and little on cloudy days

      Q5Exam-style

      A country generates 300 TWh per year, and 26% comes from wind. How much is that?

      Show answer
      1. 0.26 × 300

      Answer: 78 TWh

      Q6Exam-style

      Evaluate building a tidal barrage across an estuary.

      Show answer

      Answer: For: renewable, predictable, no CO₂. Against: very expensive, changes habitats for birds and fish, affects boats

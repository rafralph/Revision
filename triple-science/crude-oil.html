Crude oil, alkanes and alkenes

Chemistry · rank 10 of 25 · 49 marks · 4.5% of Chemistry marks · in 4 of 11 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Crude oil is a mixture of chains of different lengths. Short chains boil low and burn easily. Cracking snaps long chains into short useful ones.

2Step by step

Crude oil is a mixture of hydrocarbons, mostly alkanes (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂). It is separated by fractional distillation:

  1. Heat the crude oil so it evaporates.
  2. The vapour rises up the column. The column is hotter at the bottom.
  3. Each fraction condenses at its boiling point. Short chains rise to the top.

Shorter chains have lower boiling points, lower viscosity and are more flammable.

Complete combustion: hydrocarbon + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water.

Cracking breaks long alkanes into shorter alkanes and alkenes. Catalytic cracking: hot vapour over a hot catalyst. Steam cracking: vapour mixed with steam at a very high temperature.

Alkenes (CₙH₂ₙ) have a C=C double bond. Test: bromine water goes from orange to colourless.

CAUTIONThe atoms must balance in a cracking equation. Count C first, then H.

3Another way to see it

Crude oil is like a box of spaghetti of every length mixed together. Each strand is a hydrocarbon chain.

Fractional distillation sorts them by boiling point: short chains boil easily and rise to the top of the column; long chains stay near the bottom.

Short chains are more useful as fuels: they are runny, flammable and have low boiling points. So long chains are cracked (broken) into shorter alkanes and alkenes.

  • Alkanes: CnH2n+2, single bonds only.
  • Alkenes: CnH2n, one C=C double bond. They turn bromine water from orange to colourless.

4Watch out for

  • Saying fractions are separated by size. They are separated by boiling point (which depends on size).
  • Saying cracking only makes alkenes. It makes shorter alkanes and alkenes.
  • Saying bromine water goes 'clear'. Say colourless.
  • Unbalanced combustion equations.
  • Mixing up the general formulas CnH2n+2 (alkane) and CnH2n (alkene).

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Write the formula of the alkane with 4 carbon atoms, and name it.

  1. CnH2n+2 with n = 4

Answer: C₄H₁₀, butane

Example 2 Standard

Balance the complete combustion of propane: C₃H₈ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O.

  1. 3 C → 3CO₂
  2. 8 H → 4H₂O
  3. O: 6 + 4 = 10 → 5O₂

Answer: C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O

Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

Decane, C₁₀H₂₂, is cracked to make octane and ethene.
(a) Complete the equation: C₁₀H₂₂ → C₈H₁₈ + ____ (b) Give the conditions for catalytic cracking. (c) Give the test for an alkene and its result.

  1. (a) Carbon: 10 − 8 = 2; hydrogen: 22 − 18 = 4, so C₂H₄
  2. (b) Heat the alkane to a vapour and pass it over a hot catalyst
  3. (c) Add bromine water
  4. It changes from orange to colourless

Answer: (a) C₂H₄; (b) hot vapour over hot catalyst; (c) bromine water, orange → colourless

  1. C: 12 − 8 = 4 → 2 × C₂
  2. H: 26 − 18 = 8 → 2 × H₄ ✓

Answer: C₁₂H₂₆ → C₈H₁₈ + 2C₂H₄

Q6Exam-style

Show answer

Answer: Catalytic: hot vapour over a hot catalyst (about 550 °C). Steam cracking: high temperature with steam

Momentum

Physics · rank 19 of 29 · 27 marks · 2.7% of Physics marks · in 4 of 10 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Momentum is never lost in a collision. It is only shared out in a new way.

2Step by step

Momentum = mass × velocity (p = mv). The unit is kg m/s. Momentum is a vector.

In a closed system, the total momentum before an event equals the total momentum after it. This is conservation of momentum.

Force = change in momentum ÷ time (F = Δp ÷ t). If the time of a collision increases, the force decreases.

Safety features use this: air bags, seat belts, crumple zones, cycle helmets and soft play floors all increase the time for the momentum to change, so the force is smaller.

CAUTIONIf two objects stick together, add their masses after the collision.

3Another way to see it

Momentum = mass × velocity (p = m v). A heavy lorry and a fast bullet both have lots of momentum: both are hard to stop.

In a collision or explosion, the total momentum before = total momentum after (as long as no outside forces act). When a gun fires, the bullet goes forward and the gun recoils backward with equal and opposite momentum.

Force = change in momentum ÷ time. Make the time longer and the force gets smaller. That is how crumple zones, seat belts, air bags, helmets and soft play mats protect you.

4Watch out for

  • Forgetting that momentum has a direction: opposite directions need a minus sign.
  • Adding the masses but forgetting the stationary object's momentum is zero.
  • Saying air bags reduce the change in momentum. They increase the time, so the force is smaller.
  • Using the wrong mass after two objects stick together. Add the masses.
  • Giving momentum the wrong unit. It is kg m/s.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Calculate the momentum of a 50 kg runner at 4.0 m/s.

  1. p = mv = 50 × 4.0

Answer: 200 kg m/s

Example 2 Standard

A 0.15 kg ball at 20 m/s is stopped in 0.050 s. Calculate the force.

  1. Δp = 0.15 × 20 = 3.0 kg m/s
  2. F = 3.0 ÷ 0.050

Answer: 60 N

Example 3 Exam-style [5 marks]

A 2.0 kg trolley moving at 3.0 m/s hits a stationary 1.0 kg trolley. They stick together.
(a) Calculate their velocity after the collision. (b) Explain why a crumple zone reduces the force on a passenger.

  1. (a) Momentum before = 2.0 × 3.0 + 1.0 × 0 = 6.0 kg m/s
  2. Momentum after = 6.0 = (2.0 + 1.0) × v
  3. v = 6.0 ÷ 3.0 = 2.0 m/s
  4. (b) The crumple zone increases the time taken for the momentum to change
  5. F = Δp ÷ t, so a longer time gives a smaller force

Answer: (a) 2.0 m/s; (b) longer time → smaller rate of change of momentum → smaller force

Q3Standard

A 2.0 kg gun fires a 0.010 kg bullet at 400 m/s. Calculate the recoil speed of the gun.

Show answer
  1. Bullet momentum = 0.010 × 400 = 4.0 kg m/s
  2. Gun: 4.0 ÷ 2.0

Answer: 2.0 m/s (backwards)

Q4Standard

Explain how an air bag reduces injury.

Show answer

Answer: It increases the time taken for the person to stop, so the rate of change of momentum (the force) is smaller

Q5Exam-style

Two trolleys (1.0 kg and 3.0 kg) at rest push apart. The 1.0 kg trolley moves at 1.5 m/s. Find the velocity of the 3.0 kg trolley.

Show answer
  1. Total momentum before = 0
  2. 1.0 × 1.5 = 3.0 × v → v = 0.50

Answer: 0.50 m/s in the opposite direction

Q6Exam-style

A 1200 kg car at 15 m/s stops in 0.10 s in a crash. With a crumple zone it stops in 0.30 s. Calculate both forces.

Show answer
  1. Δp = 1200 × 15 = 18 000 kg m/s
  2. 18 000 ÷ 0.10 = 180 000 N
  3. 18 000 ÷ 0.30 = 60 000 N

Answer: 180 000 N without, 60 000 N with (3 times smaller)

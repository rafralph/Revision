Reproductive hormones and contraception

Biology · rank 28 of 31 · 13 marks · 1.5% of Biology marks · in 2 of 9 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Four hormones run the cycle in sequence. Each one switches the next on or off, a little like a relay race.

2Step by step

The menstrual cycle lasts about 28 days. Four hormones control it. AQA tests what each hormone does, hormonal and non-hormonal contraception, and IVF.

  1. FSH (from the pituitary gland) makes an egg mature in the ovary. It also stimulates the ovary to make oestrogen.
  2. Oestrogen builds up the uterus lining. (Higher) It inhibits FSH and stimulates LH.
  3. LH (from the pituitary) causes ovulation, the release of an egg, at about day 14.
  4. Progesterone keeps the uterus lining in place. (Higher) It inhibits FSH and LH.
  5. When progesterone falls, the lining breaks down. This is the period.

The contraceptive pill contains oestrogen and/or progesterone. They stop FSH, so no egg matures. Barrier methods, such as condoms, stop sperm reaching the egg.

CAUTIONLH causes ovulation, not FSH. FSH makes the egg mature.

3Another way to see it

Think of the cycle as a relay race with four runners (hormones), each passing the baton on.

  1. FSH (from the pituitary) makes an egg mature in the ovary. It also makes the ovary release oestrogen.
  2. Oestrogen (from the ovary) builds up the uterus lining and stops FSH. It triggers LH.
  3. LH (from the pituitary) makes the ovary release the egg: ovulation, around day 14.
  4. Progesterone keeps the lining thick, ready for a baby. If there is no pregnancy, it falls and the lining breaks down (a period).

Hormonal contraceptives use oestrogen and/or progesterone to stop eggs maturing or being released.

4Watch out for

  • Saying the uterus releases the egg. The ovary releases it.
  • Mixing up FSH (matures the egg) and LH (releases the egg).
  • Saying the pituitary makes oestrogen. The ovary makes oestrogen and progesterone.
  • Saying condoms work like the pill. Condoms are a barrier; they also protect against STIs.
  • Forgetting that IVF needs FSH and LH to make several eggs mature.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Which hormone keeps the lining of the uterus thick after ovulation?

    Answer: Progesterone

    Example 2 Standard

    Explain how giving FSH and LH can help a woman who cannot get pregnant.

    1. FSH makes eggs mature.
    2. LH makes the ovary release the eggs.
    3. So eggs are released and can be fertilised.

    Answer: FSH matures eggs; LH triggers their release

    Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

    (a) Which hormone causes ovulation? (b) Explain how a contraceptive pill containing oestrogen prevents pregnancy.

    1. (a) LH
    2. (b) Oestrogen inhibits the release of FSH
    3. so no egg matures (and none is released)

    Answer: (a) LH (b) oestrogen inhibits FSH, so no egg matures

    Q4Standard

    Give one advantage of a condom compared with the pill.

    Show answer

    Answer: It also protects against sexually transmitted infections (and has no hormonal side effects)

    Q5Exam-style

    Describe the main stages of IVF. [4 marks]

    Show answer
    1. The woman is given FSH and LH so several eggs mature.
    2. Eggs are collected from her ovaries.
    3. They are fertilised by sperm in the lab.
    4. Embryos grow, then one or two are put into her uterus.

    Answer: Hormones → collect eggs → fertilise in lab → insert embryos

    Q6Exam-style

    Give two disadvantages of IVF.

    Show answer

    Answer: Any two: low success rate; emotionally and physically stressful; expensive; risk of multiple births

Forces, work and springs

Physics · rank 1 of 29 · 78 marks · 7.8% of Physics marks · in 7 of 10 papers · Required practical: Force and extension

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

A spring is a fair trade: each extra newton gives the same extra stretch, until you pass the limit of proportionality.

2Step by step

A force is a push or a pull. Forces are vectors: they have size and direction. Weight = mass × gravitational field strength (W = mg, g = 9.8 N/kg).

The resultant force is the single force with the same effect as all the forces together. Resolve forces with a scale drawing (Higher).

Work done = force × distance (W = Fs). One joule is one newton metre.

Springs: force = spring constant × extension (F = ke). This is true up to the limit of proportionality. The spring stores elastic potential energy: Eₑ = ½ke².

In the required practical, hang masses on a spring. Measure the extension with a ruler at eye level. Plot force against extension. The gradient is the spring constant.

CAUTIONExtension is the increase in length, not the total length. Change cm to m before you calculate.

3Another way to see it

A spring is like a rubber band that follows a rule: double the force, double the stretch. This is Hooke's law: F = k × e, where k (the spring constant) tells you how stiff the spring is. The rule works only up to the limit of proportionality; after that the graph curves.

A stretched spring stores elastic potential energy: E = ½ k e².

Work done means energy transferred by a force: W = F × s (force × distance moved in the direction of the force). Pushing a box 3 m with 20 N transfers 60 J.

Weight is the force of gravity on a mass: W = m × g (g = 9.8 N/kg on Earth).

4Watch out for

  • Using the total length instead of the extension (new length − original length).
  • Forgetting to change cm to m before using F = ke.
  • Forgetting to square e in E = ½ke².
  • Mixing up mass (kg) and weight (N).
  • Saying work is done when nothing moves. The force must move something.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Calculate the weight of a 5.0 kg mass. (g = 9.8 N/kg)

  1. W = mg = 5.0 × 9.8

Answer: 49 N

Example 2 Standard

A 20 N force pushes a box 3.0 m. Calculate the work done.

  1. W = Fs = 20 × 3.0

Answer: 60 J

Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

A force of 4.0 N stretches a spring by 8.0 cm.
(a) Calculate the spring constant. (b) Calculate the elastic potential energy stored.

  1. (a) e = 8.0 cm = 0.080 m
  2. k = F ÷ e = 4.0 ÷ 0.080 = 50 N/m
  3. (b) Eₑ = ½ × k × e² = 0.5 × 50 × 0.080²
  4. = 0.16 J

Answer: (a) 50 N/m; (b) 0.16 J

Q6Exam-style

A 0.20 kg mass hangs on a spring (k = 25 N/m). Calculate the extension in cm. (g = 9.8 N/kg)

Show answer
  1. F = mg = 0.20 × 9.8 = 1.96 N
  2. e = F ÷ k = 1.96 ÷ 25 = 0.0784 m

Answer: 7.8 cm

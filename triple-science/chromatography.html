Purity, formulations, chromatography and gas tests

Chemistry · rank 15 of 25 · 43 marks · 3.9% of Chemistry marks · in 5 of 11 papers · Required practical: Chromatography

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

In chromatography, each substance runs a race against the solvent. Its Rf value is how far it gets as a fraction of the solvent's distance.

2Step by step

A pure substance contains one element or compound. It melts and boils at fixed temperatures. Impurities lower the melting point and widen its range.

A formulation is a mixture designed for a use, such as paint, fuel or medicine.

Chromatography:

  1. Draw a start line in pencil. Ink would dissolve and run.
  2. Put spots of the samples on the line.
  3. Stand the paper in solvent below the line. Let the solvent rise.
  4. Rf = distance moved by substance ÷ distance moved by solvent.

A pure substance gives one spot in every solvent.

Gas tests:

  • Hydrogen: lighted splint gives a squeaky pop.
  • Oxygen: relights a glowing splint.
  • Carbon dioxide: limewater turns milky (cloudy).
  • Chlorine: bleaches damp litmus paper white.

CAUTIONMeasure the solvent distance from the start line, not from the bottom of the paper.

3Another way to see it

Chromatography is a race up the paper. The solvent (mobile phase) climbs the paper (stationary phase) and carries the dyes with it. Each dye spends a different amount of time stuck to the paper, so they finish at different heights.

Rf = distance moved by the spot ÷ distance moved by the solvent. Rf is always between 0 and 1.

A pure substance gives one spot and melts at one sharp temperature. A formulation is a mixture made to a recipe for a job, like paint, medicines or fertilisers.

Gas tests: hydrogen – squeaky pop; oxygen – relights a glowing splint; carbon dioxide – limewater turns milky; chlorine – bleaches damp litmus paper.

4Watch out for

  • Dividing the wrong way: Rf = spot ÷ solvent, never more than 1.
  • Drawing the start line in pen. Use pencil (ink would run).
  • Putting the start line below the solvent. The spots would dissolve into the solvent.
  • Saying 'pure' means natural. In chemistry it means a single substance.
  • Saying limewater 'goes cloudy' for oxygen. Limewater tests for CO₂.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

What is a formulation?

    Answer: A mixture designed as a useful product, with measured amounts of each part (e.g. paint, medicine)

    Example 2 Standard

    A spot moves 2.4 cm. The solvent moves 6.0 cm. Calculate the Rf value.

    1. 2.4 ÷ 6.0

    Answer: 0.40

    Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

    In a chromatogram, the solvent moved 8.0 cm from the start line. A dye moved 3.6 cm.
    (a) Calculate the Rf value of the dye. (b) Explain why the start line is drawn in pencil.

    1. (a) Rf = distance moved by dye ÷ distance moved by solvent
    2. = 3.6 ÷ 8.0 = 0.45
    3. (b) Pencil does not dissolve in the solvent, so it does not move up the paper

    Answer: (a) 0.45; (b) pencil is insoluble, so it does not run

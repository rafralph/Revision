Reversible reactions and equilibrium

Chemistry · rank 19 of 25 · 30 marks · 2.7% of Chemistry marks · in 4 of 11 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

A system at equilibrium pushes back. Change a condition, and the position moves to oppose the change.

2Step by step

In a reversible reaction, the products can react to form the reactants again (⇌). If one direction is exothermic, the other direction is endothermic by the same amount.

In a closed system, equilibrium is reached when the forward and reverse reactions happen at the same rate. The concentrations then stay constant.

Le Chatelier's principle (Higher): if a condition changes, the position of equilibrium moves to oppose the change.

  • Temperature up: moves in the endothermic direction.
  • Pressure up (gases): moves to the side with fewer molecules.
  • Concentration of a reactant up: moves to make more product.

CAUTIONAt equilibrium the reactions have not stopped. They happen at the same rate.

3Another way to see it

In a reversible reaction (⇌), products can turn back into reactants. In a closed container it reaches dynamic equilibrium: the forward and backward reactions go at the same rate, like walking up a down escalator at exactly its speed. Things are still happening, but nothing seems to change.

Le Chatelier's principle: an equilibrium is stubborn. If you change something, it shifts to undo the change.

  • Heat it up → it shifts in the endothermic direction (to use up heat).
  • Increase pressure → it shifts to the side with fewer gas molecules.
  • Add more reactant → it makes more product.

4Watch out for

  • Saying the reaction stops at equilibrium. Both reactions keep going at the same rate.
  • Saying the amounts of reactants and products are equal. Their concentrations stay constant, not equal.
  • Counting all molecules for pressure. Count only gas molecules.
  • Saying a catalyst changes the position of equilibrium. It only gets there faster.
  • Forgetting that if the forward reaction is exothermic, the reverse is endothermic by the same amount.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

What does the symbol ⇌ mean?

    Answer: The reaction is reversible

    Example 2 Standard

    Blue hydrated copper sulfate is heated and turns white. Water is added and it turns blue and gets hot. Explain.

    1. Heating removes water (endothermic).
    2. Adding water reverses it, releasing the same energy (exothermic).

    Answer: A reversible reaction: endothermic one way, exothermic the other

    Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

    2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g). The forward reaction is exothermic.
    (a) Explain the effect of increasing the pressure on the yield of SO₃. (b) Explain the effect of increasing the temperature on the yield of SO₃.

    1. (a) There are 3 molecules on the left and 2 on the right
    2. Equilibrium moves right, to the side with fewer molecules, so the yield increases
    3. (b) The reverse reaction is endothermic
    4. Equilibrium moves left, so the yield of SO₃ decreases

    Answer: (a) Yield increases; (b) yield decreases

    Answer: A closed system (nothing can get in or out)

    Q3Standard

    N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂. The forward reaction is endothermic. What happens to the amount of NO₂ if the temperature rises?

    Show answer

    Answer: It increases (the equilibrium shifts in the endothermic, forward direction)

    Q4Standard

    For N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g), what happens if the pressure is increased?

    Show answer
    1. Left: 1 molecule. Right: 2 molecules.

    Answer: It shifts left (to fewer gas molecules): less NO₂

    Q5Exam-style

    H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g). Explain the effect of increasing pressure on the yield of HI.

    Show answer
    1. Left: 2 gas molecules. Right: 2 gas molecules.

    Answer: No effect: both sides have the same number of gas molecules

    Q6Exam-style

    Explain the effect of adding a catalyst to a reaction at equilibrium.

    Show answer

    Answer: No change to the position or yield; it speeds up both forward and reverse reactions equally, so equilibrium is reached faster

Rates of reaction

Chemistry · rank 2 of 25 · 73 marks · 6.6% of Chemistry marks · in 5 of 11 papers · Required practical: Rates of reaction

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Particles must collide with enough energy to react. Anything that makes useful collisions more frequent makes the reaction faster.

2Step by step

Mean rate of reaction = quantity of product formed ÷ time taken. Units can be g/s, cm³/s or mol/s.

For the rate at one moment, draw a tangent to the curve and find its gradient.

Collision theory: particles must collide with energy equal to or more than the activation energy. Four factors change the rate:

  • Temperature: particles move faster, so they collide more often and more collisions have enough energy.
  • Concentration or pressure: more particles in the same volume, so more frequent collisions.
  • Surface area: smaller pieces have more surface exposed, so more frequent collisions.
  • Catalyst: gives a different pathway with a lower activation energy. It is not used up.

In the required practical, measure the volume of gas, or time how long a cross takes to disappear.

CAUTIONWrite "more frequent collisions", not just "more collisions". The mark is for frequency.

3Another way to see it

Particles are like dodgem cars. A reaction only happens when they collide with enough energy (the activation energy). To speed it up, make collisions happen more often or with more energy:

  • Temperature: particles move faster, so they collide more often and more of the collisions have enough energy.
  • Concentration / pressure: more particles in the same space, so more frequent collisions.
  • Surface area: powder has more exposed particles than a lump.
  • Catalyst: gives an easier route with a lower activation energy. It is not used up.

4Watch out for

  • Saying particles collide 'more' without saying 'more often' (frequency).
  • Saying a catalyst gives particles more energy. It lowers the activation energy.
  • Saying higher temperature only increases collisions. It also gives more particles enough energy.
  • Mixing up mean rate (total ÷ time) and rate at a moment (gradient of a tangent).
  • Forgetting units: cm³/s or g/s.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Give two ways to increase the rate of a reaction.

    Answer: Any two: heat it, increase concentration, increase pressure (gases), use a powder, add a catalyst

    Example 2 Standard

    A flask loses 0.8 g of mass in 40 s as gas escapes. Calculate the mean rate.

    1. 0.8 ÷ 40

    Answer: 0.02 g/s

    Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

    Marble chips react with hydrochloric acid. 60 cm³ of gas forms in the first 40 seconds.
    (a) Calculate the mean rate of reaction. (b) Explain why the reaction is faster at a higher temperature.

    1. (a) Mean rate = 60 ÷ 40
    2. = 1.5 cm³/s
    3. (b) Particles have more energy and move faster, so collisions are more frequent
    4. More collisions have energy equal to or greater than the activation energy

    Answer: (a) 1.5 cm³/s; (b) more frequent collisions, more of them successful

    Answer: It speeds up a reaction without being used up, by giving a route with a lower activation energy

    Answer: It has a larger surface area, so more particles can collide

    Q3Standard

    48 cm³ of gas is made in 2 minutes. Calculate the mean rate in cm³/s.

    Show answer
    1. 2 min = 120 s
    2. 48 ÷ 120

    Answer: 0.4 cm³/s

    Q4Standard

    Explain why increasing the temperature increases the rate.

    Show answer

    Answer: Particles move faster, so they collide more often, and more collisions have energy above the activation energy

    Q5Exam-style

    A tangent to a gas volume graph goes through (10 s, 20 cm³) and (50 s, 60 cm³). Calculate the rate at that point.

    Show answer
    1. Gradient = (60 − 20) ÷ (50 − 10) = 40 ÷ 40

    Answer: 1.0 cm³/s

    Q6Exam-style

    In the 'disappearing cross' practical, the cross vanished in 50 s. At double the concentration it vanished in 25 s. Calculate both rates (1/t) and describe the pattern.

    Show answer
    1. 1 ÷ 50 = 0.020 s⁻¹
    2. 1 ÷ 25 = 0.040 s⁻¹

    Answer: 0.020 and 0.040 s⁻¹: doubling the concentration doubles the rate

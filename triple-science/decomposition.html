Decomposition

Biology · rank 8 of 31 · 38 marks · 4.3% of Biology marks · in 3 of 9 papers · Required practical: Effect of temperature on decay

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Decay is digestion by microorganisms. Like any enzyme reaction, it goes faster when it is warm, wet and has oxygen, until it gets too hot.

2Step by step

Decomposers are bacteria and fungi. They break down dead plants, dead animals and waste. This returns carbon dioxide to the air and mineral ions to the soil.

The rate of decay depends on three conditions:

  1. Temperature. Warm conditions make the enzymes work faster. Very hot conditions denature them.
  2. Water. Microorganisms need water to grow.
  3. Oxygen. Most decomposers use aerobic respiration.

Gardeners make compost quickly, so they keep it warm, moist and aerated. Anaerobic decay makes methane gas, which can be used as a fuel (biogas).

In the required practical, lipase breaks down the fat in milk into fatty acids. The fatty acids lower the pH. Measure the time for an indicator to change colour at different temperatures.

CAUTIONThe pH falls because of fatty acids, not because of carbon dioxide. Write "fatty acids" for the mark.

3Another way to see it

Decomposers (bacteria and fungi) are nature's recycling team. They feed on dead things and waste, and they need what you need: warmth, water and oxygen.

  • Warm (up to a point): their enzymes work faster.
  • Moist: they need water to grow.
  • Oxygen: most decomposers respire aerobically.

Gardeners make compost and keep it warm, damp and well aired. Without oxygen, decay is anaerobic and makes methane, which can be collected as biogas for fuel.

In the lipase practical, milk fat breaks down into fatty acids, so the pH falls. Faster pH fall = faster decay.

4Watch out for

  • Saying decomposers like it as hot as possible. Too hot denatures their enzymes.
  • Forgetting that compost needs oxygen (it is turned to let air in).
  • Saying the fridge kills microorganisms. Cold just slows them down.
  • Mixing up compost (aerobic) and biogas (anaerobic, methane).
  • Forgetting the units when calculating a rate of pH change.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Name the two main groups of decomposers.

    Answer: Bacteria and fungi

    Example 2 Standard

    Explain why gardeners turn their compost heaps.

    1. Turning lets oxygen in.
    2. Decomposers respire aerobically, so decay is faster.

    Answer: To add oxygen so aerobic decay is faster

    Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

    A student mixed milk, lipase and an indicator at 30 °C. The pH fell from 8.4 to 7.0 in 280 seconds.
    (a) Explain why the pH fell. (b) Calculate the mean rate of pH change in pH units per minute.

    1. (a) Lipase breaks down fat (lipids) in the milk
    2. This makes fatty acids, which lower the pH
    3. (b) Change in pH = 8.4 − 7.0 = 1.4 ; time = 280 ÷ 60 = 4.67 minutes
    4. Rate = 1.4 ÷ 4.67

    Answer: (a) Lipase makes fatty acids; (b) 0.3 pH units per minute

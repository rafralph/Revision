Infrared and black body radiation

Physics · rank 21 of 29 · 22 marks · 2.2% of Physics marks · in 4 of 10 papers · Required practical: Infrared radiation (Leslie cube)

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Everything glows with infrared. Hotter things glow more, and dull black surfaces are the best at both glowing and soaking it up.

2Step by step

All bodies emit and absorb infrared radiation. The hotter the body, the more infrared it emits in a given time, and the shorter the peak wavelength.

A perfect black body absorbs all the radiation that hits it. It is also the best possible emitter.

Matt black surfaces are the best emitters and absorbers. Shiny white or silver surfaces are the worst emitters and the best reflectors.

A body stays at a constant temperature when it absorbs radiation at the same rate as it emits it. The Earth's temperature depends on the balance between absorbed and emitted radiation, and on the atmosphere.

In the required practical, fill a Leslie cube with hot water. Use an infrared detector to compare the four surfaces at the same distance.

CAUTIONCompare surfaces at the same temperature and the same distance from the detector.

3Another way to see it

Everything gives out (emits) and takes in (absorbs) infrared radiation. Hotter objects emit more, and at shorter wavelengths: an iron glows red, then white, as it gets hotter.

Matt black surfaces are the best absorbers and the best emitters. Shiny silver surfaces are the worst at both (they reflect). A perfect black body absorbs all radiation that hits it.

An object stays at a steady temperature when it absorbs and emits energy at the same rate. The Earth's temperature depends on this balance, and greenhouse gases change it.

4Watch out for

  • Saying good absorbers are poor emitters. Good absorbers are also good emitters.
  • Saying shiny surfaces absorb more. They reflect most radiation.
  • Saying cold objects do not emit infrared. All objects emit some.
  • In the Leslie cube practical, measuring at different distances. Keep the distance the same.
  • Saying a hotter object emits only one wavelength.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

What is a perfect black body?

    Answer: An object that absorbs all the radiation that hits it (and is the best possible emitter)

    Example 2 Standard

    What happens to the radiation from an object as it gets hotter?

      Answer: It emits more radiation, and more at shorter wavelengths

      Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

      (a) Which surface of a Leslie cube emits infrared radiation at the highest rate? (b) Explain why the temperature of an object stays constant.

      1. (a) The matt black surface
      2. (b) It absorbs radiation at the same rate as it emits radiation
      3. So there is no net energy transfer, and the temperature does not change

      Answer: (a) Matt black; (b) absorbs and emits at the same rate

      Q3Standard

      Why does an object's temperature stay constant?

      Show answer

      Answer: It absorbs and emits radiation at the same rate

      Q4Standard

      Name two things that affect the temperature of the Earth.

      Show answer

      Answer: The rate it absorbs radiation and the rate it emits it (affected by greenhouse gases, clouds, ice)

      Q5Exam-style

      A Leslie cube gives readings of 45, 38, 30 and 20 units for its four faces: matt black, shiny black, matt white and shiny silver. Match them.

      Show answer

      Answer: 45 matt black, 38 shiny black, 30 matt white, 20 shiny silver

      1. Fill a Leslie cube with hot water.

      Answer: Leslie cube + IR detector at a fixed distance from each face

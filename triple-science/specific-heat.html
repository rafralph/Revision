Specific heat capacity

Physics · rank 17 of 29 · 28 marks · 2.8% of Physics marks · in 4 of 10 papers · Required practical: Specific heat capacity

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Specific heat capacity is how stubborn a material is. Water is very stubborn: it needs a lot of energy for each degree.

2Step by step

Specific heat capacity is the energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 °C.

ΔE = m × c × Δθ. The unit of c is J/kg °C.

In the required practical:

  1. Measure the mass of a metal block.
  2. Put an electric heater and a thermometer in the block. Wrap it in insulation.
  3. Use a joulemeter, or measure V, I and t, to find the energy (E = VIt or E = Pt).
  4. Plot temperature against energy. c = 1 ÷ (gradient × m).

The value is often too high, because some energy heats the surroundings and is not measured in the block.

CAUTIONΔθ is the change in temperature: final minus start.

3Another way to see it

Some materials need lots of energy to warm up; others heat up quickly. A swimming pool takes ages to warm, but a metal spoon in tea gets hot fast. Specific heat capacity (c) is the energy needed to raise 1 kg by 1 °C.

E = m c Δθ (Δθ = change in temperature).

Water has a high c (4200 J/kg °C), so it stores a lot of energy. That is why it is used in radiators and hot-water bottles.

In the practical, an electric heater warms a block. Energy = power × time. Insulating the block reduces heat lost to the room.

4Watch out for

  • Using the final temperature instead of the change in temperature.
  • Using grams instead of kg.
  • Mixing up specific heat capacity and latent heat.
  • Forgetting that heat loss makes the measured c too high.
  • Forgetting E = P × t for the heater's energy.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

How much energy raises 1.0 kg of water by 1.0 °C? (c = 4200 J/kg °C)

  1. E = 1.0 × 4200 × 1.0

Answer: 4200 J

Example 2 Standard

Calculate the energy to heat 0.50 kg of aluminium by 20 °C. (c = 900 J/kg °C)

  1. E = 0.50 × 900 × 20

Answer: 9000 J

Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

A 2.0 kW kettle heats 2.0 kg of water from 20 °C to 70 °C. Specific heat capacity of water = 4200 J/kg °C.
(a) Calculate the energy needed. (b) Calculate the time this takes, if all the energy goes into the water.

  1. (a) Δθ = 70 − 20 = 50 °C
  2. ΔE = m c Δθ = 2.0 × 4200 × 50 = 420 000 J
  3. (b) t = E ÷ P = 420 000 ÷ 2000
  4. = 210 s

Answer: (a) 420 000 J; (b) 210 s

Q4Standard

Why is water used in central heating systems?

Show answer

Q5Exam-style

A 50 W heater runs for 600 s and warms a 1.0 kg block from 20 °C to 52 °C. Calculate c. Why is this higher than the true value?

Show answer
  1. E = 50 × 600 = 30 000 J
  2. c = 30 000 ÷ (1.0 × 32)

Answer: 938 J/kg °C; some energy was lost to the surroundings, so more energy seemed to be needed

Q6Exam-style

21 000 J is given to 0.50 kg of water at 15 °C. Calculate the final temperature.

Show answer
  1. Δθ = 21 000 ÷ (0.50 × 4200) = 10 °C
  2. 15 + 10

Answer: 25 °C

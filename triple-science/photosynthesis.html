Photosynthesis

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

The rate is held back by whichever factor is in shortest supply. Increase that factor and the rate rises, until something else becomes the limit.

2Step by step

Photosynthesis is an endothermic reaction. It happens in chloroplasts. The equation is: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen. AQA tests limiting factors, the inverse square law and the pondweed practical.

  1. Name the limiting factor: light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration or temperature.
  2. On a rate graph, where the line goes up, the factor on the x-axis is limiting.
  3. Where the line is flat, another factor is limiting.
  4. (Higher) Light intensity ∝ 1 ÷ distance². Double the distance gives ¼ of the light.
  5. In the practical, count oxygen bubbles per minute at different lamp distances.

CAUTIONDo not say temperature is "used up". Temperature limits the rate because enzymes work slowly when it is cold.

3Another way to see it

A leaf is a food factory. The raw materials are carbon dioxide (from the air) and water (from the roots). The power supply is light, caught by chlorophyll. The product is glucose, and the waste is oxygen.

A factory line can only go as fast as its slowest worker. That slowest thing is the limiting factor: light, carbon dioxide or temperature.

Light spreads out as it travels, like paint from a spray can. Double the distance and the light covers 4 times the area, so it is only ¼ as bright. This is the inverse square law: intensity ∝ 1/d².

4Watch out for

  • Saying plants photosynthesise instead of respiring. Plants respire all the time; they photosynthesise only in light.
  • Thinking doubling the distance halves the light. It makes it ¼ (inverse square).
  • Saying high temperature 'kills' the enzymes. Say the enzymes are denatured.
  • Writing oxygen as a reactant. Oxygen is a product.
  • Saying the rate keeps rising with more light. It levels off when another factor limits it.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Write the word equation for photosynthesis.

  1. Reactants: carbon dioxide and water (with light)
  2. Products: glucose and oxygen

Answer: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen

Example 2 Standard

A lamp is moved from 20 cm to 10 cm from a plant. How many times brighter is the light?

  1. Distance halves
  2. Intensity ∝ 1/d² → × 2² = × 4

Answer: 4 times brighter

Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

A student moves a lamp from 10 cm to 20 cm away from some pondweed. (a) By what fraction does the light intensity change? (b) Explain why the rate of photosynthesis falls.

  1. Light intensity ∝ 1 ÷ distance²
  2. Distance × 2, so intensity × 1/2² = 1/4
  3. Light is the limiting factor, so less light energy means a slower rate

Answer: (a) ¼ of the original (b) light is limiting

Q5Exam-style

As light intensity increases, the rate of photosynthesis increases and then stays the same. Explain why.

Show answer
  1. At first light is the limiting factor.
  2. Later another factor, such as CO₂ concentration or temperature, limits the rate.

Answer: Light stops being the limiting factor; CO₂ or temperature limits the rate

Q6Exam-style

Show answer

Answer: Advantage: faster photosynthesis, so a bigger crop. Consider: the extra cost must be less than the extra income, and another factor may become limiting

Biomass, food chains and food security

Biology · rank 13 of 31 · 31 marks · 3.5% of Biology marks · in 3 of 9 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Each step up a food chain keeps only about one tenth of the biomass. Most of it is lost before the next animal can eat it.

2Step by step

A pyramid of biomass shows the mass of living material at each trophic level. Producers are at the bottom. Each level is smaller than the one below.

Biomass is lost between levels because:

  • Some parts are not eaten, for example bones and roots.
  • Some food is not absorbed and is lost as faeces.
  • Much glucose is used in respiration, and the energy goes to the surroundings.
  • Some is lost as urea and carbon dioxide (waste).

Efficiency = biomass at higher level ÷ biomass at lower level × 100.

Food security means enough food for everyone. Threats include population growth, new pests and diseases, drought and the cost of farming. Solutions include fishing quotas and larger net mesh, factory farming (less movement, so less energy lost), and mycoprotein from the fungus Fusarium.

CAUTIONEnergy is not "used up". It is transferred to the surroundings, mostly as heat from respiration.

3Another way to see it

Biomass is passed up a food chain like water poured through a leaky bucket. At each step most is lost: not everything is eaten, some is not digested (faeces), and a lot is used up in respiration and lost as heat. Only about 10% reaches the next level.

Food security means everyone has enough safe, nutritious food. Threats include a growing population, new pests and diseases, climate change, conflict and the cost of farming.

Solutions: factory farming (animals waste less energy), fishing quotas and bigger net holes, and new foods like mycoprotein (made from the fungus Fusarium).

4Watch out for

  • Saying energy is 'used up' and gone. It is transferred to the surroundings as heat.
  • Calculating efficiency the wrong way up. It is (next level ÷ previous level) × 100.
  • Saying a pyramid of biomass shows numbers. It shows the mass of living material.
  • Forgetting the ethical issues of factory farming.
  • Saying mycoprotein comes from bacteria. It comes from a fungus.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

What is food security?

    Answer: Having enough safe, nutritious food for everyone

    Example 2 Standard

    Producers have a biomass of 1200 kg. Primary consumers have 96 kg. Calculate the efficiency of transfer.

    1. 96 ÷ 1200 × 100

    Answer: 8%

    Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

    The biomass of producers in a field is 5000 g/m². The biomass of herbivores is 450 g/m².
    (a) Calculate the efficiency of biomass transfer. (b) Give two reasons why not all the biomass is transferred.

    1. (a) Efficiency = 450 ÷ 5000 × 100
    2. = 9%
    3. (b) Some parts of the plants, such as roots, are not eaten
    4. Glucose is used in respiration and the energy is transferred to the surroundings

    Answer: (a) 9%; (b) e.g. not all eaten; lost in respiration (or faeces, waste)

    Q3Standard

    Explain why factory farming makes food production more efficient.

    Show answer

    Answer: Animals cannot move much and are kept warm, so less energy is used in movement and keeping warm, so more goes into growth

    Q4Standard

    Give two ways to make fishing sustainable.

    Show answer

    Answer: Fishing quotas (limits on catch) and bigger mesh in nets so young fish escape

    Q5Exam-style

    Biomass: grass 4500 g, rabbits 360 g, foxes 27 g. Calculate both efficiencies.

    Show answer
    1. 360 ÷ 4500 × 100 = 8%
    2. 27 ÷ 360 × 100 = 7.5%

    Answer: 8% and 7.5%

    Q6Exam-style

    Give one argument for and one against factory farming.

    Show answer

    Answer: For: cheaper food, more efficient. Against: poor animal welfare; disease spreads easily, so antibiotics are overused

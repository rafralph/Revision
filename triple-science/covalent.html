Covalent bonding and simple molecules

Chemistry · rank 21 of 25 · 24 marks · 2.2% of Chemistry marks · in 6 of 11 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Inside a molecule the bonds are strong. Between molecules the forces are weak. Boiling only separates the molecules.

2Step by step

A covalent bond is a shared pair of electrons between two non-metal atoms.

Simple molecular substances (such as H₂O, CH₄, Cl₂, NH₃):

  • Have low melting and boiling points, because the intermolecular forces are weak. The covalent bonds do not break.
  • Do not conduct electricity, because the molecules have no overall charge.
  • Larger molecules have stronger intermolecular forces, so higher boiling points.

Dot and cross diagrams show which atom each electron came from. They do not show the shape of the molecule.

Polymers are very large molecules. Their intermolecular forces are stronger, so they are solid at room temperature.

CAUTIONNever write that covalent bonds break when a simple molecule boils.

3Another way to see it

A covalent bond is a shared pair of electrons, like two friends sharing a pair of headphones: both get to use them, and that holds them together.

In simple molecules, the bonds inside each molecule are strong, but the forces between molecules (intermolecular forces) are weak. Melting or boiling only breaks the weak forces between molecules, so boiling points are low.

Bigger molecules have stronger intermolecular forces, so higher boiling points. Polymers are very long molecules, so they are solids at room temperature.

4Watch out for

  • Saying covalent bonds break when a simple molecule boils. Only the weak forces between molecules break.
  • Saying simple molecules conduct. They have no overall charge and no free electrons.
  • Drawing too many or too few electrons. Check each atom has a full outer shell.
  • Forgetting double bonds in O₂ and CO₂, and the triple bond in N₂.
  • Mixing up 'intermolecular forces' and 'covalent bonds'.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

How many covalent bonds are in a molecule of ammonia, NH₃?

    Answer: 3

    Example 2 Standard

    Explain why water has a low boiling point.

    1. Water is made of small molecules.
    2. The forces between molecules are weak.
    3. Little energy is needed to overcome them.

    Answer: Weak intermolecular forces need little energy to overcome

    Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

    Methane, CH₄, boils at −162 °C.
    (a) Explain why methane has a low boiling point. (b) Explain why methane does not conduct electricity.

    1. (a) Methane is made of small molecules with weak intermolecular forces
    2. Only a little energy is needed to overcome these forces (the covalent bonds do not break)
    3. (b) The molecules have no overall charge, so there are no charged particles to carry current

    Answer: Weak intermolecular forces; no charged particles

Plant diseases and defences

Biology · rank 23 of 31 · 18 marks · 2.0% of Biology marks · in 4 of 9 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

A sick plant photosynthesises less. Follow the chain: less chlorophyll or less leaf area, then less glucose, then less growth.

2Step by step

Plant diseases and problems that AQA tests:

  • Tobacco mosaic virus (TMV): a mosaic pattern on leaves. Less chlorophyll, so less photosynthesis.
  • Rose black spot (fungus): black spots, leaves fall off. Spread by water and wind.
  • Aphids (insect pests): suck sap from phloem.
  • Nitrate deficiency: stunted growth, because nitrate is needed to make protein.
  • Magnesium deficiency: chlorosis (yellow leaves), because magnesium is needed to make chlorophyll.

To identify a disease, look at the symptoms, use a gardening manual or website, send a sample to a lab, or use a testing kit with monoclonal antibodies.

Plant defences:

  • Physical: cellulose cell walls, waxy cuticle, layers of dead cells (bark).
  • Chemical: antibacterial chemicals, poisons that stop herbivores.
  • Mechanical: thorns and hairs, leaves that droop when touched, mimicry.

CAUTIONChlorosis means yellow leaves from too little chlorophyll. Link it to magnesium, not nitrate.

3Another way to see it

Plants cannot run away, so they defend themselves like a castle:

  • Physical walls: cellulose cell walls, a waxy cuticle on leaves, and bark (dead cells that fall off with pathogens on them).
  • Chemical weapons: antibacterial chemicals (e.g. mint, pine) and poisons that stop animals eating them.
  • Mechanical tricks: thorns and hairs, leaves that droop when touched, and mimicry.

Plants also get ill without pathogens. They need mineral ions: nitrate for proteins (lack it → stunted growth) and magnesium for chlorophyll (lack it → yellow leaves, called chlorosis).

4Watch out for

  • Mixing up nitrate deficiency (stunted growth) and magnesium deficiency (yellow leaves).
  • Saying aphids are pathogens. They are pests; they can carry pathogens.
  • Forgetting to link less chlorophyll to less photosynthesis and less glucose.
  • Calling thorns a chemical defence. They are mechanical.
  • Saying the cuticle is made of cellulose. The cuticle is waxy.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Give two signs that a plant has a disease.

    Answer: Any two: stunted growth, spots on leaves, areas of decay, growths, malformed stems or leaves, discolouration, pests present

    Example 2 Standard

    Explain why a plant short of nitrate ions grows slowly.

    1. Nitrate is needed to make amino acids and proteins.
    2. Proteins are needed for growth.

    Answer: Less nitrate → fewer proteins → stunted growth

    Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

    A farmer sees that his crop has yellow leaves and grows slowly. A soil test shows a lack of magnesium ions. Explain why the crop grows slowly.

    1. Magnesium is needed to make chlorophyll, so the plant has less chlorophyll
    2. Less light is absorbed, so the rate of photosynthesis falls
    3. Less glucose is made for respiration and for making new cells (proteins, cellulose)

    Answer: No magnesium → less chlorophyll → less photosynthesis → less glucose for growth

    Q5Exam-style

    Rose black spot is a fungus. Describe how it affects the plant and how it is treated.

    Show answer
    1. Purple or black spots form on leaves.
    2. Leaves turn yellow and drop early, so less photosynthesis.
    3. Spread by water or wind.

    Answer: Spots → leaves drop → less photosynthesis; treat with fungicides and remove and burn affected leaves

    Q6Exam-style

    Two plants: one has yellow leaves, the other is very small but green. Which mineral is each lacking? Explain.

    Show answer

    Answer: Yellow leaves: magnesium (needed for chlorophyll). Small but green: nitrate (needed for proteins for growth)

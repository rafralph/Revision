The Haber process and NPK fertilisers

Chemistry · rank 18 of 25 · 38 marks · 3.5% of Chemistry marks · in 4 of 11 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

The Haber conditions are a compromise. Lower temperature gives more ammonia, but too slowly. Industry picks a rate and yield that make the most money.

2Step by step

The Haber process makes ammonia: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃. The forward reaction is exothermic.

  • Raw materials: nitrogen from the air, hydrogen from natural gas.
  • Conditions: about 450 °C, 200 atmospheres, iron catalyst.
  • The gases are cooled. Ammonia liquefies and is removed. Unreacted gases are recycled.

Why these conditions:

  • Temperature: a lower temperature gives a higher yield, but the rate is too slow. 450 °C is a compromise.
  • Pressure: a higher pressure gives a higher yield (fewer gas molecules on the right) and a faster rate. But it is expensive and dangerous, so 200 atm is a compromise.
  • The catalyst increases the rate. It does not change the yield.

NPK fertilisers contain nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium. Ammonia makes nitric acid and ammonium salts. Phosphate rock is treated with acid, because the rock is insoluble. Potassium chloride and potassium sulfate are mined.

CAUTIONA catalyst does not move the equilibrium. It only helps it to be reached faster.

3Another way to see it

The Haber process makes ammonia for fertilisers: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ (exothermic). Nitrogen comes from the air; hydrogen from natural gas.

The conditions are a compromise, like choosing a speed on a motorway: faster gets you there sooner but costs more fuel.

  • 450 °C: a low temperature gives a higher yield but is too slow.
  • 200 atm: higher pressure gives a higher yield, but it is expensive and dangerous.
  • Iron catalyst: faster, no change to yield.

The ammonia is cooled and removed as a liquid; unreacted gases are recycled. NPK fertilisers provide nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium.

4Watch out for

  • Saying the catalyst increases the yield. It only increases the rate.
  • Saying 450 °C is used because it gives the highest yield. It is a compromise.
  • Forgetting that unreacted nitrogen and hydrogen are recycled.
  • Saying the hydrogen comes from water. In the Haber process it comes from natural gas.
  • Forgetting that phosphate rock must be treated with acid, because it is insoluble.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Name the raw materials used to get nitrogen and hydrogen for the Haber process.

    Answer: Nitrogen from the air; hydrogen from natural gas

    Example 2 Standard

    Explain why a pressure of 200 atm is used.

    1. There are fewer gas molecules on the right (2 vs 4).
    2. Higher pressure moves the equilibrium right: higher yield.
    3. Even higher pressure would be too expensive and dangerous.

    Answer: Higher pressure gives a higher yield, but more would cost too much and be unsafe

    Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

    In the Haber process, the forward reaction is exothermic. Explain why a temperature of 450 °C is used.

    1. A lower temperature would give a higher equilibrium yield of ammonia, because the forward reaction is exothermic
    2. But at a lower temperature the rate of reaction would be too slow
    3. 450 °C is a compromise between a reasonable yield and a fast rate

    Answer: Compromise: lower T = higher yield but too slow

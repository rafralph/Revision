Chemical cells and fuel cells

Chemistry · rank 20 of 25 · 28 marks · 2.5% of Chemistry marks · in 5 of 11 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

A cell is a displacement reaction that sends its electrons through a wire. The bigger the difference in reactivity, the bigger the voltage.

2Step by step

A simple cell has two different metals in an electrolyte. The voltage depends on the metals and the electrolyte. A bigger difference in reactivity gives a bigger voltage.

Non-rechargeable (alkaline) batteries stop when a reactant is used up. Rechargeable cells can be reversed by an external current.

A hydrogen fuel cell uses hydrogen and oxygen to make water and electricity. Overall: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O.

Half equations (Higher): H₂ → 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ ; O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O.

Fuel cells compared with rechargeable batteries:

  • Advantages: only water is made; they do not need recharging; they do not lose charge over time; fewer toxic materials to dispose of.
  • Disadvantages: hydrogen is a flammable gas, hard to store, and often made from fossil fuels.

CAUTIONThe voltage depends on the difference in reactivity, not the size of the metal pieces.

3Another way to see it

A chemical cell is two different metals in an electrolyte. The more reactive metal gives up electrons, which flow through the wire to the other metal. It is like water flowing downhill: the bigger the difference in reactivity, the bigger the "drop", and the higher the voltage.

A battery is two or more cells joined in series. Non-rechargeable cells stop when a reactant is used up. Rechargeable cells can be reversed by an external current.

A hydrogen fuel cell is fed with hydrogen and oxygen all the time, and the only product is water.

4Watch out for

  • Saying the voltage depends on the size of the metals. It depends on the difference in reactivity.
  • Saying fuel cells produce no pollution at all. Making hydrogen may use fossil fuels.
  • Saying a fuel cell needs recharging. It needs a fuel supply.
  • Unbalanced half equations in the fuel cell.
  • Saying the more reactive metal gains electrons. It loses them.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

What is a battery?

    Answer: Two or more cells connected in series

    Example 2 Standard

    Which pair of metals gives the highest voltage: Mg/Cu, Zn/Cu or Fe/Cu?

    1. Magnesium is the most reactive, so it has the biggest difference from copper.

    Answer: Mg/Cu

    Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

    (a) Write the overall equation for a hydrogen fuel cell. (b) Give two advantages of hydrogen fuel cells compared with rechargeable batteries.

    1. (a) 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
    2. (b) Only water is produced, so there are no polluting products
    3. They do not need to be recharged (or: they do not lose charge when not used)

    Answer: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O; e.g. only water made, no recharging

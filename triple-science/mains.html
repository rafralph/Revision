Mains electricity and electrical power

Physics · rank 11 of 29 · 40 marks · 4.0% of Physics marks · in 4 of 10 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Power is energy per second. A kettle's power tells you how many joules it moves every second.

2Step by step

UK mains electricity is a.c. at 230 V and 50 Hz.

  • Live wire (brown): at 230 V. It carries the alternating p.d.
  • Neutral wire (blue): near 0 V. It completes the circuit.
  • Earth wire (green and yellow): a safety wire. It carries current only if there is a fault.

The live wire is dangerous even when a switch is off, because your body is at 0 V.

Power: P = VI and P = I²R. Energy transferred: E = Pt and E = QV.

The National Grid uses step-up transformers to make the p.d. very high. Then the current is low, so less energy is lost by heating the cables. Step-down transformers make it safe for homes.

CAUTIONChange kW to W and minutes to seconds before you use E = Pt.

3Another way to see it

Mains electricity in the UK is AC (alternating current): it changes direction 50 times a second (50 Hz) at about 230 V. A battery gives DC: one direction only.

The three wires: live (brown) carries the high p.d.; neutral (blue) completes the circuit at about 0 V; earth (green and yellow) is a safety wire. If a fault makes a metal case live, current flows down the earth wire instead of through you.

Power: P = V I or P = I² R. Energy: E = P t or E = Q V.

4Watch out for

  • Mixing up the wire colours. Live = brown, neutral = blue, earth = green/yellow.
  • Saying the live wire is safe if the switch is off. It can still be dangerous.
  • Forgetting to change kW to W and minutes or hours to seconds.
  • Using P = VI when you only know I and R. Use P = I²R.
  • Saying the earth wire normally carries current. It only does when there is a fault.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Give the p.d. and frequency of UK mains electricity.

    Answer: About 230 V and 50 Hz

    Example 2 Standard

    A heater takes 4.0 A from the 230 V mains. Calculate its power.

    1. P = VI = 230 × 4.0

    Answer: 920 W

    Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

    A kettle has a power of 2.3 kW. It is connected to the 230 V mains.
    (a) Calculate the current. (b) Calculate the energy transferred in 3.0 minutes.

    1. (a) P = 2.3 kW = 2300 W; I = P ÷ V = 2300 ÷ 230
    2. = 10 A
    3. (b) t = 3.0 × 60 = 180 s; E = P × t = 2300 × 180
    4. = 414 000 J

    Answer: (a) 10 A; (b) 414 000 J (414 kJ)

    Practice 6 questions · easy to exam-style

    Try each one first. Then open the answer to check your working.

    Q1Warm-up

    What colour is the earth wire?

    Show answer

    Answer: Green and yellow stripes

    Q2Warm-up

    Why is touching the live wire dangerous?

    Show answer

    Answer: There is a large p.d. between it and your body (at 0 V), so a current flows through you

    Q3Standard

    A current of 3.0 A flows through an 8.0 Ω resistor. Calculate the power.

    Show answer
    1. P = I²R = 3.0² × 8.0

    Answer: 72 W

    Q4Standard

    A 60 W lamp is on for 2.0 hours. Calculate the energy transferred in joules.

    Show answer
    1. t = 7200 s
    2. E = 60 × 7200

    Answer: 432 000 J

    Q5Exam-style

    10 C of charge passes through a 12 V supply. Calculate the energy transferred.

    Show answer
    1. E = QV = 10 × 12

    Answer: 120 J

    Q6Exam-style

    Explain how the earth wire protects someone if a toaster's metal case becomes live.

    Show answer
    1. Current flows from the case through the earth wire to the ground (low resistance).
    2. This large current usually blows the fuse or trips the circuit breaker.

    Answer: Current goes through the earth wire, not the person, and the circuit is cut off

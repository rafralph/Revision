Alcohols, carboxylic acids and polymers

Chemistry · rank 12 of 25 · 46 marks · 4.2% of Chemistry marks · in 4 of 11 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

A functional group is the business end of a molecule. Once you know the group, you know the reactions, whatever the chain length.

2Step by step

Alcohols contain –OH (methanol, ethanol, propanol, butanol). They burn, react with sodium to make hydrogen, and dissolve in water. They are oxidised to carboxylic acids. Ethanol is made by fermentation of sugar with yeast, at about 30 °C without oxygen.

Carboxylic acids contain –COOH. They are weak acids, because they only partly ionise in water. They react with carbonates to make carbon dioxide.

Alcohol + carboxylic acid → ester + water (with an acid catalyst). Ethanol + ethanoic acid → ethyl ethanoate.

Addition polymers form from alkenes. The double bond opens, and many monomers join. No other product forms.

Condensation polymers form from monomers with two functional groups. A small molecule, usually water, is lost at each link. Polyesters and proteins (from amino acids) are examples. DNA, starch and cellulose are natural polymers.

CAUTIONIn a repeating unit, draw the bonds through the brackets and remove the double bond.

3Another way to see it

A homologous series is a family of compounds with the same functional group, like a family who all share a surname:

  • Alcohols: –OH (methanol, ethanol, propanol, butanol).
  • Carboxylic acids: –COOH (methanoic, ethanoic...).
  • Esters: –COO– (made from an alcohol + a carboxylic acid; they smell fruity).

Addition polymers (e.g. poly(ethene)) join alkenes like clicking Lego bricks: nothing else is made. Condensation polymers join monomers that each have two functional groups, and a small molecule (usually water) is given off each time. Natural polymers: proteins (from amino acids), starch and cellulose (from sugars) and DNA (from nucleotides).

4Watch out for

  • Saying alcohols are alkaline because of –OH. They are neutral.
  • Saying condensation polymerisation makes only the polymer. Water (or another small molecule) is also made.
  • Naming esters the wrong way round: ethanol + ethanoic acid → ethyl ethanoate.
  • Saying ethanoic acid is a strong acid. It is weak (partly ionised).
  • Forgetting a catalyst (acid) is needed to make an ester.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Write the formula of ethanol.

    Answer: C₂H₅OH

    Example 2 Standard

    Name the products when ethanol burns completely in air.

      Answer: Carbon dioxide and water

      Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

      Ethanol reacts with ethanoic acid.
      (a) Name the type of compound made. (b) Name the other product. (c) Explain why ethanoic acid is described as a weak acid.

      1. (a) An ester (ethyl ethanoate)
      2. (b) Water
      3. (c) Ethanoic acid only partly ionises in water, so it releases few H⁺ ions

      Answer: (a) Ester; (b) water; (c) only partly ionised

      Q4Standard

      Give one difference between addition and condensation polymerisation.

      Show answer

      Answer: Condensation also makes a small molecule (e.g. water); addition makes only the polymer. Addition uses one alkene monomer; condensation usually uses two monomers with two functional groups

      Q5Exam-style

      Explain how amino acids form proteins.

      Show answer

      Answer: Each amino acid has an amine group and a carboxylic acid group; they join by condensation polymerisation, releasing water, to form a polypeptide (protein)

      Name three natural polymers and their monomers.

      Show answer

      Answer: Proteins – amino acids; starch/cellulose – sugars (glucose); DNA – nucleotides

Circuits, current, p.d. and resistance

Physics · rank 10 of 29 · 43 marks · 4.3% of Physics marks · in 4 of 10 papers · Required practical: Resistance

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Current is the flow of charge. Potential difference is the push. Resistance is how hard the path is.

2Step by step

Charge flow = current × time (Q = It). Potential difference = current × resistance (V = IR).

Series circuits: the same current everywhere. The p.d. of the supply is shared between the components. Total resistance: R = R₁ + R₂.

Parallel circuits: the same p.d. across each branch. The total current is the sum of the branch currents. Adding a resistor in parallel decreases the total resistance, because there are more paths for the current.

An ammeter goes in series. A voltmeter goes in parallel.

In the required practical, measure the resistance of a wire at different lengths. Resistance is directly proportional to length.

CAUTIONChange minutes to seconds in Q = It.

3Another way to see it

Think of a circuit as water in pipes. Current is how much water flows past each second (amps). Potential difference is the push from the pump (volts). Resistance is how narrow the pipe is (ohms).

V = I R, and charge Q = I t.

  • Series: one loop. The same current everywhere; p.d.s add up to the supply; resistances add up.
  • Parallel: branches. Each branch gets the full p.d.; branch currents add up to the total; total resistance is less than the smallest one.

4Watch out for

  • Putting the voltmeter in series. It goes in parallel across the component.
  • Adding resistances in parallel. In parallel, the total is less than the smallest resistor.
  • Using minutes in Q = It. Time must be in seconds.
  • Saying current is used up around a circuit. It is the same all round a series loop.
  • Saying adding a parallel branch increases the total resistance. It decreases it.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

A current of 2.0 A flows for 10 s. Calculate the charge.

  1. Q = It = 2.0 × 10

Answer: 20 C

Example 2 Standard

A 4.0 Ω and a 6.0 Ω resistor are in series with a 5.0 V supply. Calculate the current.

  1. R = 4.0 + 6.0 = 10 Ω
  2. I = V ÷ R = 5.0 ÷ 10

Answer: 0.50 A

Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

A resistor has a p.d. of 6.0 V across it. The current is 0.40 A for 2.0 minutes.
(a) Calculate the charge that flows. (b) Calculate the resistance.

  1. (a) t = 2.0 × 60 = 120 s
  2. Q = I × t = 0.40 × 120 = 48 C
  3. (b) R = V ÷ I = 6.0 ÷ 0.40
  4. = 15 Ω

Answer: (a) 48 C; (b) 15 Ω

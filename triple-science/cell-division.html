Cell division and stem cells

Biology · rank 21 of 31 · 19 marks · 2.2% of Biology marks · in 4 of 9 papers

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

If 5% of the cells you see are dividing, the cell spends 5% of its cycle dividing. The snapshot gives the timing.

2Step by step

Mitosis makes two genetically identical cells for growth and repair. Stem cells are unspecialised cells that can become other types of cell. AQA tests the cell cycle, stem cells and their uses.

  1. The cell grows, makes more organelles and copies its DNA.
  2. One set of chromosomes is pulled to each end of the cell.
  3. The cytoplasm and cell membrane divide.
  4. Two identical cells form.
  5. To find time in mitosis: (cells in mitosis ÷ total cells) × length of cycle.

Embryonic stem cells can become any cell. Adult bone marrow stem cells form some types of cell. Meristems in plants can form any plant cell.

CAUTIONMitosis gives identical cells. Meiosis gives gametes that are all different.

3Another way to see it

Mitosis is like photocopying a whole book and then splitting the pages into two identical folders.

  1. The cell grows. It copies its DNA and makes more mitochondria and ribosomes.
  2. The copies of each chromosome are pulled to opposite ends.
  3. The cytoplasm and membrane split. You now have two identical cells.

Stem cells are "blank" cells that can become many types of cell. Embryos have the most flexible ones. Adults have some in bone marrow. Plants have them in meristems (tips of roots and shoots) all their lives.

4Watch out for

  • Saying mitosis makes gametes. Mitosis makes identical body cells; meiosis makes gametes.
  • Forgetting that DNA is copied before the cell divides.
  • Saying adult stem cells can become any cell. They can only become some types.
  • Forgetting the risks of stem cell treatments (infection, tumours).
  • Using the wrong fraction in the 'time in mitosis' calculation.

Example 1 Warm-up

What is a stem cell?

    Answer: An undifferentiated cell that can divide and become other types of cell

    Example 2 Standard

    Give one possible use of stem cells and one concern.

      Answer: Use: treat diabetes or paralysis. Concern: embryos are destroyed, or there is a risk of infection or tumours

      Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

      In a root tip, 20 out of 400 cells are in mitosis. The cell cycle takes 24 hours. Estimate the time spent in mitosis, in minutes.

      1. Fraction in mitosis = 20 ÷ 400 = 0.05
      2. Time = 0.05 × 24 = 1.2 hours
      3. 1.2 × 60

      Answer: 72 minutes

      Q1Warm-up

      Where are stem cells found in plants?

      Show answer

      Answer: In meristems (tips of roots and shoots)

      Q2Warm-up

      How many cells does mitosis make from one cell?

      Show answer

      Answer: Two genetically identical cells

      Q3Standard

      Why must the DNA be copied before the cell divides?

      Show answer

      Answer: So each new cell gets a full, identical set of chromosomes

      Q4Standard

      What is therapeutic cloning?

      Show answer

      Answer: Making an embryo with the same genes as the patient, so its stem cells are not rejected

      Q5Exam-style

      In a root tip, 30 of 600 cells are in mitosis. One cell cycle takes 20 hours. Estimate the time spent in mitosis in minutes.

      Show answer
      1. Fraction = 30 ÷ 600 = 0.05
      2. 0.05 × 20 h = 1 h

      Answer: 60 minutes

      Q6Exam-style

      Give two reasons why some people object to using embryonic stem cells.

      Show answer

      Answer: Any two: the embryo is destroyed (it could become a person); religious or ethical beliefs; risk of infection or tumours

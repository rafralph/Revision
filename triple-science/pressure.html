Pressure in fluids

Physics · rank 13 of 29 · 37 marks · 3.7% of Physics marks · in 4 of 10 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Pressure under water is the weight of the water above you, spread over an area. Go deeper and there is more water on top.

2Step by step

Pressure = force ÷ area (p = F ÷ A). The unit is the pascal (Pa). One Pa is one N/m².

A fluid (liquid or gas) pushes at right angles to every surface.

Pressure due to a column of liquid: p = hρg (h = depth, ρ = density, g = 9.8 N/kg). Pressure increases with depth and with density.

Upthrust: the pressure on the bottom of an object is greater than on the top, because the bottom is deeper. This gives a resultant upward force. An object floats if its weight equals the upthrust before it is fully under water.

Atmospheric pressure decreases with height, because there is less air above, and the air is less dense.

CAUTIONUse depth in metres and density in kg/m³. Water is 1000 kg/m³.

3Another way to see it

Pressure = force ÷ area. The same force on a smaller area gives a bigger pressure. That is why a sharp knife cuts and why snowshoes stop you sinking.

In a liquid, pressure grows with depth because there is more liquid above pushing down: p = h ρ g. Divers feel it in their ears.

Pressure is bigger on the bottom of an object than on its top, so there is an overall upward push: upthrust. An object floats when upthrust equals its weight.

The atmosphere also has pressure. It gets lower with height because there is less air above you.

4Watch out for

  • Using cm² instead of m² for area. 1 m² = 10 000 cm².
  • Forgetting that pressure in a liquid depends on depth and density, not the shape of the container.
  • Saying upthrust is caused by the water 'holding up' the object. It is caused by the pressure difference.
  • Saying atmospheric pressure increases with height. It decreases.
  • Mixing up density ρ (kg/m³) and pressure p (Pa).

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

A 600 N force acts on 0.020 m². Calculate the pressure.

  1. p = 600 ÷ 0.020

Answer: 30 000 Pa

Example 2 Standard

Calculate the pressure due to water at a depth of 12 m. (ρ = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.8 N/kg)

  1. p = hρg = 12 × 1000 × 9.8

Answer: 117 600 Pa

Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

(a) Calculate the pressure due to the water at a depth of 5.0 m. Density of water = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.8 N/kg. (b) Explain why an object under water has an upthrust.

  1. (a) p = hρg = 5.0 × 1000 × 9.8
  2. = 49 000 Pa
  3. (b) The bottom of the object is deeper than the top, so the pressure on the bottom is bigger
  4. So the upward force is bigger than the downward force: a resultant upward force

Answer: (a) 49 000 Pa; (b) more pressure on the deeper bottom surface

Answer: Pascal (Pa), which is N/m²

Q2Warm-up

Why does atmospheric pressure decrease with height?

Show answer

Answer: There is less air (fewer air molecules) above, so less weight of air

Q3Standard

A pressure of 2000 Pa acts on an area of 0.50 m². Calculate the force.

Show answer
  1. F = pA = 2000 × 0.50

Answer: 1000 N

Q4Standard

Explain why a sharp knife cuts better than a blunt one.

Show answer

Answer: The same force acts on a smaller area, so the pressure is greater

Q5Exam-style

Explain why a boat floats.

Show answer
  1. Water pressure is greater on the bottom of the boat than on the top surfaces.
  2. This gives an upward force (upthrust).
  3. The boat sinks until the upthrust equals its weight.

Answer: Upthrust from the pressure difference balances the weight

Q6Exam-style

Calculate the pressure difference between depths of 2.0 m and 10 m in sea water. (ρ = 1030 kg/m³, g = 9.8 N/kg)

Show answer
  1. Δh = 8.0 m
  2. 8.0 × 1030 × 9.8

Answer: 80 752 Pa (about 81 000 Pa)

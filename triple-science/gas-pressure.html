Gas pressure and temperature

Physics · rank 20 of 29 · 26 marks · 2.6% of Physics marks · in 3 of 10 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Gas pressure is particles drumming on the walls. More hits, or harder hits, give more pressure.

2Step by step

Gas particles move randomly. When they hit a wall, they exert a force. Pressure = total force ÷ area.

At constant volume, if the temperature increases, the particles move faster. They hit the walls more often and harder, so the pressure increases.

At constant temperature, for a fixed mass of gas: pV = constant. If the volume decreases, the particles hit the walls more often, so the pressure increases.

Doing work on a gas (such as pumping a tyre) increases its internal energy, so its temperature can rise.

CAUTIONpV = constant only works if the temperature and the mass of gas do not change.

3Another way to see it

Gas particles zoom around and crash into the walls of their container. Each crash is a tiny push; billions of pushes make the pressure.

  • Heat the gas (same volume): particles move faster, hit the walls harder and more often → higher pressure.
  • Squash the gas (same temperature): particles hit the walls more often → higher pressure. pV stays constant.

Doing work on a gas (like pumping a bike tyre) increases its internal energy, so it gets warmer.

4Watch out for

  • Saying the particles get bigger when heated. They move faster.
  • Using pV = constant when the temperature changes. It only works at constant temperature.
  • Saying particles collide with each other to make pressure. Pressure comes from collisions with the walls.
  • Forgetting to keep units the same on both sides (e.g. kPa and m³).
  • Saying pressure rises because there are more particles, when only the volume changed.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

What happens to the pressure of a gas in a sealed can when it is heated?

    Answer: It increases

    Example 2 Standard

    A gas at 150 kPa has a volume of 4.0 m³. It is compressed to 2.0 m³ at constant temperature. Calculate the new pressure.

    1. pV = constant: 150 × 4.0 = p × 2.0

    Answer: 300 kPa

    Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

    (a) A gas at 100 kPa has a volume of 2.0 m³. It is squashed to 0.50 m³ at constant temperature. Calculate the new pressure. (b) Explain, in terms of particles, why the pressure of a gas increases when it is heated at constant volume.

    1. (a) p₁V₁ = p₂V₂: 100 × 2.0 = p₂ × 0.50
    2. p₂ = 200 ÷ 0.50 = 400 kPa
    3. (b) The particles gain kinetic energy and move faster
    4. They collide with the walls more often and with more force, so the pressure increases

    Answer: (a) 400 kPa; (b) faster particles, more frequent and harder collisions

    Q4Standard

    Why does a bicycle pump get warm when you use it?

    Show answer

    Answer: Work is done on the gas, which increases its internal energy (and temperature)

    Q5Exam-style

    Explain, using particles, why reducing the volume of a gas at constant temperature increases its pressure.

    Show answer

    Answer: Particles have less space, so they hit the walls more often; the speed is unchanged, so the force per unit area increases

    Q6Exam-style

    Explain why tyre pressure is higher after a long drive.

    Show answer

    Answer: The tyre warms up, so the air particles move faster and hit the walls harder and more often

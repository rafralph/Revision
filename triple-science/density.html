Density

Physics · rank 22 of 29 · 21 marks · 2.1% of Physics marks · in 3 of 10 papers · Required practical: Density

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Density tells you how tightly the mass is packed. Same mass in a smaller volume means a higher density.

2Step by step

Density = mass ÷ volume (ρ = m ÷ V). Units: kg/m³ or g/cm³. 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³.

Solids are usually denser than liquids, and liquids are much denser than gases, because the particles are closer together.

In the required practical:

  1. Measure the mass with a balance.
  2. For a regular object, measure the sides with a ruler and calculate the volume.
  3. For an irregular object, put it in a eureka (displacement) can. Measure the volume of water that is pushed out with a measuring cylinder.
  4. Calculate the density.

CAUTION1 cm³ of water is 1 ml. Read the measuring cylinder at the bottom of the meniscus.

3Another way to see it

Density tells you how tightly packed the mass is: ρ = m ÷ V. A brick and a sponge of the same size have very different masses, because the brick is denser.

Solids are usually densest (particles packed close), liquids nearly as dense, gases very low (particles far apart).

To find density: measure the mass on a balance. For a regular block, volume = length × width × height. For an odd shape, lower it into a eureka can and measure the water it pushes out.

An object floats in water if its density is less than 1 g/cm³ (1000 kg/m³).

4Watch out for

  • Mixing units: 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³.
  • Dividing volume by mass. Density = mass ÷ volume.
  • Forgetting that 1 cm³ of water has a mass of 1 g.
  • Reading the measuring cylinder from the top of the meniscus.
  • Saying heavy objects always sink. It depends on density, not mass.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

An object has a mass of 200 g and a volume of 80 cm³. Calculate its density.

  1. ρ = 200 ÷ 80

Answer: 2.5 g/cm³

Example 2 Standard

A block is 2.0 cm × 3.0 cm × 5.0 cm and has a mass of 81 g. Calculate its density.

  1. V = 30 cm³
  2. ρ = 81 ÷ 30

Answer: 2.7 g/cm³

Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

A stone has a mass of 54 g. It pushes 20 cm³ of water out of a eureka can.
Calculate the density of the stone in g/cm³ and in kg/m³.

  1. ρ = m ÷ V = 54 ÷ 20
  2. = 2.7 g/cm³
  3. × 1000 = 2700 kg/m³

Answer: 2.7 g/cm³ = 2700 kg/m³

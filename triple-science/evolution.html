Variation, evolution and selective breeding

Biology · rank 15 of 31 · 24 marks · 2.7% of Biology marks · in 5 of 9 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Natural selection is a filter on a population. The individuals that fit the environment best survive more often, so their alleles become more common in each new generation.

2Step by step

Evolution is a change in the inherited characteristics of a population over time. It happens by natural selection. AQA also tests selective breeding, fossils, extinction, classification and antibiotic resistance.

To explain natural selection, use these steps:

  1. State the variation. Some individuals have a different allele, often from a mutation.
  2. Name the selection pressure, for example predators, disease or an antibiotic.
  3. Say which individuals are better adapted, and that they survive.
  4. Say that they reproduce and pass on the allele.
  5. Say that over many generations the allele becomes more common.

Selective breeding is the same idea, but humans choose the parents.

CAUTIONDo not write that organisms "choose" to change or "try" to adapt. The mutation happens by chance first.

3Another way to see it

Think of the peppered moth in England 200 years ago. Most moths were pale, and a few were dark because of a mutation. Factories made the tree trunks black with soot.

  • Birds could see the pale moths on dark trees, so they ate more of them.
  • The dark moths survived and had more babies, and the babies got the "dark" allele.
  • After many generations, most moths were dark.

Nobody chose this. Nature "selected" the dark moths. In selective breeding, humans do the choosing instead, for example cows that give a lot of milk.

4Watch out for

  • Saying an animal changes because it needs to. The variation must already be there (from mutation).
  • Saying the antibiotic causes the mutation. The mutation happened by chance before.
  • Saying one animal evolves. A population evolves over many generations.
  • Forgetting to say the survivors reproduce and pass on the allele. This step gets a mark.
  • Mixing up natural selection (nature chooses) and selective breeding (humans choose).

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

What is a mutation?

  1. It is a change in the DNA.
  2. It can make a new allele (a new version of a gene).

Answer: A random change in DNA

Example 2 Standard

Describe how a farmer could use selective breeding to get cows that make more milk.

  1. Choose the cows that make the most milk (and bulls from high-milk families).
  2. Breed them together.
  3. From the offspring, choose the best again.
  4. Repeat for many generations.

Answer: Choose best → breed → choose best offspring → repeat

Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

Some bacteria are resistant to an antibiotic. Explain how a population of antibiotic-resistant bacteria develops.

  1. A mutation gives some bacteria resistance to the antibiotic
  2. The antibiotic kills the non-resistant bacteria
  3. The resistant bacteria survive and reproduce
  4. They pass on the resistance allele, so the resistant population grows

Answer: Mutation → selection by antibiotic → survive → reproduce and pass on the allele

Answer: Genes (inherited) and the environment

Q3Standard

Give one problem caused by selective breeding.

Show answer
  1. Breeding close relatives (inbreeding) reduces variation.

Answer: Inbreeding: more inherited disease and less variation, so the population is less able to survive a new disease

Q4Standard

Explain why dark peppered moths became more common in industrial areas.

Show answer
  1. Soot made trees dark.
  2. Pale moths were easier for birds to see, so more were eaten.
  3. Dark moths survived and reproduced.
  4. They passed on the dark allele.

Answer: Dark moths were camouflaged, survived, bred and passed on the allele

Q5Exam-style

Hares in a cold place have thick fur. Explain how the thick fur became common in the population. [4 marks]

Show answer
  1. Fur thickness varied because of mutations.
  2. Hares with thicker fur kept warm, so they were more likely to survive the cold.
  3. They reproduced more.
  4. They passed on the allele for thick fur, so over many generations most hares had thick fur.

Answer: Variation → survival → reproduction → allele passed on, over many generations

Q6Exam-style

Give two reasons why many people did not accept Darwin's theory at first.

Show answer

Answer: Any two: it went against the belief that God made all living things; there was not enough evidence then; nobody knew how features were inherited (genes were not discovered)

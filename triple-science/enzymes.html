Digestion and enzymes

Biology · rank 4 of 31 · 51 marks · 5.8% of Biology marks · in 4 of 9 papers · Required practical: Effect of pH on amylase; food tests

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

An enzyme works like a lock and key. Its active site fits one substrate. Heat or the wrong pH changes the shape, so the key no longer fits.

2Step by step

Enzymes are biological catalysts. Digestive enzymes break large molecules into small ones that can be absorbed. AQA tests the lock and key model, temperature and pH, the amylase practical and food tests.

  1. Carbohydrase (amylase) breaks starch into sugars.
  2. Protease breaks proteins into amino acids.
  3. Lipase breaks lipids into fatty acids and glycerol.
  4. Above the optimum temperature, the active site changes shape. The enzyme is denatured.
  5. In the amylase practical, test samples with iodine. Rate = 1 ÷ time for the starch to disappear.

Food tests: iodine turns blue-black with starch; Benedict's turns brick red with sugar (heat it); biuret turns purple with protein.

CAUTIONDo not say enzymes are "killed". They are proteins, not living things. Say "denatured".

3Another way to see it

An enzyme is like a key and its substrate is the lock. Only the right shape fits the enzyme's active site. This is the lock-and-key model.

Heat or the wrong pH bends the active site out of shape, like melting a key. Now nothing fits: the enzyme is denatured, and this is permanent.

  • Carbohydrases (e.g. amylase): starch → sugars.
  • Proteases: proteins → amino acids.
  • Lipases: lipids → fatty acids + glycerol.

Bile (made in the liver) is not an enzyme. It neutralises stomach acid and breaks fat into small droplets so lipase works faster.

4Watch out for

  • Saying enzymes are 'killed'. They are not alive. Say denatured.
  • Saying bile is an enzyme, or that it digests fat. It emulsifies fat.
  • Thinking every enzyme has an optimum of 37 °C or pH 7. Protease in the stomach works best in acid.
  • Forgetting that rate = 1 ÷ time.
  • Saying the substrate is the same shape as the active site. It is complementary.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

What does a protease break down, and what are the products?

    Answer: Proteins → amino acids

    Example 2 Standard

    Starch disappears in 50 s. Calculate the rate of reaction.

    1. Rate = 1 ÷ time = 1 ÷ 50

    Answer: 0.02 s⁻¹

    Example 3 Exam-style [2 marks]

    In a practical, amylase is mixed with starch at pH 7. Samples are tested with iodine every 10 seconds. The iodine stops turning blue-black after 40 seconds. Calculate the rate of reaction.

    1. Rate = 1 ÷ time
    2. = 1 ÷ 40

    Answer: 0.025 per second (s⁻¹)

    Q1Warm-up

    Show answer

    Answer: Fatty acids and glycerol

    Q2Warm-up

    Which organ makes bile?

    Show answer

    Answer: The liver (it is stored in the gall bladder)

    Q3Standard

    Explain why an enzyme stops working at 60 °C.

    Show answer
    1. High temperature changes the shape of the active site.
    2. The substrate no longer fits.

    Answer: The enzyme is denatured: the active site changes shape so the substrate cannot fit

    Q4Standard

    Explain two ways bile helps digest fat.

    Show answer

    Answer: It neutralises stomach acid (lipase works best in alkaline conditions) and emulsifies fat into small droplets (larger surface area)

    Q5Exam-style

    Amylase takes 120 s at pH 5 and 40 s at pH 7. How many times faster is the reaction at pH 7?

    Show answer
    1. Rates: 1/120 = 0.0083 s⁻¹ and 1/40 = 0.025 s⁻¹
    2. 0.025 ÷ 0.0083

    Answer: 3 times faster

    Q6Exam-style

    Describe how to test a food sample for starch and for protein.

    Show answer
    1. Starch: add iodine solution; orange-brown → blue-black if starch is present.
    2. Protein: add biuret reagent; blue → purple if protein is present.

    Answer: Iodine: blue-black = starch. Biuret: purple = protein

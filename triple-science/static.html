Static electricity

Physics · rank 26 of 29 · 15 marks · 1.5% of Physics marks · in 2 of 10 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Rubbing moves electrons, nothing else. The object that gains electrons becomes negative. The one that loses them becomes positive.

2Step by step

When two insulators rub together, electrons move from one to the other.

  • The material that gains electrons becomes negatively charged.
  • The material that loses electrons becomes positively charged.

Like charges repel. Unlike charges attract. These are non-contact forces.

A charged object has an electric field around it. The field is strongest close to the object. A second charged object in the field feels a force.

If the p.d. between an object and the earth gets high enough, the air can break down and a spark jumps across.

CAUTIONPositive charges do not move. Always explain static with electrons.

3Another way to see it

Rub a balloon on your hair and electrons are rubbed off one material onto the other. Only electrons move, never positive charges.

  • The one that gains electrons becomes negative.
  • The one that loses electrons becomes positive.

Like charges repel; opposite charges attract. A charged object has an electric field around it; the field is stronger closer to the object. If the p.d. becomes very high, a spark jumps through the air.

4Watch out for

  • Saying positive charges (protons) move. Only electrons move.
  • Saying the object that gains electrons becomes positive.
  • Drawing field lines pointing towards a positive charge. They point away from positive.
  • Saying static only happens with metals. It happens best with insulators.
  • Forgetting why fuel tankers are earthed (to stop sparks).

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

What moves when an object is charged by rubbing?

    Answer: Electrons

    Example 2 Standard

    A cloth is rubbed on a rod and the cloth becomes positive. Explain why.

      Answer: Electrons moved from the cloth to the rod, so the cloth has lost electrons

      Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

      A polythene rod is rubbed with a cloth. The rod becomes negatively charged. Explain why the rod and the cloth become charged.

      1. Friction moves electrons from the cloth to the rod
      2. The rod gains electrons, so it becomes negative
      3. The cloth loses electrons, so it becomes positive

      Answer: Electrons transferred from cloth to rod

      Practice 6 questions · easy to exam-style

      Try each one first. Then open the answer to check your working.

      Q1Warm-up

      What happens when two negatively charged objects are brought close?

      Show answer

      Answer: They repel

      Q2Warm-up

      What is a spark?

      Show answer

      Answer: Charge jumping through the air when the potential difference is very high

      Q3Standard

      Explain why a charged balloon sticks to a wall.

      Show answer

      Answer: The balloon's charge repels electrons in the wall surface, leaving an opposite charge, so they attract

      Q4Standard

      Which way do electric field lines point near a positive charge?

      Show answer

      Answer: Away from the charge

      Q5Exam-style

      Explain why aircraft are earthed while refuelling.

      Show answer
      1. Fuel flowing through the pipe can build up static charge.
      2. A spark could ignite the fuel vapour.
      3. Earthing lets the charge flow safely to the ground.

      Answer: To stop charge building up and causing a spark that could ignite the fuel

      Q6Exam-style

      How does the strength of an electric field change with distance, and how is this shown on a diagram?

      Show answer

      Answer: It gets weaker further away; the field lines get further apart

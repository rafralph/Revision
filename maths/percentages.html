Percentages, interest and growth

Rank 3 of 36 · 91 marks · 4.7% of all marks · in 20 of 24 papers

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

A percentage change is a multiplier. Growth is a chain of the same multiplier. Five years of 3% growth is ×1.03 five times, not 15% once.

2Step by step

A percentage is a number of parts in 100. AQA tests percentage change, reverse percentages, compound interest, depreciation and growth or decay.

To find the value after a repeated percentage change:

  1. Change the percentage to a multiplier. For an increase of r%, use 1 + r ÷ 100. For a decrease of r%, use 1 − r ÷ 100.
  2. Raise the multiplier to the power of the number of years.
  3. Multiply the start value by the result.

To find an original value (reverse percentage), divide the new value by the multiplier.

CAUTIONDo not find the percentage of the new value to go back. Divide by the multiplier.

3Another way to see it

Turn every percentage change into one multiplier. 100% is the starting amount.

  • Up 15% → 115% → × 1.15
  • Down 20% → 80% → × 0.80

Compound interest is the same multiplier again and again: once for each year. So £100 at 10% grows to £110, then £121, because the second year earns interest on the interest.

For a reverse percentage (finding the original), divide by the multiplier instead of multiplying.

4Watch out for

  • Using simple interest when the question says compound.
  • For a reverse percentage, finding the percentage of the new amount instead of dividing by the multiplier.
  • Using 1.2 for a 20% decrease. A decrease must be less than 1: 0.8.
  • Forgetting to divide the percentage by 100 in the formula.
  • Rounding during the working. Keep the full calculator value until the end.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Increase £60 by 15%.

  1. Multiplier = 1.15
  2. 60 × 1.15

Answer: £69

Example 2 Standard

In a sale, a coat costs £36 after a 20% reduction. Work out the original price.

  1. After 20% off, the price is 80% → × 0.8
  2. Original × 0.8 = 36
  3. Original = 36 ÷ 0.8

Answer: £45

Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

Sam invests £4000 at 3% compound interest per year. Work out the value of the investment after 5 years.

  1. Multiplier = 1 + 3 ÷ 100 = 1.03
  2. Value = 4000 × 1.035
  3. = 4637.0963… (simple interest gives only £4600)

Answer: £4637.10

Q2Warm-up

Show answer

Answer: 0.88

Q3Standard

A car costs £12 000. It loses 15% of its value each year. Work out its value after 3 years.

Show answer
  1. Multiplier 0.85
  2. 12 000 × 0.85³ = 7369.5

Answer: £7369.50

Q4Standard

A price rises from £250 to £290. Work out the percentage increase.

Show answer
  1. Change = 40
  2. 40 ÷ 250 × 100

Answer: 16%

Q5Exam-style

A TV costs £84 including VAT at 20%. Work out the price before VAT.

Show answer
  1. £84 is 120%
  2. 84 ÷ 1.2

Answer: £70

Q6Exam-style

A town has 5000 people. The population grows by 4% each year. After how many whole years will it first be more than 6000?

Show answer
  1. Multiplier 1.04
  2. After 4 years: 5000 × 1.04⁴ = 5849.3 (not yet)
  3. After 5 years: 5000 × 1.04⁵ = 6083.3 (more than 6000)

Answer: 5 years

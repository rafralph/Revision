Bounds, rounding and estimation

Rank 17 of 36 · 52 marks · 2.7% of all marks · in 17 of 24 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

A rounded number is a range, not a point. Draw the band on a number line. It goes half a step each way.

2Step by step

A measurement that is rounded has an upper bound and a lower bound. AQA tests error intervals, upper and lower bounds in calculations, and estimates using numbers rounded to 1 significant figure.

  1. Find the size of the rounding step. "To 1 dp" means a step of 0.1.
  2. Halve the step. Subtract it for the lower bound. Add it for the upper bound.
  3. Write the error interval: lower bound ≤ x < upper bound.
  4. For an upper bound of a sum or product, use both upper bounds.
  5. For an upper bound of a division, use the upper bound on top and the lower bound on the bottom.

CAUTIONFor subtraction and division, do not use two upper bounds. Ask: which values make the answer biggest?

3Another way to see it

A rounded number hides a range of possible real values. Go half a unit either way.

7 cm to the nearest cm could be anything from 6.5 cm up to (but not including) 7.5 cm: 6.5 ≤ x < 7.5.

For calculations, ask: "how do I make the answer as big (or small) as possible?"

  • Biggest sum or product: use both upper bounds.
  • Biggest difference: upper − lower.
  • Biggest division: upper ÷ lower.

4Watch out for

  • Writing 7.49 as the upper bound. Use 7.5, with the < sign.
  • Using upper ÷ upper for the biggest division. Use upper ÷ lower.
  • Using upper − upper for the biggest difference.
  • Halving the wrong unit, e.g. nearest 10 → ± 5, not ± 0.5.
  • Mixing up rounding and truncating. Truncated 3.7 means 3.7 ≤ x < 3.8.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

A length is 7 cm to the nearest cm. Write down the error interval.

  1. Half of 1 cm is 0.5 cm

Answer: 6.5 ≤ x < 7.5

Example 2 Standard

A rectangle is 5 cm by 8 cm, both to the nearest cm. Work out the upper bound of its area.

  1. Upper bounds 5.5 and 8.5
  2. 5.5 × 8.5

Answer: 46.75 cm²

Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

A runner runs 100 m, correct to the nearest metre. The time is 12.3 s, correct to 1 decimal place. Work out the upper bound of the runner's average speed.

  1. Distance: 99.5 ≤ d < 100.5
  2. Time: 12.25 ≤ t < 12.35
  3. Upper bound of speed = upper distance ÷ lower time = 100.5 ÷ 12.25 = 8.204…

Answer: 8.20 m/s (3 sf)

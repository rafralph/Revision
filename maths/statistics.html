Histograms, box plots and cumulative frequency

Rank 5 of 36 · 89 marks · 4.6% of all marks · in 18 of 24 papers

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

In a histogram, area is frequency. A tall bar can hold fewer people than a short wide bar.

2Step by step

These diagrams show how data is spread. AQA uses all three. They often appear together with a median or interquartile range (IQR) question.

  1. For a histogram, find the frequency density of each class. Frequency density = frequency ÷ class width.
  2. Draw each bar with height equal to the frequency density.
  3. To find a frequency from a histogram, find the area of the bar: width × height.
  4. For a cumulative frequency graph, plot each running total at the upper end of its class.
  5. Read the median at half the total. Read the quartiles at one quarter and three quarters.
  6. For a box plot, mark the lowest value, lower quartile, median, upper quartile and highest value.

CAUTIONThe vertical axis of a histogram is frequency density. It is not frequency.

3Another way to see it

Histogram: the area of each bar is the frequency, not the height. A wide, short bar can hold as many people as a narrow, tall bar. That is why the height is frequency density = frequency ÷ class width.

Cumulative frequency is a running total: "how many so far?". Plot each total at the end of its class, then join with a smooth S-shaped curve.

To read the median, go to half the total on the vertical axis, across to the curve, then down. Use ¼ and ¾ of the total for the quartiles. A box plot draws these five numbers: minimum, lower quartile, median, upper quartile, maximum.

4Watch out for

  • Plotting frequency instead of frequency density on a histogram with unequal widths.
  • Plotting cumulative frequency at the midpoint instead of the upper class boundary.
  • Finding the median by halving the highest x-value instead of half the total frequency.
  • Comparing only one thing. Compare an average (median) AND a spread (IQR), in context.
  • Leaving gaps between histogram bars. The data is continuous.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

A class 10 < t ≤ 15 has frequency 30. Work out the frequency density.

  1. Class width = 15 − 10 = 5
  2. FD = 30 ÷ 5

Answer: 6

Example 2 Standard

Box plot A has median 34 and IQR 10. Box plot B has median 41 and IQR 18. Compare the two data sets.

  1. Average: B's median is higher, so B is higher on average
  2. Spread: A's IQR is smaller, so A is more consistent

Answer: B higher on average; A more consistent (smaller IQR)

Example 3 Exam-style [5 marks]

The table shows the waiting times of 38 people. 0 < t ≤ 10: 8 people. 10 < t ≤ 15: 12 people. 15 < t ≤ 30: 18 people. (a) Draw a histogram. (b) Estimate how many people waited more than 12 minutes.

  1. (a) Frequency densities: 8 ÷ 10 = 0.8, 12 ÷ 5 = 2.4, 18 ÷ 15 = 1.2
  2. (b) From 12 to 15: width 3 × height 2.4 = 7.2 people
  3. From 15 to 30: all 18 people
  4. 7.2 + 18 = 25.2

Answer: About 25 people

Practice 6 questions · easy to exam-style

Try each one first. Then open the answer to check your working.

Q1Warm-up

A histogram class has frequency 24 and width 8. Work out the frequency density.

Show answer
  1. 24 ÷ 8

Answer: 3

Q2Warm-up

A histogram bar has frequency density 1.5 and class width 20. Work out the frequency.

Show answer
  1. Frequency = FD × width = 1.5 × 20

Answer: 30

Q3Standard

Find the median and the interquartile range of: 3, 5, 7, 8, 10, 12, 15.

Show answer
  1. n = 7, median is the 4th value = 8
  2. LQ = 2nd value = 5, UQ = 6th value = 12
  3. IQR = 12 − 5

Answer: Median 8, IQR 7

Q4Standard

Frequencies for 5 classes are 4, 5, 11, 8, 2. Write down the cumulative frequencies.

Show answer
  1. Running totals: 4, 4+5, 9+11, 20+8, 28+2

Answer: 4, 9, 20, 28, 30

Q5Exam-style

On a histogram, the class 10 < t ≤ 30 has frequency density 2.4, and the class 30 < t ≤ 40 has frequency density 3. Estimate how many values are between 25 and 35.

Show answer
  1. 25 to 30: 2.4 × 5 = 12
  2. 30 to 35: 3 × 5 = 15
  3. 12 + 15

Answer: 27

Q6Exam-style

A cumulative frequency graph shows 80 students. Reading across at 20 gives 41 minutes, at 40 gives 52 minutes and at 60 gives 63 minutes. Write down the median and the interquartile range.

Show answer
  1. Median at ½ × 80 = 40 → 52
  2. LQ at 20 → 41, UQ at 60 → 63
  3. IQR = 63 − 41

Answer: Median 52 min, IQR 22 min

Straight-line graphs

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Turn a gradient triangle through 90°. 'Along 1, up 2' becomes 'back 2, up 1'. That is why perpendicular gradients are negative reciprocals.

2Step by step

A straight line has the equation y = mx + c. m is the gradient (the slope). c is the y-intercept (where the line crosses the y-axis). AQA tests gradients, parallel and perpendicular lines, and the line through given points.

  1. Find the gradient. Gradient = change in y ÷ change in x.
  2. For a parallel line, use the same gradient.
  3. For a perpendicular line, use the negative reciprocal. Turn the fraction upside down and change the sign.
  4. Put the gradient and one point into y = mx + c.
  5. Solve to find c. Write the full equation.

Check: the two perpendicular gradients multiply to −1.

CAUTIONThe gradient is change in y over change in x. Do not put x on top.

3Another way to see it

In y = mx + c:

  • m is the gradient: how far the line goes up for every 1 step across. Like the steepness of a staircase.
  • c is where the line crosses the y-axis.

Gradient = change in y ÷ change in x ("rise over run"). Draw a right-angled triangle under the line to see it.

Parallel lines have the same gradient. Perpendicular lines have gradients that are "flipped and negated": 2 becomes −½.

4Watch out for

  • Reading the gradient before rearranging into y = mx + c, e.g. 2y = 6x + 8 has gradient 3, not 6.
  • Using the reciprocal without changing the sign for a perpendicular gradient.
  • Dividing x-change by y-change (upside down).
  • Sign errors when a coordinate is negative: 5 − (−3) = 8.
  • Forgetting to substitute a point to find c.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Work out the gradient of the line through (1, 3) and (4, 9).

  1. Change in y = 9 − 3 = 6
  2. Change in x = 4 − 1 = 3
  3. 6 ÷ 3

Answer: 2

Example 2 Standard

Find the equation of the line through (2, 7) that is parallel to y = 3x − 1.

  1. Parallel → same gradient, m = 3
  2. 7 = 3 × 2 + c, so c = 1

Answer: y = 3x + 1

Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

Find the equation of the line that passes through (1, 4) and is perpendicular to y = 2x − 5.

  1. Gradient of y = 2x − 5 is 2
  2. Perpendicular gradient = −½ (because 2 × −½ = −1)
  3. y = −½x + c. Put in (1, 4): 4 = −½ + c, so c = 4.5

Answer: y = −½x + 4.5

Q1Warm-up

Write down the gradient and y-intercept of y = 4x − 7.

Show answer
  1. m = 4, c = −7

Answer: gradient 4, crosses at (0, −7)

Q2Warm-up

Find the gradient of the line 2y = 6x + 8.

Show answer
  1. Divide by 2: y = 3x + 4

Answer: 3

Q3Standard

Find the equation of the line through (0, 2) and (3, 11).

Show answer
  1. m = (11 − 2) ÷ (3 − 0) = 3
  2. c = 2

Answer: y = 3x + 2

Q4Standard

Write down the gradient of a line perpendicular to y = −4x + 1.

Show answer
  1. Flip and change the sign of −4

Answer: ¼

Q5Exam-style

Find the equation of the line through (−2, 5) that is perpendicular to y = 2x + 3.

Show answer
  1. m = −½
  2. 5 = −½ × (−2) + c = 1 + c, so c = 4

Answer: y = −½x + 4

Q6Exam-style

A is (1, 2) and B is (5, 10). Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of AB.

Show answer
  1. Midpoint = (3, 6)
  2. Gradient AB = 8 ÷ 4 = 2, so perpendicular gradient = −½
  3. 6 = −½ × 3 + c, so c = 7.5

Answer: y = −½x + 7.5

Scatter graphs, charts and sampling

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

A line of best fit turns a cloud of points into a rule you can read from. It is only trustworthy inside the range of the data.

2Step by step

A scatter graph shows two sets of data for the same items. AQA tests correlation, lines of best fit, outliers, and also pie charts, time-series graphs and sampling.

  1. Describe the correlation: positive, negative or none.
  2. Draw a line of best fit. Use a ruler. Put roughly equal points on each side.
  3. To estimate, go from one axis to the line, then across to the other axis.
  4. Estimate only inside the range of the data (interpolation).
  5. Ignore an outlier when you draw the line of best fit.

CAUTIONAn estimate outside the data (extrapolation) is not reliable. Correlation does not prove that one thing causes the other.

3Another way to see it

Each dot is one person (or thing) with two facts, like hours of revision and test score.

  • Dots go uphill → positive correlation. Downhill → negative. No pattern → no correlation.
  • A line of best fit goes through the middle, with about the same number of dots each side.
  • Estimating inside the data is fine (interpolation). Outside it is risky (extrapolation): the pattern may not continue.

Correlation does not mean one thing causes the other. Ice-cream sales and sunburn both rise in summer, but ice cream does not cause sunburn.

4Watch out for

  • Forcing the line of best fit through the origin or through the first and last dots.
  • Trusting an estimate far outside the data (extrapolation).
  • Saying one variable causes the other just because they correlate.
  • Including an obvious outlier when drawing the line.
  • Describing 'positive correlation' without saying what it means in context.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

As hours of revision increase, test scores increase. Describe the correlation.

  1. Both go up together

Answer: Positive correlation

Example 2 Standard

A school has 600 students; 240 are in Year 10. A stratified sample of 50 is taken. How many Year 10 students should be in it?

  1. Year 10 fraction = 240/600 = 0.4
  2. 0.4 × 50

Answer: 20

Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

The scatter graph shows hours of revision and test scores for 11 students. The line of best fit passes through (2, 45) and (8, 75). (a) Estimate the score for 5 hours of revision. (b) Why is it not sensible to use the line for 20 hours?

  1. (a) Gradient = (75 − 45) ÷ (8 − 2) = 5 points per hour
  2. At 5 hours: 45 + 3 × 5 = 60
  3. (b) 20 hours is outside the data. The line would predict 135, which is more than the maximum score

Answer: (a) about 60 (b) extrapolation is not reliable

Practice 6 questions · easy to exam-style

Try each one first. Then open the answer to check your working.

Q1Warm-up

As the temperature goes up, fewer coats are sold. Describe the correlation.

Show answer
  1. One goes up, the other goes down

Answer: Negative correlation

Q2Warm-up

What is a point that does not fit the pattern called?

Show answer
  1. It sits far away from the other points

Answer: An outlier

Q3Standard

A line of best fit passes through (2, 40) and (8, 85). Estimate y when x = 6.

Show answer
  1. Gradient = 45 ÷ 6 = 7.5
  2. 40 + 7.5 × 4

Answer: 70

Q4Standard

The data goes from x = 1 to x = 10. Why is an estimate at x = 20 unreliable?

Show answer
  1. It is outside the range of the data

Answer: Extrapolation: the pattern may not continue

Q5Exam-style

A club has 400 boys and 350 girls. A stratified sample of 60 is taken. How many boys and girls?

Show answer
  1. Total 750
  2. Boys = 400/750 × 60 = 32
  3. Girls = 350/750 × 60 = 28

Answer: 32 boys and 28 girls

Q6Exam-style

40 fish are caught, marked and released. Later 50 fish are caught and 8 are marked. Estimate the number of fish in the lake.

Show answer
  1. Fraction marked in the sample = 8/50
  2. 40 ÷ (8/50) = 40 × 50 ÷ 8

Answer: 250 fish

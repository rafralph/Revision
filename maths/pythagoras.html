Pythagoras and right-angled trigonometry

Rank 24 of 36 · 39 marks · 2.0% of all marks · in 9 of 24 papers

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Draw a square on each side of a right-angled triangle. The two small squares together have the same area as the big square.

2Step by step

These methods work only in right-angled triangles. Use Pythagoras when you know two sides and need the third side. Use SOH CAH TOA when an angle is involved. AQA often puts them inside 3D shapes.

  1. Find the hypotenuse. It is the longest side, opposite the right angle.
  2. To find the hypotenuse, add the squares of the two shorter sides. Then take the square root.
  3. To find a shorter side, subtract the squares. Then take the square root.
  4. For angles, label the sides opposite (O), adjacent (A) and hypotenuse (H).
  5. Choose sin = O/H, cos = A/H or tan = O/A. Use the inverse function to find the angle.

CAUTIONSet the calculator to degrees (D on the screen).

3Another way to see it

Pythagoras is about squares built on the sides. The big square on the longest side (the hypotenuse) has the same area as the two smaller squares added together: a² + b² = c².

  • Finding the longest side? Square, add, square-root.
  • Finding a shorter side? Square, subtract, square-root.

SOHCAHTOA: label the sides from the angle you are using. H is the longest. O is opposite the angle. A is next to it. Choose the ratio that uses the two sides in the question: O and H → sin, A and H → cos, O and A → tan.

4Watch out for

  • Adding when finding a shorter side. Subtract instead.
  • Labelling O and A from the wrong angle.
  • Calculator in radians instead of degrees.
  • Using Pythagoras when an angle is involved (use SOHCAHTOA).
  • Forgetting the inverse (sin⁻¹) when finding an angle.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

The two shorter sides of a right-angled triangle are 6 cm and 8 cm. Work out the hypotenuse.

  1. 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100
  2. √100

Answer: 10 cm

Example 2 Standard

In a right-angled triangle the opposite side is 5 cm and the hypotenuse is 13 cm. Work out the angle.

  1. O and H → sin
  2. θ = sin⁻¹(5 ÷ 13) = 22.6…

Answer: 22.6°

Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

A ladder 6.5 m long leans against a vertical wall. The top of the ladder is 6 m above the ground. (a) How far is the foot of the ladder from the wall? (b) Work out the angle between the ladder and the ground.

  1. (a) d² = 6.5² − 6² = 42.25 − 36 = 6.25, so d = √6.25
  2. (b) Opposite = 6, hypotenuse = 6.5, so use sin
  3. sin θ = 6 ÷ 6.5 = 0.923… → θ = sin⁻¹(0.923…) = 67.38…°

Answer: (a) 2.5 m (b) 67.4°

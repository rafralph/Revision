Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Probability is area. Draw all outcomes as one square of area 1. Each event cuts the square. The answer is the area of the pieces you need.

2Step by step

Probability is a number from 0 to 1. It shows how likely an event is. A probability of 0 means the event cannot occur. A probability of 1 means the event is certain.

AQA uses tree diagrams, Venn diagrams, relative frequency and "without replacement" questions. To find the probability of two events, one after the other:

  1. Draw a tree diagram. Draw one set of branches for each event.
  2. Write a probability on each branch. Branches from one point must add up to 1.
  3. If the first item is not put back, change the second probabilities. There is one item less in the bag.
  4. Multiply the probabilities along each path.
  5. Add the results of all the paths that you need.

CAUTIONDo not add along a path. Multiply along a path. Add between paths.

3Another way to see it

Picture a bag you can see into. If you take a counter out and keep it, the bag now has one fewer counter, and one fewer of that colour. So the second fraction changes: the bottom number always goes down by 1.

On a tree diagram:

  • Along a path (this AND then that): multiply.
  • Between paths (this way OR that way): add.

For a Venn diagram, "given that" means: only look inside that circle. The circle's total becomes the bottom of the fraction.

4Watch out for

  • Not changing the second fraction when the counter is not replaced.
  • Adding along the branches instead of multiplying.
  • Simplifying fractions too early. Keep the same denominator, so they are easy to add.
  • For 'given that', dividing by the whole total instead of the circle's total.
  • Forgetting that the probabilities on each pair of branches must add to 1.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

A fair coin is flipped twice. Work out the probability of getting two heads.

  1. P(H) = 1/2 on each flip
  2. Two heads means H AND H, so multiply: 1/2 × 1/2

Answer: 1/4

Example 2 Standard

Use the Venn diagram above. A student who plays basketball is chosen at random. Work out the probability that they also play football.

  1. 'Given that they play basketball': only look inside circle B
  2. Circle B total = 5 + 7 = 12
  3. Both = 5

Answer: 5/12

Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

A bag has 5 red counters and 3 blue counters. Ali takes a counter at random and does not put it back. He then takes a second counter. Work out the probability that both counters are the same colour.

  1. P(red, red) = 58 × 47 = 2056
  2. P(blue, blue) = 38 × 27 = 656
  3. P(same colour) = 2056 + 656 = 2656

Answer: 1328

Practice 6 questions · easy to exam-style

Try each one first. Then open the answer to check your working.

Q1Warm-up

A bag has 4 red and 6 green sweets. One sweet is taken at random. Work out P(green).

Show answer
  1. Total = 10
  2. Green = 6, so 6/10

Answer: 6/10 = 3/5

Q2Warm-up

The probability that it rains tomorrow is 0.35. Work out the probability that it does not rain.

Show answer
  1. Probabilities add to 1
  2. 1 − 0.35

Answer: 0.65

Q3Standard

A bag has 3 red and 2 blue counters. A counter is taken, put back, and a second counter is taken. Work out P(both red).

Show answer
  1. With replacement, the fractions stay the same
  2. 3/5 × 3/5

Answer: 9/25

Q4Standard

40 people were asked. 18 drink tea, 15 drink coffee and 6 drink both. Work out the probability that a person chosen at random drinks neither.

Show answer
  1. Tea only = 12, coffee only = 9, both = 6
  2. Tea or coffee = 12 + 9 + 6 = 27
  3. Neither = 40 − 27 = 13

Answer: 13/40

Q5Exam-style

A bag has 6 red and 4 blue counters. Two counters are taken without replacement. Work out the probability that they are the same colour.

Show answer
  1. P(R, R) = 6/10 × 5/9 = 30/90
  2. P(B, B) = 4/10 × 3/9 = 12/90
  3. Same colour = 30/90 + 12/90 = 42/90

Answer: 7/15

Q6Exam-style

P(Amy is late) = 0.4. If Amy is late, P(Ben is late) = 0.3. If Amy is not late, P(Ben is late) = 0.6. Work out P(Ben is late).

Show answer
  1. Late, late: 0.4 × 0.3 = 0.12
  2. Not late, late: 0.6 × 0.6 = 0.36
  3. Add the two paths

Answer: 0.48

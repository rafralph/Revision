Circle theorems

Rank 18 of 36 · 52 marks · 2.7% of all marks · in 14 of 24 papers

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Slide point C round the circle. The angle at C does not change. It is always half the angle at the centre.

2Step by step

Circle theorems are rules for angles and lines in circles. AQA asks you to find angles and to give reasons. Some questions ask for a full proof.

  1. Mark all the given angles on the diagram.
  2. Look for radii. Two radii make an isosceles triangle.
  3. Look for the main theorems: the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference; angles in the same segment are equal; the angle in a semicircle is 90°; opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°; a tangent meets a radius at 90°; two tangents from a point are equal; the alternate segment theorem.
  4. Find one angle at a time. Write the reason next to each angle.

CAUTIONUse the exact words of the theorem as your reason. "Because of the circle" gets no marks.

3Another way to see it

Learn the rules in kid-friendly words:

  • The angle at the centre is double the angle at the edge.
  • An angle standing on a diameter is 90°.
  • Angles looking at the same chord from the same side are equal.
  • Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°.
  • A tangent meets the radius at 90°. Two tangents from one point are equal.
  • Tangent and chord make an angle equal to the angle in the other segment.

Top tip: two radii make an isosceles triangle. Mark every radius you see.

4Watch out for

  • Halving instead of doubling for the angle at the centre.
  • Assuming an angle is 90° when the chord is not a diameter.
  • Saying opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral are equal. They add to 180°.
  • Not giving a reason for each step. Most marks need the theorem named.
  • Assuming a triangle is isosceles without two radii.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

AB is a diameter and C is on the circle. Angle CAB = 35°. Work out angle ABC.

  1. Angle in a semicircle: ACB = 90°
  2. Angles in a triangle: 180 − 90 − 35

Answer: 55°

Example 2 Standard

An angle at the circumference is 48°. Work out the angle at the centre on the same arc.

  1. Angle at centre is twice the angle at the circumference

Answer: 96°

Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

A, B, C and D are points on a circle, centre O. C is on the major arc and D is on the minor arc. Angle AOB = 124°. Work out angle ACB and angle ADB. Give reasons.

  1. Angle ACB = 124 ÷ 2 = 62°
  2. Reason: the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference
  3. Angle ADB = 180 − 62 = 118°
  4. Reason: opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°

Answer: ACB = 62°, ADB = 118°

Show answer
  1. Tangent meets radius at 90°
  2. 180 − 90 − 58

Answer: 32°

Q3Standard

Show answer
  1. Angles in the same segment are equal

Q4Standard

The angle at the centre O is 140°. Work out the angle at the circumference on the same arc.

Show answer
  1. Half of the angle at the centre

Answer: 70°

Q5Exam-style

Tangents from P touch the circle at A and B. Angle APB = 50°. Work out angle AOB.

Show answer
  1. OAP = OBP = 90° (tangent ⟂ radius)
  2. Quadrilateral angles add to 360°: 360 − 90 − 90 − 50

Answer: 130°

Q6Exam-style

Show answer
  1. Alternate segment theorem

Answer: 64°

Fractions, decimals and recurring decimals

Rank 14 of 36 · 59 marks · 3.1% of all marks · in 17 of 24 papers

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

A recurring tail never ends. Shift a copy so the two tails line up exactly. Then subtraction removes the whole infinite tail at once.

2Step by step

Fractions, decimals and percentages are three forms of the same value. AQA tests fraction arithmetic without a calculator, mixed numbers, and changing recurring decimals to fractions (often as a proof).

To change a recurring decimal to a fraction:

  1. Let x equal the decimal.
  2. Count the digits that repeat. Multiply x by 10 for one digit, 100 for two digits, 1000 for three digits.
  3. Write the two equations so the recurring parts line up.
  4. Subtract the smaller equation from the larger one. The recurring parts cancel.
  5. Divide to find x. Simplify the fraction.

CAUTIONIn a "show that" question, write every step. A calculator answer gets no marks.

3Another way to see it

Think of fractions as pizza slices.

  • Add or subtract: the slices must be the same size first, so find a common denominator.
  • Multiply: top × top, bottom × bottom.
  • Divide: keep the first, flip the second, then multiply ("keep, flip, change").

Change mixed numbers to improper fractions before you multiply or divide.

Recurring decimals: write two copies of the number so the repeating tails line up exactly. Subtract, and the never-ending tail disappears.

4Watch out for

  • Adding the denominators: 1/3 + 1/4 is not 2/7.
  • Flipping the wrong fraction when dividing.
  • Using ×10 when two digits repeat. For 0.181818…, use ×100.
  • Leaving a decimal inside the fraction, e.g. 2.1/9. Multiply to remove it.
  • Not simplifying the final fraction.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Work out 2/3 + 1/4.

  1. Common denominator 12: 8/12 + 3/12

Answer: 11/12

Example 2 Standard

Write 0.272727… as a fraction in its simplest form.

  1. Let x = 0.2727…; two digits repeat, so ×100
  2. 100x = 27.2727…; subtract: 99x = 27
  3. x = 27/99

Answer: 3/11

Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

Prove that 0.4̇5̇ (0.454545…) = 5/11.

  1. Let x = 0.454545…
  2. Two digits repeat, so 100x = 45.454545…
  3. 100x − x: 99x = 45
  4. x = 4599 = 511

Answer: 5/11 (shown)

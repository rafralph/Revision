Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

5² − 3² is a 5 × 5 square with a 3 × 3 square removed. The ring left over has 4 sides of 4: sixteen, a multiple of 8.

2Step by step

An algebraic proof shows that a statement is true for every number, not only for examples. AQA usually asks about odd, even or consecutive numbers.

  1. Write the numbers in algebra. Let n be any integer.
  2. Even number: 2n. Odd number: 2n + 1. Consecutive numbers: n, n + 1, n + 2. Consecutive odd numbers: 2n − 1, 2n + 1.
  3. Write the expression the question describes.
  4. Expand and simplify.
  5. Factorise to show the result. For example, 8n = 8 × n.
  6. Write a final sentence: "8n is a multiple of 8, so the statement is true."

CAUTIONExamples with numbers do not prove anything. You must use n.

3Another way to see it

An example only shows a rule works once. A proof uses letters to show it works every time.

Use these building blocks (n is any whole number):

  • Even: 2n. Odd: 2n + 1.
  • Consecutive numbers: n, n + 1, n + 2.
  • Consecutive odd numbers: 2n + 1, 2n + 3.

Expand and simplify, then take out the factor you need. "Multiple of 8" means you must show 8 × (something). End with a sentence that says what you proved.

To show a statement is false, one counterexample is enough.

4Watch out for

  • Testing a few numbers and calling it a proof.
  • Using n and n + 2 for 'two odd numbers'. These may be even.
  • Expanding (2n + 3)² as 4n² + 9. Write out (2n + 3)(2n + 3).
  • Stopping at 8n + 8 without writing 8(n + 1).
  • Not writing a final sentence explaining what you have shown.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Prove that the sum of two consecutive whole numbers is always odd.

  1. n + (n + 1) = 2n + 1
  2. 2n is even, so 2n + 1 is odd

Answer: 2n + 1 is always odd

Example 2 Standard

Prove that (n + 3)² − (n − 3)² is a multiple of 12.

  1. (n² + 6n + 9) − (n² − 6n + 9)
  2. = 12n

Answer: 12n = 12 × n, a multiple of 12

Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

Prove that the difference between the squares of any two consecutive odd numbers is a multiple of 8.

  1. Let the odd numbers be 2n − 1 and 2n + 1
  2. (2n + 1)² − (2n − 1)² = (4n² + 4n + 1) − (4n² − 4n + 1)
  3. = 8n
  4. 8n = 8 × n, and n is an integer, so the result is a multiple of 8

Answer: 8n, a multiple of 8 (proved)

Practice 6 questions · easy to exam-style

Try each one first. Then open the answer to check your working.

Q1Warm-up

Find a counterexample to 'all prime numbers are odd'.

Show answer
  1. 2 is prime and even

Answer: 2

Q2Warm-up

Write an expression for any odd number.

Show answer
  1. Even plus one

Answer: 2n + 1

Q3Standard

Prove that the sum of three consecutive integers is a multiple of 3.

Show answer
  1. n + (n + 1) + (n + 2) = 3n + 3

Answer: 3(n + 1), a multiple of 3

Q4Standard

Prove that the product of two odd numbers is odd.

Show answer
  1. (2a + 1)(2b + 1) = 4ab + 2a + 2b + 1
  2. = 2(2ab + a + b) + 1

Answer: Even + 1, so odd

Q5Exam-style

Prove that the difference between the squares of two consecutive odd numbers is a multiple of 8.

Show answer
  1. (2n + 3)² − (2n + 1)²
  2. = (4n² + 12n + 9) − (4n² + 4n + 1)
  3. = 8n + 8

Answer: 8(n + 1), a multiple of 8

Q6Exam-style

Prove that n² − n is always even for any whole number n.

Show answer
  1. n² − n = n(n − 1)
  2. n and n − 1 are consecutive, so one of them is even

Answer: An even number times anything is even

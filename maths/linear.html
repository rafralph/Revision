Linear equations and inequalities

Rank 9 of 36 · 67 marks · 3.5% of all marks · in 16 of 24 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

An equation is a balance. Do the same thing to both sides and it stays level. An inequality is a balance that is already tipped.

2Step by step

A linear equation has no powers of x above 1. AQA tests solving equations, forming equations from words or shapes, and inequalities on number lines.

  1. Expand any brackets.
  2. Move the x terms to one side. Do the same operation to both sides.
  3. Move the numbers to the other side.
  4. Divide both sides by the number in front of x.
  5. For an inequality, show the answer on a number line. An open circle means "not included". A closed circle means "included".

WARNINGIf you multiply or divide an inequality by a negative number, reverse the sign.

3Another way to see it

An equation is a balanced scale. Whatever you do to one side, do to the other, and it stays level.

An inequality is a scale that is tipped. You solve it in the same way, with one extra rule: if you multiply or divide by a negative number, flip the sign (< becomes >).

On a number line: an open circle means "not included" (< or >). A filled circle means "included" (≤ or ≥).

4Watch out for

  • Writing x = 4 when the answer is x < 4.
  • Forgetting to flip the sign when dividing by a negative number.
  • Only multiplying the first term in a bracket: 3(x + 2) is 3x + 6, not 3x + 2.
  • When listing integers, including an end that is not allowed (check < or ≤).
  • Doing the opposite operation on only one side.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Solve 5x − 7 = 18.

  1. Add 7: 5x = 25
  2. Divide by 5

Answer: x = 5

Example 2 Standard

Solve 3(x + 2) = x + 14.

  1. Expand: 3x + 6 = x + 14
  2. Subtract x: 2x + 6 = 14
  3. 2x = 8

Answer: x = 4

Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

Solve 3(2x − 1) < 2x + 9. Then list the integers that satisfy the inequality and x ≥ −1.

  1. Expand: 6x − 3 < 2x + 9
  2. Subtract 2x: 4x − 3 < 9
  3. Add 3: 4x < 12
  4. Divide by 4: x < 3. With x ≥ −1 the integers are −1, 0, 1, 2

Answer: x < 3; −1, 0, 1, 2

Q1Warm-up

Solve x/4 + 3 = 7.

Show answer
  1. Subtract 3: x/4 = 4
  2. Multiply by 4

Answer: x = 16

Q2Warm-up

Solve 3x + 2 ≤ 17.

Show answer
  1. 3x ≤ 15

Answer: x ≤ 5

Q3Standard

Solve 7x − 4 = 3x + 10.

Show answer
  1. 4x − 4 = 10
  2. 4x = 14

Answer: x = 3.5

Q4Standard

List the integers n such that −3 < 2n ≤ 6.

Show answer
  1. Divide by 2: −1.5 < n ≤ 3

Answer: −1, 0, 1, 2, 3

Q5Exam-style

Solve (x + 1)/3 − (x − 2)/4 = 2.

Show answer
  1. Multiply every term by 12: 4(x + 1) − 3(x − 2) = 24
  2. 4x + 4 − 3x + 6 = 24
  3. x + 10 = 24

Answer: x = 14

Q6Exam-style

Solve 5 − 2x > 11.

Show answer
  1. −2x > 6
  2. Divide by −2 and flip the sign

Answer: x < −3

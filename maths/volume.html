Volume, surface area and 3D shapes

Rank 11 of 36 · 64 marks · 3.3% of all marks · in 16 of 24 papers

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

A prism is a stack of identical slices. Its volume is the slice area times the length. A cylinder is a stack of circles.

2Step by step

Volume is the space inside a 3D shape. Surface area is the total area of all faces. AQA tests prisms, cylinders, cones, spheres, hemispheres and compound solids. The formulas for cones and spheres are given on the paper.

  1. Split the solid into simple parts.
  2. For a prism or cylinder, find the area of the cross-section. Multiply it by the length.
  3. For a sphere, use ⁴⁄₃πr³. A hemisphere is half of this.
  4. For a cone, use ⅓πr²h.
  5. Add the volumes of the parts. Keep π in the working. Round only at the end.

CAUTIONUse the radius, not the diameter. Halve the diameter first.

3Another way to see it

A prism is like a loaf of bread: every slice is the same shape. Volume = area of one slice (the cross-section) × length. A cylinder is a prism with a circle slice.

A cone or pyramid holds exactly one third of the prism around it: × ⅓.

Surface area is the wrapping paper: add the area of every face. For a cylinder, the curved part unrolls into a rectangle, 2πr long.

4Watch out for

  • Confusing volume (cm³) with surface area (cm²).
  • Using the diameter instead of the radius.
  • Forgetting the ⅓ for cones and pyramids.
  • Rounding too early in a multi-step question.
  • Giving a decimal when the question asks for an answer in terms of π.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Work out the volume of a cuboid 4 cm by 5 cm by 6 cm.

  1. V = 4 × 5 × 6

Answer: 120 cm³

Example 2 Standard

A cylinder has radius 3 cm and height 10 cm. Work out its volume to 1 d.p.

  1. V = πr²h = π × 9 × 10 = 90π
  2. 90π = 282.74…

Answer: 282.7 cm³

Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

A solid is a cylinder with a hemisphere on top. The cylinder has radius 3 cm and height 10 cm. The hemisphere has the same radius. Work out the total volume. Give your answer to 1 decimal place.

  1. Cylinder: π × 3² × 10 = 90π
  2. Hemisphere: 12 × 43 × π × 3³ = 18π
  3. Total: 90π + 18π = 108π = 339.29… cm³

Answer: 339.3 cm³

Practice 6 questions · easy to exam-style

Q1Warm-up

A cube has side 5 cm. Work out its volume and its surface area.

Show answer
  1. V = 5³
  2. SA = 6 × 5²

Answer: 125 cm³ and 150 cm²

Q2Warm-up

A triangular prism has cross-section area 12 cm² and length 9 cm. Work out its volume.

Show answer
  1. 12 × 9

Answer: 108 cm³

Q3Standard

A cone has radius 6 cm and height 8 cm. Work out its volume to 1 d.p.

Show answer
  1. ⅓ × π × 36 × 8 = 96π = 301.59…

Answer: 301.6 cm³

Q4Standard

A sphere has radius 3 cm. Work out its volume in terms of π. (V = 4/3 πr³)

Show answer
  1. 4/3 × π × 27

Answer: 36π cm³

Q5Exam-style

A closed cylinder has radius 4 cm and height 10 cm. Work out its total surface area to 1 d.p.

Show answer
  1. Two circles: 2 × π × 16 = 32π
  2. Curved part: 2π × 4 × 10 = 80π
  3. Total 112π = 351.85…

Answer: 351.9 cm²

Q6Exam-style

A hemisphere bowl of radius 6 cm is full of water. The water is poured into a cylinder of radius 4 cm. How deep is the water in the cylinder?

Show answer
  1. Hemisphere = ½ × 4/3 × π × 216 = 144π
  2. Cylinder: π × 16 × h = 144π
  3. h = 144 ÷ 16

Answer: 9 cm

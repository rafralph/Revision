Similarity and congruence

Rank 26 of 36 · 36 marks · 1.9% of all marks · in 15 of 24 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Double every length of a cube and 8 small cubes fit inside. Lengths scale by k, areas by k², volumes by k³.

2Step by step

Similar shapes have the same shape but different sizes. All lengths change by the same scale factor k. Congruent shapes are identical (k = 1). AQA tests similar triangles, area and volume factors, and congruence proofs (SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS).

  1. Find two matching lengths. Divide them to get k.
  2. For areas, use k². For volumes, use k³.
  3. Multiply or divide by the correct factor.
  4. To find k from areas, take the square root. From volumes, take the cube root.

CAUTIONDo not use k for an area or a volume. Square it or cube it first.

3Another way to see it

Similar means same shape, different size, like a photo and its enlargement. Every length is multiplied by the same scale factor k.

Areas and volumes grow faster. A square with sides × 2 has area × 4. A cube with sides × 2 has volume × 8. The units tell you the power:

  • cm → length factor k
  • cm² → area factor k²
  • cm³ → volume factor k³

Congruent means identical. For triangles, prove it with SSS, SAS, ASA or RHS.

4Watch out for

  • Using the length scale factor for an area or a volume.
  • Matching the wrong sides. Match sides by the angles they are opposite.
  • Measuring the diagram. It is usually not drawn to scale.
  • Going from an area ratio back to lengths without square-rooting.
  • Using AAA to prove congruence. It only proves similarity.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Two similar shapes have matching sides 4 cm and 10 cm. Another side on the small shape is 6 cm. Find the matching side on the large shape.

  1. k = 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5
  2. 6 × 2.5

Answer: 15 cm

Example 2 Standard

Two similar shapes have lengths in the ratio 2 : 3. The smaller has area 20 cm². Work out the area of the larger.

  1. Length factor 1.5
  2. Area factor 1.5² = 2.25
  3. 20 × 2.25

Answer: 45 cm²

Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

Two cylinders are mathematically similar. Their heights are 6 cm and 9 cm. The smaller cylinder has volume 160 cm³. Work out the volume of the larger cylinder.

  1. Length scale factor k = 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5
  2. Volume scale factor = 1.5³ = 3.375
  3. Volume = 160 × 3.375

Answer: 540 cm³

Q1Warm-up

A length of 5 cm is enlarged to 15 cm. Work out the scale factor.

Show answer
  1. 15 ÷ 5

Answer: 3

Q2Warm-up

Two triangles have two pairs of equal sides, and the angles between them are equal. Which congruence condition is this?

Show answer
  1. Side, angle (between), side

Answer: SAS

Q3Standard

Similar triangles: sides 6 cm and 9 cm match. Another side of the small triangle is 8 cm. Find the matching side.

Show answer
  1. k = 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5
  2. 8 × 1.5

Answer: 12 cm

Q4Standard

Two similar solids have length scale factor 2. The small one has volume 30 cm³. Find the volume of the large one.

Show answer
  1. Volume factor 2³ = 8
  2. 30 × 8

Answer: 240 cm³

Q5Exam-style

Two similar shapes have areas 25 cm² and 64 cm². The small one has height 10 cm. Find the height of the large one.

Show answer
  1. Area factor 64/25
  2. Length factor √(64/25) = 8/5 = 1.6
  3. 10 × 1.6

Answer: 16 cm

Q6Exam-style

Two similar solids have volumes 54 cm³ and 250 cm³. The smaller has surface area 36 cm². Find the surface area of the larger.

Show answer
  1. Volume ratio 54 : 250 = 27 : 125 → length ratio 3 : 5
  2. Area ratio 9 : 25
  3. 36 × 25/9

Answer: 100 cm²

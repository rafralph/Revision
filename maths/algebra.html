Algebraic manipulation

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

A factorised expression is a rectangle. x² + 5x + 6 is an area with sides (x + 2) and (x + 3). You can cancel a side length. You cannot cancel one piece of the area.

2Step by step

Algebraic manipulation means changing the form of an expression. The value stays the same. AQA tests expanding brackets, factorising, algebraic fractions and changing the subject of a formula.

To simplify an algebraic fraction:

  1. Factorise the numerator (the top).
  2. Factorise the denominator (the bottom).
  3. Find a factor that is in the top and in the bottom.
  4. Divide the top and the bottom by that factor.
  5. Check your answer. Put a number in for x in the start and in the answer. Both values must be the same.

Know these patterns: x² − a² = (x − a)(x + a). For x² + bx + c, find two numbers that multiply to c and add to b.

CAUTIONCancel only factors. Do not cancel terms that are added or subtracted.

3Another way to see it

Expanding and factorising are the same journey in opposite directions. Expanding opens a parcel. Factorising packs it back up.

Use a grid (area model) to expand two brackets. Each box in the grid is one multiplication. Then collect the like terms.

To simplify an algebraic fraction, factorise the top and the bottom first. You can only cancel a whole bracket that is multiplied on both the top and the bottom, like cancelling a whole box, not one item from inside it.

4Watch out for

  • Cancelling single terms, e.g. (x + 3)/(x + 5) → 3/5. You can only cancel factors.
  • Sign errors with negative numbers inside brackets, e.g. (x − 2)(x − 3) has +6, not −6.
  • Taking out a common factor wrongly: 3x + 12 = 3(x + 4), not 3(x + 12).
  • Collecting unlike terms: 5x + 4 is not 9x.
  • Stopping too early: always check whether the answer can be factorised further.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Expand and simplify (x + 4)(x − 2).

  1. x × x = x², x × −2 = −2x
  2. 4 × x = 4x, 4 × −2 = −8
  3. Collect: −2x + 4x = 2x

Answer: x² + 2x − 8

Example 2 Standard

Factorise 2x² + 7x + 3.

  1. Multiply a × c: 2 × 3 = 6. Find two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to 7: 6 and 1
  2. Split: 2x² + 6x + x + 3
  3. Group: 2x(x + 3) + 1(x + 3)

Answer: (2x + 1)(x + 3)

Example 3 Exam-style [3 marks]

Simplify fully x² − 9x² + 5x + 6

  1. Top: x² − 9 = (x − 3)(x + 3) (difference of two squares)
  2. Bottom: x² + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3) (2 × 3 = 6, 2 + 3 = 5)
  3. (x − 3)(x + 3)(x + 2)(x + 3) → divide top and bottom by (x + 3)
  4. Check with x = 1: 1 − 91 + 5 + 6 = −23 and 1 − 31 + 2 = −23

Answer: x − 3x + 2

Practice 6 questions · easy to exam-style

Try each one first. Then open the answer to check your working.

Q1Warm-up

Expand 3(2x − 5).

Show answer
  1. 3 × 2x = 6x, 3 × −5 = −15

Answer: 6x − 15

Q2Warm-up

Factorise fully 12x + 18.

Show answer
  1. HCF of 12 and 18 is 6

Answer: 6(2x + 3)

Q3Standard

Expand and simplify (2x − 3)(x + 4).

Show answer
  1. 2x² + 8x − 3x − 12

Answer: 2x² + 5x − 12

Q4Standard

Factorise x² − 7x + 12.

Show answer
  1. Two numbers that multiply to +12 and add to −7: −3 and −4

Answer: (x − 3)(x − 4)

Q5Exam-style

Simplify fully (x² − 16)/(x² + x − 12).

Show answer
  1. Top: difference of two squares (x − 4)(x + 4)
  2. Bottom: (x + 4)(x − 3)
  3. Cancel (x + 4)

Answer: (x − 4)/(x − 3)

Q6Exam-style

Expand and simplify (x + 2)(x − 1)(x + 3).

Show answer
  1. (x + 2)(x − 1) = x² + x − 2
  2. (x² + x − 2)(x + 3) = x³ + 3x² + x² + 3x − 2x − 6

Answer: x³ + 4x² + x − 6

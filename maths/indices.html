Indices and surds

Rank 13 of 36 · 61 marks · 3.2% of all marks · in 14 of 24 papers

VideoExplanationsDiagramsWorked examplesPractice

Explanations 4 ways

1The picture idea

Walk down the powers of 2. Each step divides by 2. This forces 2⁰ = 1 and 2⁻¹ = ½. The rules are not magic; the pattern makes them.

2Step by step

An index (power) shows how many times a number multiplies by itself. A surd is a root that is not a whole number, for example √2. AQA tests the index laws, negative and fractional powers, and simplifying surds. Many of these are on the non-calculator paper.

  1. When you multiply powers of the same number, add the indices. When you divide, subtract them.
  2. For a negative index, write one over the positive power: a−n = 1 ÷ an.
  3. For a fractional index, the bottom is the root and the top is the power. Find the root first.
  4. To simplify a surd, find the largest square factor. √50 = √25 × √2 = 5√2.
  5. To rationalise a denominator, multiply the top and bottom by the surd.

CAUTIONA negative index does not make the answer negative. 2−2 = ¼.

3Another way to see it

An index counts copies. a³ means a × a × a. Every law comes from counting copies:

  • a³ × a² = (a·a·a)(a·a) = 5 copies → add the powers.
  • a⁵ ÷ a² → cancel 2 copies → subtract.
  • (a²)³ = three lots of 2 copies → multiply.

A negative power means "flip it": 2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8. It does not make the answer negative.

A fraction power means "root, then power": the bottom is the root, the top is the power. Do the root first, so the numbers stay small.

Surds: pull out square numbers. √50 = √25 × √2 = 5√2.

4Watch out for

  • Multiplying the powers when you multiply numbers: x⁵ × x³ = x⁸, not x¹⁵.
  • Thinking a negative power gives a negative answer: 2⁻³ = 1/8, not −8.
  • Mixing up a² (square) with a^½ (square root).
  • Writing √(9 + 16) = 3 + 4. You cannot split a root over + or −.
  • Not simplifying a surd fully: √72 = 6√2, not 2√18.

Worked examples 3

Example 1 Warm-up

Work out (a) 5⁰ (b) 2⁻³.

  1. Anything to the power 0 is 1
  2. 2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8

Answer: (a) 1 (b) 1/8

Example 2 Standard

Simplify √72.

  1. 72 = 36 × 2
  2. √36 = 6

Answer: 6√2

Example 3 Exam-style [4 marks]

(a) Work out the value of 8−⅔. (b) Simplify √50 + √18.

  1. (a) 8−⅔ = 18⅔ ; ∛8 = 2 ; 2² = 4
  2. (b) √50 = √25 × √2 = 5√2 ; √18 = √9 × √2 = 3√2
  3. 5√2 + 3√2 = 8√2

Answer: (a) ¼ (b) 8√2
